# Central → Northern Vietnam, 12–21 December

**Group:** 6–8 adults · **Riders:** all experienced, motorcycle licences held · **Hiking:** mixed appetite, need easy and hard options · **Priorities:** nature and the must-see landmarks first; good food alongside, not instead

**The shape of it:** land in Hanoi on Saturday 12 December at 10:00, fly straight on to Da Nang, and travel north by road and rail: Hoi An, Hue, Phong Nha, the night train, Ninh Binh, and finish in Hanoi, flying home from there on Monday 21 December. 12 December 2026 is a **Saturday**, so Hoi An is a weekend, Hue and Phong Nha are midweek (quieter), Ninh Binh is Friday–Saturday, and Hanoi gets its weekend at the end: the Hoan Kiem walking street on Saturday night and the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum on Sunday morning.

**The trade-off of going north:** the central coast comes first, in the wetter half of December. The chance of a wet day in Hoi An and Hue falls from about 41% on 1 December to about 22% by the 31st, and Hoi An's flood risk runs to mid-December. Every central day has a rain plan; take them seriously.

**Exchange rate used throughout:** 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND (mid-September 2026; it has ranged 25,900–26,400 over the past year). Prices were re-checked in October 2026; where sources still disagree, that's flagged.

## 1. The route, and what it costs you

### Chosen route

| Nights | Base | Nights | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12–13 Dec | **Hoi An** | 2 | The UNESCO old town: lanterns, the river, assembly halls and the Japanese Covered Bridge, and *cao lầu* at its source. Two nights give you a whole day here after the long flight, and a rest before the ride. |
| 14 Dec | **Hue** | 1 | The imperial capital: the Imperial City and the royal tombs of Tu Duc, Khai Dinh and Minh Mang, split across the arrival afternoon and the next morning. Also Vietnam's most distinctive regional cooking. |
| 15–16 Dec | **Phong Nha (Son Trach)** | 2 | The nature centrepiece. World-class karst, real caving, and the least performative tourism economy on the route. |
| 17 Dec | **Night train SE20** | 1 (on rails) | Converts a long road day into a night you'd have spent in bed anyway, and arrives in Ninh Binh in daylight. |
| 18 Dec | **Ninh Binh (Tam Coc / Hoa Lu)** | 1 | The best karst-and-river landscape reachable in the dry northern winter: Trang An's cave boats, Mua Cave's view, Van Long's wetland. |
| 19–20 Dec | **Hanoi** | 2 | The capital's landmarks (Hoan Kiem Lake, the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, the Temple of Literature, Hoa Lo Prison) with the north's best food in between, and the flight home from the same airport. |

Five bases, nine nights, one of them on a train. One flight at the start, no backtracking: the route is a single northward line.

### What you're giving up

**Hue's second night.** One night means the Imperial City on the afternoon you ride in, and the three tombs early the next morning before the drive north. Thien Mu Pagoda and a slow evening by the river are the casualties.

**Cuc Phuong — cut.** It was a full day of old-growth forest and the Endangered Primate Rescue Centre. The nature on this route is Trang An, Van Long, Phong Nha's caves and the Hai Van ride.

**Ha Long Bay and Lan Ha Bay — the biggest landmark not on the route.** Vietnam's best-known landscape and the omission people will ask about. Adding it costs roughly two days; Ninh Binh's karst, seen from rowed boats, is this route's substitute. A separate Ha Long version of the trip exists alongside this one.

**Pu Luong and Mai Chau — cut.** Quieter than Ninh Binh with better walking, but in mid-December the terraces are bare stubble, the valley sits under fog for days at a time, and daytime highs are 13–17°C with high humidity.

**Da Nang — ridden through, not stayed in.** You land at its airport on Day 1 and ride through it on Day 3, past the Marble Mountains.

**Bach Ma — cut.** The summit sits in cloud for most of December and the trails are slick.

**The far north (Ha Giang, Sapa) — a different trip.** For licensed riders the far-north mountain loops are the obvious temptation; they need several days on their own.

### Variant A — Da Nang instead of Hoi An

If Hoi An is flooding when you land (its risk runs to mid-December), sleep in Da Nang's Hai Chau district instead, near the Han River: **Brilliant Hotel**, **Avora Hotel** and **Hadana Boutique** were the well-rated options in October 2026 (from ~$52–85 a room). Hoi An becomes a 40-minute ride for the day.

### Variant B — keep Pu Luong, drop Ninh Binh

Take the night train from Dong Hoi to Thanh Hoa instead, then 2.5 hours to **Pu Luong** for the night of the 18th, and on to Hanoi on the 19th. Same shape. You trade Trang An's karst rivers for Thai-minority villages, better homestays and worse weather.

### One thing I'd add if the budget allows

**Oxalis Hang En Cave Adventure (2 days, 1 night)** — 9,500,000 VND per person, sleeping on a beach inside one of the world's largest caves. It runs "December to mid-September"; the first December departure wasn't published in October. It would take both Phong Nha days, so only if it runs on 16–17 December.

### How to book each of these

- **The Da Nang flight:** you land at Terminal 2 (international) at 10:00 and fly from Terminal 1 (domestic). On separate tickets you clear immigration, collect bags, take the free shuttle or walk 10 minutes, and check in again: allow **3.5–4 hours**. Vietjet lists afternoon departures at 14:05, 14:35 and 15:05; Vietnam Airlines around 16:05 (a July schedule; check December). Vietnam Airlines economy includes 23 kg checked; Vietjet's cheapest fares include only 7 kg of cabin bags, and a 20 kg bag costs 180,000–350,000 VND bought online. Book the domestic leg on the same ticket as the international one if you can: the airline then protects the connection.
- **Private vans:** Ford Transit or Hyundai Solati 16-seaters. For eight people plus luggage, insist on a 16-seater, not a 9-seat Dcar limousine. Book by WhatsApp two to three weeks out.
- **Hoi An → Hue motorbikes:** **Hidden Land Travel / Hue Scooter Rental** (hiddenlandtravel.com, huescooterrental.com) delivers bikes to your Hoi An hotel and takes them back at its Hue office, 24A Alley 11 Ly Thuong Kiet; from US$22 a bike, luggage either free or 50,000 VND a piece (its two sites disagree). **Hoianit**, 84 Phan Chau Trinh, Hoi An, charges 750,000 VND one-way including luggage to your Hue hotel. **Motorvina** lists about US$23; **Style Motorbikes** has a Hoi An shop at 171 Cua Dai but no published one-way price. Reserve eight bikes at least three weeks ahead. **Hand over cash deposits, not passports** (see §11).
- **Hue → Phong Nha with the tombs and Vinh Moc:** **Culture Pham Travel** quotes 3,000,000 VND for a minivan Hue ↔ Phong Nha plus US$15 for Vinh Moc; **Explore Vietnam** US$130 for a van (max 12) plus US$20. Ask for a 07:00 start that takes in Tu Duc, Khai Dinh and Minh Mang before heading north, and get the quote in writing.
- **Night train:** **dsvn.vn** is the state railway (cheapest, often rejects foreign cards); **Baolau** and **12go** take foreign cards. You want **two whole 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins** on **SE20, 17 December** (*giường nằm mềm điều hòa*). Private cabins on SE20: Lotus 1,800,000, Charmy and New Livitrans 1,900,000, Violette 2,000,000, Laman 2,200,000 VND per berth (Baolau, October 2026). Exchange the e-ticket voucher at the station 30–60 minutes before departure.
- **Tam Coc → Hanoi:** any of the Hanoi or Ninh Binh van operators, ~1,800,000 VND one way for a 16-seater.

## 3. Day by day

## Day 1 — Saturday 12 December · Hanoi → Hoi An
*Theme: land, connect, and reach the river by lantern light.*

### Movements
- **10:00** Land at Noi Bai, **Terminal 2** (international). Immigration, collect bags, customs.
- **12:00** The free shuttle to **Terminal 1** (domestic), 8–10 minutes, every 15 minutes in the day (the airport's two pages give 06:00–01:30 and 05:00–01:00). Or walk: about 10 minutes. Check in again at Terminal 1.
- **14:05 Flight Hanoi → Da Nang.** About 1h20. Book a departure from about 14:00: on separate tickets you need 3.5–4 hours from landing. *The afternoon times here come from a July schedule; check the December one.*
- **16:00** Van from Da Nang airport to Hoi An, 40–50 minutes, about an hour around 17:00 and up to 90 minutes in heavy rain. ~750,000–840,000 VND for a 16-seater with eight bags.
- **17:00** Arrive Hoi An.

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **Emm Hotel Hoi An** — 0.7 km from the old town, rated 9.2, from ~US$76 a room (October 2026 snapshot).
2. **River Suites Hoi An** — 0.6 km, rated 8.8, from ~US$66.
3. **Hoi An Historic Hotel** — 0.5 km, rated 9.1, from ~US$99.

**Budget note:** your stated band is $40–80 per person per night. At eight people sharing four rooms, nothing on this route needs the top of it. See §6.

### Activities
- **17:00** Check in, shower. It's been a long day; keep tonight short and on foot.
- **18:00 Hoi An Ancient Town at night.** The pedestrian zone runs to 21:00 in winter, and Phan Chau Trinh St is pedestrian from 17:30 to 21:00. The ticketed houses close at 17:00, so tonight is the streets, the lanterns and the river; the sites are tomorrow.
- **18:30 Lantern boat on the Thu Bon.** About 170,000 VND for 1–3 people and 220,000 for 4–5, roughly 16:00–21:30. Lanterns to float are 20,000–40,000 VND each.
- **19:30** The **night market** on Nguyen Hoang, about 17:00–22:00 or 23:00.

### Food
- **Lunch, 12:30 — at Noi Bai, Terminal 1**, between flights.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — Hoi An: cao lầu.** Thick, chewy noodles with char siu, greens and crackling, made with water from a specific Hoi An well; it genuinely only exists here. Order it with *mì Quảng* at any busy local place in the old town; ask the hotel which this year.

### Local-life element
The old town after dark is where Hoi An itself comes out: families on the riverbank, lantern sellers, and the night market on the far bank.

### Rain plan
If it's pouring, eat near the hotel and leave the old town for tomorrow. **If Hoi An is flooding** (its risk runs to mid-December; the riverside streets were under water on 15 December 2016), use Variant A and sleep in Da Nang.

## Day 2 — Sunday 13 December · Hoi An
*Theme: the old town by day, and the Cham ruins if you want them.*

### Movements
- On foot and by bicycle in town (most hotels lend them). A car or van for My Son: ask the hotel for a quote.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 1.

### Activities
- **07:00 My Son Sanctuary**, optional. The Cham temple ruins, 37–50 km from Hoi An, 1–1.5 hours each way: 150,000 VND, open 06:00–18:00 (some sources say 17:00). Allow 2–4 hours on site; back for lunch. Go early, before the heat and the groups.
- **13:30 Hoi An Ancient Town.** A ticket for foreigners is **120,000 VND**, open 08:00–17:00. It covers a set of the old houses (Tan Ky, Phung Hung, Duc An, Quan Thang), the assembly halls (Fujian, Cantonese, Chaozhou, Hainan), the Tran Family Chapel, Quan Cong Temple, the museums and the **Japanese Covered Bridge**, which reopened on 3 August 2024 after restoration. *Sources describe the ticket differently (5 sites valid 10 days, or two tiers of 80,000 and 120,000 VND valid up to 3 days).* Folk performances at 10:15, 15:15 and 16:15.
- **17:30** The river and the lantern boats again if you skipped them last night, or rest: tomorrow is the long ride.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:30 — at the hotel**, or *bánh mì* to go if you're heading to My Son.
- **Lunch, 12:30 — Mì Quảng** in the old town. Thick turmeric rice noodles with barely any broth, shrimp and pork, peanuts, herbs and a rice cracker snapped over the top: Quang Nam's defining dish, and a daytime one.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — wherever you didn't eat cao lầu last night.** Early night.

### Local-life element
Take the bicycles out of the old town along the river towards the rice fields: Hoi An's edges are farmland, and quieter than the lanes inside the ticketed zone.

### Rain plan
The old houses and assembly halls are indoors: a wet day is a good old-town day. Skip My Son in heavy rain; its paths are open ground.

## Day 3 — Monday 14 December · Hai Van Pass → Hue
*Theme: the ride north, then the imperial city in the afternoon light.*

### Movements
- **08:00** Bikes delivered to your Hoi An hotel. 8 one-way rentals to Hue with luggage transfer: **Hidden Land Travel / Hue Scooter Rental** (from US$22 a bike, drop-off at 24A Alley 11 Ly Thuong Kiet, Hue), **Hoianit** (750,000 VND including luggage to your Hue hotel), or **Motorvina**. ~6,000,000 VND for the group. **Reserve three weeks out.**
- **Bikes:** take **semi-automatic or manual 110–150 cc** rather than automatic scooters. The descent off the pass into Lang Co is long and steep and you want engine braking; a CVT scooter on that descent cooks its brakes.
- **Route:** Hoi An → **Marble Mountains** → Da Nang (Thuan Phuoc Bridge) → **Hai Van Pass** → Lap An lagoon and **Lang Co** → Hue. **About 150 km; guided versions take 7–9 hours with stops.** The pass is 21 km of switchbacks topping out around 500 m. *One 2026 source mentions a new 70,000 VND gate ticket at Hai Van per person; no other source confirms it.*
- **Motorbikes are banned from the Hai Van tunnel.** That's fine: the tunnel is what makes the pass quiet, because the trucks and coaches are underneath you.
- **Fuel:** fill up in Hoi An, and again in Da Nang before the climb. There's nothing on the pass.
- **Luggage:** goes ahead by the rental company's van and reaches Hue in the afternoon. Carry a day bag only. Confirm the drop-off address in writing.
- **16:00** Arrive Hue.

### Accommodation (1 night)
1. **White Lotus Hue** — 5–7 Hoang Hoa Tham, south bank. Rated 9.2; ~US$68 a room with breakfast in autumn 2026 snapshots.
2. **Alba Spa Hotel** — 29 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. Rated 8.9–9.0; ~US$28–30 a room. A 10-minute walk to Dong Ba Market across Truong Tien Bridge.
3. **Silk Path Grand Hue** — 2 Le Loi, on the river. Upper mid-range, ~US$59–110 a room.

### Activities
- **07:30** Breakfast, check out; bags to the rental company's van.
- **08:15 Marble Mountains**, on the coast road south of Da Nang. Limestone hills with caves and pagodas inside: 40,000 VND to climb, *80,000 VND with the lift (the listing's wording is ambiguous)*. An hour.
- **10:30–11:30 Hai Van Pass.** Stop at the top: French bunkers, a Nguyen-dynasty gate, and the view back over Da Nang Bay on one side and ahead to Lang Co on the other. There's a café. **Go slowly on the descent**; the switchbacks are tight, the surface is patched, and the sea mist makes it wet even on a dry day.
- **12:00 Lang Co / Lap An Lagoon.** Oyster rafts, mountains behind, lunch and fuel.
- **16:00** Hue. Check in.
- **16:15 Hue Imperial City (Đại Nội).** Ticket sales close at 17:00 and the site at 17:30, so this is a first look: Thai Hoa Palace, the Nine Dynastic Urns, the To Mieu ancestral temple and the restored Kien Trung Palace. Buy the **combo for the Imperial City plus the tombs of Minh Mang, Tu Duc and Khai Dinh, 530,000 VND, valid two days**, so tomorrow's tombs are covered (200,000 VND for the Imperial City alone). *Winter hours are given as 07:00–17:30 in the most recent source; older sources say 06:30.*
- **18:00** Walk the riverfront on Le Loi and cross **Truong Tien Bridge**.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 07:00 — Hoi An**, at the hotel.
- **Lunch, 12:00 — Lang Co.** Seafood at a beach *quán*: grilled squid, clams, oysters. 150,000–250,000 VND/person. Don't eat heavily before the afternoon's riding.
- **Dinner part one, 18:30 — Hue rice cakes.** **Quán Bà Đỏ**, 7 Nguyen Binh Khiem, just off Chi Lang, or **Hàng Me Mé**, 60 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. 120,000–180,000 VND/person for a full spread: **bánh bèo**, **bánh bột lọc**, **bánh nậm** and **bánh ram ít** as a set. Court cooking that escaped into the street.
- **Dinner part two, 20:00 — Bánh khoái.** **Lạc Thiện**, 6 Dinh Tien Hoang, ~08:00–22:30, or **Chị Hạnh**, 11–15 Pho Duc Chinh, 09:00–21:00. ~100,000 VND/person. A small, turmeric-yellow crisp pancake eaten by hand with herbs and a peanut-and-liver dipping sauce; order **nem lụi** alongside.
- **Reserve for eight at Bà Đỏ and Lạc Thiện.** Both are small. The hotel will phone.

### Local-life element
**Chợ Đông Ba (Dong Ba Market)**, on the north bank by Truong Tien Bridge, roughly 06:00–19:00. Hue's central market; go just before it closes for *bánh ép*, rice-and-shrimp batter pressed flat on a hot iron, 10,000 VND a piece.

### Rain plan
**Take this seriously.** A wet Hai Van Pass with eight riders on unfamiliar bikes is the single highest-risk item in this itinerary, and mid-December is still the wet season here.

**If there is meaningful rain forecast, do not ride.** Instead:
- **The Heritage Train, Da Nang → Hue.** **HĐ2** leaves Da Nang at 07:05 and reaches Hue at 11:03; **HĐ4** leaves at 15:20 and arrives 18:15. Large observation windows and a photo stop at **Lang Co station**. *Fares are listed at 207,000 VND on weekdays, 257,000 or 330,000 VND, or 180,000–500,000 by class (sources disagree); older times were HĐ2 07:50 and HĐ4 15:00.* Sit on the **right** going north for the sea. Taxi from Hoi An to Da Nang station first.
- **Or a private van over the pass**: you still get the view from the top, with a roof.

**Decide by 07:00 on the day**, and tell the rental company when you reserve that you may cancel; get that in writing.

## Day 4 — Tuesday 15 December · Hue → Phong Nha
*Theme: the emperors at opening time, then the DMZ on the way north.*

### Movements
- **07:00 Van, Hue → Phong Nha with the tombs and Vinh Moc.** One van all day, with your bags: the tombs first, then north. ~4,500,000 VND including the stops (ask for a written quote: Culture Pham Travel quotes 3,000,000 VND Hue → Phong Nha plus US$15 for Vinh Moc; Explore Vietnam US$130 plus US$20).
- **17:00** Arrive Son Trach (Phong Nha town).

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **Phong Nha Farmstay** — 8 km out of town, surrounded by paddy. Direct rates in October 2026: standard double or twin 1,300,000 VND, family room 2,400,000 VND; check-out 11:00; a 2-night minimum has been reported. French-colonial style, pool, free bikes, and the original foreign-run guesthouse in the region (open since 2010).
2. **Thao Nguyen Homestay**, **Phong Nha Riverside Homestay** or **Jungle Boss Homestay** — In town, family-run, roughly US$15–40 a room (Jungle Boss quoted ~800,000 VND for a double, ~1,000,000 for a family room). **Several Phong Nha homestays are cash-only.**
3. **Chay Lap Farmstay** — If you book Oxalis, a discounted rate of 1,100,000 VND/room/night (~$42). Comfortable rather than local.

**Because the train leaves near midnight on Day 6**, keep two rooms until about 22:00 on the 17th for showers and repacking: book them for a third night or agree late check-out in writing.

### Activities
- **07:00 Tomb of Tu Duc** — about 7 km from town, open 07:00–17:30. Pavilions and a lake in a wooded park, the emperor's retreat as much as his tomb. About an hour.
- **08:15 Tomb of Khai Dinh** — about 10 km out. The steep, dark concrete-and-mosaic one; 40–45 minutes, with a long stair.
- **09:15 Tomb of Minh Mang** — about 12 km out, the most symmetrical of the three; about 50 minutes.
- **10:30** North through Hue towards the DMZ.
- **13:30–14:30 Vinh Moc Tunnels**, 07:00–17:00, *50,000 or 100,000 VND/person (sources disagree)*. An entire village of 300 people lived underground here for six years, in three levels of tunnel dug into the coastal clay, with a maternity room where 17 children were born. Low, dark and narrow; the most affecting hour on this route.
- **14:45** **Hien Luong Bridge** and the Ben Hai River at the 17th parallel, ten minutes on and free: half the bridge painted each colour.
- **17:00** Phong Nha. Check in; arrange tomorrow's early breakfast and Thursday's scooters.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:15 — Bún bò Huế.** **Bún Bò Huế Bà Tuyết**, 47 Nguyen Cong Tru St, Phu Hoi, ~50,000 VND, or **Quán Cẩm**, 38 Tran Cao Van, roughly 06:00–19:00. *Several well-known bún bò places open only for a few hours in the morning; breakfast is when to eat it.* Or **Cơm Hến Bà Đen** (06:00–11:30): cold rice with baby clams from the Perfume River and a lot of chilli.
- **Lunch, 12:30 — on the road.** Have the driver stop at a *quán cơm bình dân* (a point-and-choose rice shop) in Dong Ha or Gio Linh. 50,000–70,000 VND/person.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — home-cooked at your homestay.** 150,000–250,000 VND/person. Ask when you book.

### Local-life element
Ask the driver to stop at a roadside market in Dong Ha: the old DMZ towns are working places with no tourist trade, and the markets show it.

### Rain plan
The tombs are short walks from the van; in heavy rain keep **Khai Dinh** (the most covered) and drop the other two, and add the **Hue Royal Antiquities Museum** (50,000 VND) before leaving. Vinh Moc is underground.

## Day 5 — Wednesday 16 December · Phong Nha
*Theme: the actual reason you came.*

### Movements
- **07:00–07:30** Oxalis guide and driver collect you from your hotel in Phong Nha. Return around **19:30–20:00**. All transport included.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 4.

### The tour — and which one

**Oxalis day tours run on fixed dates with a 12-person cap, so your eight can effectively own a departure. Book the day you commit to the trip.** Two good December choices, both 2,000,000 VND / US$75 per person, ages 16–70:

- **Hang Tien Cave Day Trek** — Dry cave, enormous entrance, huge calcite formations, and a natural swimming pool you can decline to enter. Season November to end of September. **The better December choice for a mixed group, and the default here.**
- **Oxalis Tu Lan Experience (1 day)** — 9 km trek through valleys and river crossings, 800 m of cave, a **15 m ladder descent on harness and rope**, and **150 m swimming through an underground river in a life vest**. *Oxalis's own pages disagree on its season (one says year-round, another mid-November to mid-September).* The December caveat: cave water sits around 20–22°C and the air outside is 17–21°C with no sun to warm you afterward.

You can split the group across two tours on the same date: call the hotline rather than trying to do it through the website. Book online at oxalisadventure.com; full payment confirms; free cancellation to 7 days out. **Declare honestly** on the booking form: health conditions, medications, trekking experience.

### What to bring
Quick-dry long-sleeved shirt and long trousers, a waterproof jacket (Oxalis specifies one for November–March), closed trekking shoes with grip, **calf-length thick socks** (leeches), a change of clothes for afterward, insect repellent, towel, swimwear, passport. Oxalis lends trekking shoes in sizes 36–44 for a 50,000 VND cleaning fee; bring your own if you can.

### Food
- **All meals on the tour are included**: snack, a cooked picnic lunch, tea and coffee. Note dietary requirements in the booking form.
- **Breakfast, 06:15** — before pickup. Your homestay will do it early if you ask the night before; otherwise *bánh mì* from the market at 06:00.
- **Dinner, 20:15 — Phong Nha town.** 100,000–200,000 VND/person. **Bomb Crater Bar** and **Omar's Namaste** are the standby foreigner options; for local food, eat at whatever *quán* on the main street your homestay recommends and order **gà nướng** (grilled chicken) and rice.

### Local-life element
Oxalis employs porters, cooks and safety staff from **Tan Hoa**, a village that was 85.7% below the poverty line in 2010 and is at around 2% now, largely through this. The village also built floating houses to survive the annual floods. Ask the porters about the floods. It is not a rehearsed story.

### Rain plan
Oxalis adjusts or reroutes for weather and tells you the morning of; they don't usually cancel for rain. **If they do cancel**: swap with Day 6's show caves, or do the **Dark Cave** (zipline, mud bath and kayak; *350,000 or 450,000 VND in 2026, less off-season*). Book Dark Cave in a Phong Nha town office, not through a Hoi An agency.

## Day 6 — Thursday 17 December · Phong Nha, then the night train
*Theme: the show caves by day, then go to bed on a train.*

### Movements
- **07:00–09:30 Motorbikes, Bong Lai valley loop.** 8 scooters, ~150,000–200,000 VND each (~1,400,000 VND total), arranged on arrival. An automatic Honda Vision or Yamaha Janus is right for this. **Fill up before you leave: there is nothing out there.**
- Private van for the day from 10:00: ~2,000,000 VND. Paradise Cave is 30 km out; the Phong Nha Cave wharf is in town.
- **22:15** Transfer to **Dong Hoi station**, Thuan Ly St (now Quang Tri province, formerly Quang Binh), 40–45 km, 45–60 minutes. Two 7-seaters or one van, ~1,300,000 VND (*private-car prices run from 300,000 to 1,200,000 VND depending on the source*).
- **23:54 Train SE20 → Ninh Binh, arriving 07:59.** Two 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins. *This is Baolau's timetable for 1 November 2026; older listings give SE20 from Dong Hoi at 00:10–00:53, and no December timetable was out. Reconfirm on dsvn.vn a week out and again the day before.*

### Accommodation
**On the train tonight.** Two private 4-berth cabins: bedding, air-con and a power socket per berth. **Pack an overnight bag** so you're not opening suitcases at 07:30. The two Phong Nha rooms kept until 22:00 are for showers and repacking (see Day 4).

### Activities
- **07:00 Bong Lai valley by scooter.** A farming basin 10 km out of Son Trach with a handful of family-run stops: the **Duck Stop** (ducks, buffalo, ~150,000 VND), **Wild Boar Eco Farm**, and **the Pub With Cold Beer** (a farmhouse at the end of a dirt track). **Flag: this loop has become a circuit**, with signage and mid-morning minibuses. **Ride it at 07:00 and you'll have it to yourselves.**
- **10:00–12:00 Phong Nha Cave by boat.** Entry **150,000 VND/person**; the boat is priced per boat, max 12 people, so one boat for your eight. *Sources disagree on the boat price: 550,000 VND, 750,000 VND, or 55,000 VND per person.* You motor 1.5 km up the underground Son River into the cave, then walk out onto a sandbar. **Tien Son Cave** (80,000 VND) above it is a dry cave up a flight of steps.
- **13:30–16:00 Paradise Cave (Thiên Đường).** **270,000 VND/person** including the electric buggy (October 2026; older sources say 250,000 plus the buggy). **It closes at 16:00**, so arrive by 13:30. 500 steps up, then a boardwalk through a chamber so large the lighting doesn't reach the ceiling; the full cave runs 31 km.
- **16:30** **The Phong Nha Caves Exploration Museum** in town: free, 08:00–17:30. Hồ Khanh, a local logger, found the entrance to Son Doong in 1991 while sheltering from a storm and couldn't relocate it for years.
- **19:00** Dinner, showers in the kept rooms, rest. **22:15** Station.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:30 — Chợ Troc / Son Trach morning market**, central Phong Nha, from ~05:30. 30,000–40,000 VND/person. Order **bánh mì** with pâté and pickled carrot and a bowl of **bún**.
- **Lunch, 12:15 — Phong Nha Bamboo Restaurant** or **Mountain View Restaurant**, both on the main street in Son Trach. 80,000–150,000 VND/person. Order **cá kho tộ** (fish braised in a clay pot) and **lươn xào nghệ** (eel stir-fried with turmeric). *Restaurant turnover in Phong Nha is high; ask your hosts what's good this month.*
- **Dinner, 19:00 — at the homestay.** **Eat properly before the train**: the trolley on board sells instant noodles and beer, and that's all.

### Local-life element
Ask your hosts about the floods: Phong Nha's homes and farms sit on the river plain, and every family has a flood story.

### Rain plan
Both caves are caves: rain changes nothing about Phong Nha Cave or Paradise Cave except the drive. **What rain does change:** the road to Paradise Cave can flood after heavy rain, and the park very occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm. If it's raining at 06:30, skip the scooters. If Paradise Cave is shut, do the museum and the Dark Cave instead.

## Day 7 — Friday 18 December · Ninh Binh
*Theme: off the train and straight onto the karst.*

### Movements
- **07:59** Arrive **Ninh Binh Railway Station**, 1 Hoang Dieu St (some listings say 41 Hoang Hoa Tham — the station has been re-addressed; both refer to the same place, central Ninh Binh city).
- **08:15** Van to Tam Coc, 20 minutes, then on standby for the day with bicycles, ~2,000,000 VND. Provincial names changed in July 2025: Tam Coc's area is now **Ninh Hải**. Give drivers the hotel name and a pinned map location, not the postal address.

### Accommodation (1 night)
1. **Tam Coc River View Homestay** — 4.7 km from Ninh Binh centre, 300 m from Tam Coc wharf. Five rooms including quadruples and a family room, so the eight of you can take the whole property.
2. **Tam Coc Rice Fields Resort** — From about €39 a room (Expedia, July 2026 snapshot).
3. **Tam Coc Garden** — Restored brick-and-tile buildings in a garden of fish ponds. Now a boutique resort rather than a homestay, from about US$248 a night for two (Expedia, May 2026).

**You arrive at 08:30, long before check-in**: ask for early check-in or at least a room for showers, in writing.

### Activities
- **08:30** Drop bags, shower, breakfast.
- **10:00–11:00 Bích Động Pagoda**, 2 km from Tam Coc wharf. Free. Three tiers of temple built into a limestone cliff, reached by a stone stair through a cave. Usually nearly empty because everyone is at the boats.
- **11:30** Cycle or drive to **Hoa Lu ancient capital**. 20,000 VND. Vietnam's capital from 968 to 1010, now two modest temples to the Dinh and Le emperors in a bowl of limestone. Forty minutes.
- **12:30** Lunch.
- **14:00 Mua Cave (Hang Múa)**, **150,000 VND** since 1 April 2026, open 06:00–19:00. **486 stone steps** to a Quan Am statue and a dragon ridge with the best aerial view of the Tam Coc valley. Steep, uneven, with a handrail. **This is the split point for the mixed hiking appetite**: there's a café and lotus pond at the bottom for anyone who'd rather not.
- **15:45–17:30 Van Long Nature Reserve**, ~40 minutes north (Gia Vien). Entry around 20,000 VND plus a boat at 50,000–60,000 VND per person (one older provincial list gives 100,000 VND all-in), **cash only, small notes**. Flat-bottomed boats rowed by local women, under limestone cliffs. **Late afternoon is right**: the herons and egrets come in to roost and, if you're lucky, the Delacour's langurs come down the cliffs.
- **18:30** Back to the hotel.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 08:45 — at the hotel.**
- **Lunch, 12:45 — Nhà hàng Đức Dê** or any of the goat restaurants signposted *dê núi* along the Tam Coc road. ~200,000 VND/person. Order **dê tái chanh** (goat "cooked" in lime juice with sesame and herbs), **dê nướng lá lốt** (grilled in wild betel leaf) and **cơm cháy**. *Hours for individual goat restaurants aren't verifiable; there are a dozen and they're all open at lunch.*
- **Dinner, 19:00 — at the hotel.** Ask for **ốc nấu chuối đậu** (snails stewed with green banana and tofu), a northern home dish you will almost never see on a menu.

### Local-life element
**Rent bicycles** (usually free or ~50,000 VND/day) and ride the lanes between Tam Coc and Ninh Hai after Hoa Lu. In December the paddies are between crops: fields being turned, ducks herded down the lanes. Slow down, nod, and don't photograph people's houses without a gesture first.

### Rain plan
Van Long boats run in light rain: take the rain cape the boatwoman offers. **Skip Mua Cave if it's wet**: the steps are polished limestone and genuinely dangerous slick. Substitute **Bái Đính Pagoda** (free entry, electric cart 60,000 VND), the largest pagoda complex in Vietnam, vast and covered.

*Theme: the famous karst at opening time, then the capital on a Saturday night.*

- **Breakfast, 07:00 — at the hotel**, or *bánh mì* to go before Trang An.
- **Lunch, 12:00 — Nhà hàng Đức Dê** again, or *cơm cháy* at the hotel before the van.

One dish only: **chả cá**, turmeric-marinated catfish grilled in banana leaf and finished at your table with a hedge of dill and spring onion, over rice vermicelli with peanuts and *mắm tôm* shrimp paste. **Reserve for eight**: the pale-yellow townhouse at 6B is the good branch of three on the same street.

Trang An operates year-round and is largely sheltered: go in rain. In Hanoi, the walking street is suspended in bad weather; the water puppets are indoors either way.

## Day 9 — Sunday 20 December · Hanoi
*Theme: the national landmarks, in the right order.*

### Movements
- All on foot and by Grab. Download **Grab** before you fly and add a card; a Grab bike across the Old Quarter is 20,000–30,000 VND. Eight people in Grab cars is two or three vehicles.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 8.

### Activities
- **06:15** Market breakfast at Chau Long (below), ten minutes' walk from the mausoleum.
- **07:45 Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum** — Free. Open Sundays 08:00–11:00 in the cool season, closed Mondays and Fridays, so today is the day. Arrive early: the queue is long and the rules are strict (shoulders and knees covered, no shorts, bags stored, phones and cameras collected, silence, hats off).
- **09:00** **The Presidential Palace grounds and the stilt house** behind the mausoleum: 25,000 or 40,000 VND, open mornings. The **One Pillar Pagoda**, next to the mausoleum, is free.
- **10:30 Temple of Literature** — 58 Quoc Tu Giam St; 08:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30); 70,000 VND, cash. Vietnam's 1070 Confucian academy: the stelae of doctoral laureates sit on stone tortoises in the third courtyard. One hour is enough.
- **12:00** Lunch (below).
- **13:30 Hoa Lo Prison** — 08:00–17:00 daily, ~50,000 VND (sources range 30,000–50,000), audio guide 50,000 VND. The French colonial prison later known as the "Hanoi Hilton". About an hour; ten minutes' walk south of the lake.
- **15:00 Museum of Ethnology** — In Cau Giay, 08:30–17:30 (closed Mondays), 40,000 VND, 25 minutes by Grab. The outdoor collection of reconstructed minority houses is the best thing of its kind in the country. **Or Thang Long Imperial Citadel** (100,000 VND, 08:00–17:00), the excavated royal citadel near the mausoleum.
- **17:30** **Long Bien Bridge** at dusk (below).
- **20:00** *Bia hơi* — fresh draught beer, brewed and delivered daily, 15,000–20,000 VND a glass. Go to the corner of **Nguyen Huu Huan and Hang Thung** or **Bia Hơi Hải Xồm**, rather than Ta Hien, which is now largely a backpacker street.

### Food
- **Market breakfast, 06:15 — Chợ Châu Long (Chau Long Market)**, Chau Long St, Ba Dinh. Opens around 05:30, busiest 06:00–08:00. A working neighbourhood market: live fish in basins, women shelling crab, a dozen breakfast stalls along the interior aisles. 30,000–50,000 VND/person. Eat **bánh cuốn** and **xôi xéo**. Bring small notes; nobody here takes cards.
- **Lunch, 12:00 — Bún Chả Đắc Kim**, 1 Hang Manh St, Hoan Kiem. Open ~10:00–14:00. 70,000–100,000 VND/person. Order **bún chả** (grilled pork patties and belly in a sweet-sour fish sauce broth with a mountain of herbs) and **nem cua bể**, the square sea-crab spring rolls. In the 2026 Michelin Guide, still a plastic-stool operation.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — Phở Hà**, 15 Hang Hom St, Hang Gai, Hoan Kiem. ~60,000–80,000 VND. Order **phở gà** and **bún thang**, the most technically demanding dish in the Hanoi repertoire.

### Local-life element
Walk up to **Long Bien Bridge** at dusk and cross the pedestrian walkway on the upstream side. It's the steel cantilever bridge over the Red River whose builder's plate reads 1899–1902, Daydé & Pillé, Paris, still carrying scooters and some trains (fewer since February 2026). At dusk it fills with commuters, couples and people fishing off the piers. Stand aside for scooters.

### Rain plan
Everything today is indoors or a short walk between buildings. If it's properly wet, drop the Imperial Citadel for the Museum of Ethnology and add the **Vietnamese Women's Museum** (36 Ly Thuong Kiet, 08:00–17:00, 40,000 VND) or **Bảo tàng Mỹ thuật Việt Nam** (Fine Arts Museum, 66 Nguyen Thai Hoc, 08:30–17:00, 40,000 VND).

## 4. Regional food primer

Food on this route is the thing you do between the landmarks, not instead of them, but it's very good, and it changes every few hundred kilometres.

**Hoi An, Da Nang and Quang Nam.** Bold, coastal, built on turmeric. Broth is used sparingly.
- **Must eat:** *cao lầu* (Hoi An only) · *mì Quảng* · seafood at Lang Co
- **Best on your route:** Hoi An, Days 1–2; Lang Co on the ride.

**Hue.** The most distinctive regional cuisine in Vietnam: the imperial court's habit of many small, precisely composed dishes, and a street tradition of chilli-and-shrimp-paste noodle soups. *Mắm ruốc* (fermented shrimp paste) is the base note under almost everything.
- **Must eat:** *bún bò Huế* · *cơm hến* · *bánh bèo / bánh bột lọc / bánh nậm / bánh ram ít* (as a set) · *bánh khoái* with *nem lụi* · *bánh ép* from Dong Ba
- **Best on your route:** Hue, Day 3 evening and Day 4 breakfast.

**Quang Binh / Phong Nha.** The plainest cooking on the route, and not a failing: river fish, eel, chicken and clay-pot braises.
- **Must eat:** *cá kho tộ* (clay-pot braised fish) · *lươn xào nghệ* (eel with turmeric)
- **Best on your route:** Son Trach town, and your homestay's kitchen.

**Ninh Binh.** A small, specific repertoire built on the limestone hills.
- **Must eat:** *cơm cháy* (crisped rice cake) · *dê núi* (mountain goat) · *ốc nấu chuối đậu* if you can find someone's grandmother to cook it
- **Best on your route:** Tam Coc and Ninh Binh city.

**Hanoi and the north.** Restrained, savoury, sour, herb-forward. Salt and fish sauce rather than sugar; vinegar and lime rather than chilli. Dill appears in northern cooking and nowhere else in Vietnam.
- **Must eat:** *bún chả* · *chả cá* · *bún thang* · *bánh cuốn* · *phở gà*
- **Best on your route:** Hanoi, Days 8–9.

**Ordering mechanics.** Most street places serve one thing; the menu is the sign. At a *quán*, sit and someone will come. Pay after eating. *Rau* (herbs) and *tương ớt* (chilli sauce) on the table are free and unlimited. Ice in cylinders with a hole through the middle is factory-made and safe; crushed ice is less reliable.

## 7. Cash and payments

**Carry 3,000,000–4,000,000 VND per person in cash** for a trip of this length, and top up from ATMs rather than arriving with all of it. Where cards work, use them; where they don't, it's absolute.

**Cards fail at:** every street food stall and market · Van Long · Mua Cave · the Temple of Literature (cash only) · most Phong Nha homestays · goat restaurants in Tam Coc · Bong Lai valley farms · the Phong Nha Cave boat · petrol stations for small fills · Grab bikes if you haven't linked a card.

**Cards work at:** hotels, Trang An, the Hue Imperial City, Oxalis (pay in advance online), mid-range restaurants in Hoi An, Hue and Hanoi, supermarkets.

**ATMs.** Use bank-branded machines inside bank premises: **Vietcombank, BIDV, Techcombank, VietinBank, TPBank**. Avoid standalone machines in tourist lanes. Most cap withdrawals at 3,000,000–5,000,000 VND per transaction and charge 22,000–55,000 VND per withdrawal; **TPBank and some Techcombank machines dispense up to 10,000,000 VND** with lower fees. ATMs in Son Trach and Tam Coc village are sparse to nonexistent — **withdraw at Hanoi airport on arrival, then in Hoi An and Hue before Phong Nha.**

**Notes.** 500,000 VND notes are hard to break at a market stall. Ask for a mix. The 20,000 (blue) and 500,000 (blue-green) notes look similar in poor light and this is a real and common error.

**Tipping.** Not expected, increasingly present.
- Street food, markets, *quán*: nothing.
- Restaurants: round up, or 5–10% if there's no service charge.
- Homestay family dinner: nothing, but a gift for the household if they've gone out of their way.
- Boat rowers at Van Long and Trang An: 50,000–100,000 VND per boat is normal and welcome.
- **Oxalis guides, porters and cooks: the one place to tip properly.** A pooled 500,000–1,000,000 VND from the group, handed to the guide for the team.
- Drivers: 100,000–200,000 VND for a good full day.
- Hotel staff: 20,000–50,000 VND for bags.

**Payment apps.** Vietnamese QR payment (VietQR) is nearly universal among locals, and you can't use it without a Vietnamese bank account. Cash remains the tourist's tool.

## 8. Packing for December, central to north

The temperature swing on this route is the thing people get wrong, and going north it gets colder as the trip goes on. Hue averages a high of about **24°C** and a low of about **19°C**, with around 350 mm of rain in December, more of it in the first half. Ninh Binh is **15–21°C**. Hanoi sits at **15–22°C** with lows near 14°C, high humidity and grey drizzle; it feels considerably colder than the number, and buildings are not heated. You are packing for two different climates nine days apart.

**Bring:**
- **A proper rain shell**, not a poncho. You will use it in Hoi An and Hue, and possibly on Hai Van. Oxalis specifies a waterproof jacket for November–March tours.
- **Layers for the north at the end:** a thin merino or fleece mid-layer, a long-sleeved shirt, and something warmer for Hanoi evenings. Nothing heavy.
- **Quick-dry long trousers and a long-sleeved quick-dry shirt** for the Oxalis day: required for protection against rock, insects and plants.
- **Trekking or approach shoes with real grip**, broken in. Mua Cave's 486 steps and the Oxalis trail both demand it.
- **Calf-length thick socks** — for leeches on the Oxalis trek in wet conditions.
- **Swimwear and a quick-dry towel** — the cave pools, whether or not you go in.
- **A second pair of shoes** so you're not putting wet ones on the next morning.
- **Clothes that cover shoulders and knees** for the Hue tombs, pagodas and the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum.
- **A small dry bag** for phones on the boats and in the caves.
- **Motorcycle gloves and, if you're serious, your own helmet.** Rental helmets in Vietnam are legally compliant and not much more. A jacket with some abrasion resistance for the Hai Van day.
- **Headtorch** — useful on unlit lanes and boarding a train at midnight.
- **Small notes wallet** separate from your main one.
- **A universal adapter** — Vietnam uses Type A/C/F at 220 V.
- **Rehydration salts and loperamide.** Not pessimism, arithmetic: eight people, nine days, a lot of street food.
- **An overnight bag for the train** so you don't open suitcases in a sleeper cabin.
- **Passports on you** for the flights, the Oxalis tour, the train and bike rental.

**Don't bring:** a heavy coat, hiking poles (unnecessary on these trails), a drone (restricted around the Citadel, the national parks and the caves), or expectations about laundry — it's 20,000–40,000 VND/kg everywhere, done same-day; use it in Hoi An before the ride.

## 9. Group logistics: where eight people will be awkward

**The first day.** Eight people through immigration, baggage claim and a terminal change in under four hours is the tightest moment of the trip. Agree before you fly who carries the Da Nang booking reference, and regroup at Terminal 1 check-in rather than waiting for everyone at the carousel.

**Boats.** Trang An boats hold 4–5, so you're split across two. Van Long boats hold 2–3, so three or four boats. **You will not all be in one boat at any point.** Agree in advance who's with whom, and a meeting point at the other end.

**Restaurant tables.** Almost no street-food place in Vietnam has a table for eight. The normal solution is two or three tables of three or four, pushed roughly together. **The places where you must reserve:** Quán Bà Đỏ and Lạc Thiện (Hue), Chả Cá Thăng Long (Hanoi), and any goat restaurant in Tam Coc. Everywhere else, arrive before the rush — 11:30 for lunch, 18:00 for dinner.

**Vehicles.** A 16-seater takes eight people and eight large bags with room to spare. A 9-seat "Dcar limousine" does not, because the rear seats recline into the luggage space. Do not accept two 7-seaters split across the group unless you have to.

**The night train.** Two 4-berth cabins means the group splits 4/4 for the night. Decide the split before you book, because you're buying specific berths. Upper berths have less headroom; assign accordingly.

**The Oxalis tour.** Eight of a 12-person cap. **Book all eight in one transaction** so you're on the same departure. Ask on the phone whether you can have a private departure.

**Motorbikes.** Eight bikes is more than a small shop stocks in one model. Book early, and expect a mix of models. **Ride in two groups of four** with a designated lead and sweep in each, and agree stop points in advance: eight bikes trying to stay together through Da Nang traffic will fragment within a kilometre.

**Hotels.** Room counts are the constraint, not price. **Ask for the room count and configuration explicitly**: "two doubles and two twins" is what you want, and "one dorm and a family room" is not the same thing.

**The mausoleum queue.** Eight people, no bags, no phones: decide who holds the bags outside, or use the bag store, before you join the queue.

**Pace.** Eight adults move at roughly 70% of the speed of two. Every time above has slack in it, but the compounding effect is real: assume 20 minutes of drift on every departure and build it into the morning starts.

## 11. Risk notes

### Motorbike licensing — read this before you book bikes

This is the highest-consequence detail in the document, and the one your group is most likely to get wrong precisely because you all hold motorcycle licences.

**Vietnam recognises only the 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit.** It is not a party to the 1949 Geneva Convention. A 1949 IDP has, in the words of one rental operator, "zero legal weight": Vietnamese police treat it as no licence at all.

- **1968 IDPs** are issued by the UK, Germany, France and most of the EU, including Greece. The **UK issues both versions**: you must explicitly ask for the 1968 permit.
- **1949 IDPs** are issued by the **USA, Canada and Japan**. *Australia is reported inconsistently across sources — verify with your own state motoring authority rather than trusting any travel site.*
- The IDP must show **motorcycle category A**, must be carried **together with your original national licence**, and your national licence must itself entitle you to ride motorcycles. An IDP based on a car licence does not let you ride.
- Carry **both physical documents** whenever you ride. Enforcement has tightened since the 2024 traffic law overhaul: more checkpoints, higher fines, body cameras.

**The insurance consequence is the real risk, not the fine.** If you are riding without a licence Vietnam recognises, **your travel and medical insurance is voided.** In a serious accident you are personally liable for your own medical costs, third-party damage and the bike.

**Practical upshot.** Check every rider's IDP version **now**, not in December. If any rider is on a 1949 IDP, that person takes the train or a van on Day 3 and skips the scooters on Day 6. Rental shops will hand bikes to anyone; that transfers none of the liability.

Separately: the bike must carry **compulsory Vietnamese third-party liability insurance** — ask to see it, it's a paper slip. And read your own travel policy's motorcycle clause, including any engine-capacity cap (many exclude over 125 cc) and helmet requirements.

### Road safety
- **Helmets always**, including on the Bong Lai farm lanes. It's the law and it's enforced.
- **Ride in two groups of four.** Eight bikes cannot hold formation.
- **The Hai Van descent** northbound drops into Lang Co: tight switchbacks, patched surface, sea mist. Engine braking, not brake braking. Go slowly.
- **Day 3 is two days after a long-haul flight.** Sleep properly on Day 2; nobody rides tired.
- **Buffalo, dogs and children** appear without warning on rural lanes. Do not ride Bong Lai or the Tam Coc lanes after dark.
- **Deposits:** rental shops commonly ask for a passport or $300 per bike. **Pay the cash deposit, never surrender a passport.** You legally need it with you.
- **Photograph every bike** from all sides before you ride it, with the odometer and any existing damage, and get the rental contract in writing.

### Cave tour safety
Oxalis is the serious operator here and the safety infrastructure is real: gear from France, the UK and Switzerland, guides trained by British Caving Association members, a satellite phone on tour, and dedicated rescue teams. But note the honest limitation in their own material: **rescue and medical support take 30 minutes to 2 hours to reach you**, and phone signal is negligible in the valleys.

Declare medical conditions truthfully on the booking form. Bring your own medication. Follow the guides. If anyone has a serious problem with heights or enclosed dark water, **book Hang Tien, not Tu Lan.**

### Seasonal and weather
- **The central coast comes first, in the wetter half of December.** The chance of a wet day in Hoi An and Hue falls from about 41% on 1 December to about 22% by the 31st; Hue averages around 350 mm of rain in December. Days 1–4 are the ones most likely to be rained on.
- **Hoi An floods.** The old town's flood risk runs from late October to mid-December. On 15 December 2016 the riverside streets were under water; in 2024 Hue's rivers were near alarm level 2 on 14 December. Watch the forecast in the week before you fly, and use Variant A if Hoi An is flooding.
- **Phong Nha–Ke Bang** occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm, and the road to Paradise Cave floods. Have Days 5 and 6 ready to swap.
- **Cold water.** Cave water sits at 20–22°C and December air won't warm you afterward. A comfort risk, not a safety one.
- **The north is dry but grey.** Persistent drizzle and fog are normal in Hanoi in December, and buildings are unheated.
- **Leeches** on the Oxalis trails in wet conditions. Thick calf-length socks.

### Health and general
- **Ice:** cylindrical with a hole through the middle is factory-made and safe. Crushed ice is less reliable.
- **Street food:** pick busy stalls. Turnover is the safety mechanism.
- **Water:** bottled or filtered only.
- **Pharmacies** are on every corner and stock more than you'd expect without prescription.
- **Nearest serious hospital to Phong Nha** is in Dong Hoi, 45 km. Nearest international-standard care is Da Nang or Hanoi. Factor this into your insurance choice.
- **Traffic as a pedestrian:** cross at a constant speed without stopping. Scooters flow around you. Stopping mid-road is what causes accidents.
- **Train Street (Hanoi):** groups are banned and barriers are up; the cafés operate in a grey zone under police watch. Optional and at your own risk.

## 12. Verify before you go

Things that could not be pinned down in the October 2026 re-check, or that moved recently. Check each a week out.

**Administrative reorganisation.** On 1 July 2025 Vietnam consolidated from 63 provinces to 34 and abolished the district tier. On your route: **Quang Nam no longer exists** — Hoi An is now in **Da Nang City**. **Thua Thien Hue** is now simply **Hue**, a centrally governed city. **Quang Binh no longer exists** — Phong Nha is now in **Quang Tri province**. **Ninh Binh** absorbed Ha Nam and Nam Dinh. Booking-site addresses, Google Maps labels and guidebook references are inconsistent; commune names have changed (Tam Coc's area is now **Ninh Hải**). **Give drivers hotel names and pinned map locations, not postal addresses.**

**Places I can't confirm:**
- **Hanoi hotels.** Essence Hanoi (22 Ta Hien) now appears as "Aira Boutique"; La Siesta's own site returned an error, though booking sites list it.
- **Tam Coc Garden.** Now a boutique resort, not the homestay older guides describe; the late check-out fee isn't published.
- **Phong Nha restaurants.** Turnover is high and several names in older guides have closed. Ask your hosts.
- **Individual goat restaurants in Tam Coc.** A dozen signposted *dê núi* places; hours unverifiable.
- **Bún bò Huế opening hours.** The best shops open early and close when the pot empties.

**Weather to check:**
- **Hoi An floods.** Risk runs to mid-December, which is when you arrive; check the forecast in the week before you fly.

**Exchange rate.** 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND in mid-September 2026, having ranged 25,900–26,400 over the preceding year. All USD figures in this document will drift.

**Visa.** Vietnam's e-visa programme covers nationals of all countries and territories, with visa exemptions for some. Check your own nationality's current requirement and apply well ahead, and note that hotels and tour operators are legally required to register your temporary residence, so **everyone needs their passport with them throughout**.
