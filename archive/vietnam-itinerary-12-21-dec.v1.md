# Northern → Central Vietnam, 12–21 December

**Group:** 6–8 adults · **Riders:** all experienced, motorcycle licences held · **Hiking:** mixed appetite, need easy and hard options · **Diet:** no restrictions

**Dates fall on a weekend you should know about:** 12 December 2026 is a **Saturday**. So Hanoi is a weekend (good for the Hoan Kiem walking street and night market), Ninh Binh is Mon–Wed (good — fewer domestic coaches), and Hue/Da Nang are Sat–Sun (busier, and the heritage train charges a weekend surcharge). Departure day, 21 December, is a Monday.

**Exchange rate used throughout:** 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND (mid-September 2026; it has ranged 25,900–26,400 over the past year). All prices are stated in VND where I have them and converted for convenience.

## 1. The route, and what it costs you

### Chosen route

| Nights | Base | Nights | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12–13 Dec | **Hanoi** | 2 | Food is a primary reason for the trip; this is the deepest food city on the route. Also the only sane place to absorb jet lag. |
| 14–15 Dec | **Ninh Binh (Tam Coc / Hoa Lu)** | 2 | Best karst-and-river landscape reachable in the dry northern winter, plus Cuc Phuong and Van Long as genuine nature days. |
| 16 Dec | **Night train SE19** | 1 (on rails) | Converts a 9-hour road slog into a night you'd have spent in bed anyway. This is the single biggest time win in the itinerary. |
| 17–18 Dec | **Phong Nha (Son Trach)** | 2 | The nature centrepiece. World-class karst, real caving, and the least performative tourism economy on the route. |
| 19 Dec | **Hue** | 1 | Vietnam's most distinctive regional cuisine. One night is a compromise — see below. |
| 20 Dec | **Da Nang (Hai Chau)** | 1 | Hai Van Pass ride terminates here, and DAD is 3 km from the city centre for the pre-dawn flight. |

Five bases, nine nights, one of them on a train. No backtracking: the route is a single southward line.

### What you're giving up

**Pu Luong and Mai Chau — cut.** This is the decision you're most likely to argue with, so here is the reasoning. Pu Luong is quieter than Ninh Binh, has better homestays and better walking, and is exactly the kind of place your brief describes. But in mid-December: the terraces are bare stubble (harvest is September–October), the valley sits under fog for days at a time, and daytime highs are 13–17°C with high humidity. You would also pay for it in driving hours — Hanoi → Pu Luong is 4.5–5 hours west, then Pu Luong → Ninh Binh is another 3, versus a flat 2h15 Hanoi → Ninh Binh on the CT01 expressway. That's roughly six hours of van time bought for landscape that's at its worst point in the year. **If you want it anyway, see Variant B below.**

**Hoi An — cut as a base, optional as an evening.** Cao lau genuinely only exists properly in Hoi An, and I don't want to pretend otherwise. But the old town is the single most tour-managed place on your list, a pre-dawn airport run from Hoi An is 45 minutes rather than 10, and squeezing it into the evening of Day 9 after riding Hai Van makes for a miserable last night. Da Nang gives you mi quang and banh xeo at their source with none of that. Optional Hoi An evening is noted on Day 9 if you disagree.

**Bach Ma — cut.** Asked and answered: don't go in December. The summit sits in cloud for most of the month, the waterfall trails are slick, and the road to the top is sometimes shut after rain. Entry is only 60,000 VND, so it isn't the money — it's that you would burn half a day in fog. Phong Nha is the better forest.

**Halong Bay — never on the list, correctly.** Nothing to add except agreement.

**Cuc Phuong — kept, as a day trip not a base.** Accommodation inside the park is institutional and the food is poor. One hour from Tam Coc gets you the Endangered Primate Rescue Centre and old-growth forest without sleeping there.

**Hue at one night — the real cost of this route.** You get an afternoon, an evening, a dinner, a breakfast and two hours at the Citadel. That's enough to eat well but not enough to see the tombs. There is no way to fix it inside nine nights without breaking your two-night rule somewhere else.

### Variant A — give Hue two nights

Cut the Phong Nha arrival-day cave programme and leave Phong Nha on the morning of 19 Dec having done only the Oxalis trek: Ninh Binh 14–15, train 16, Phong Nha 17–18, **Hue 19–20**, then a pre-dawn 21 Dec transfer Hue → DAD (2.5 hours by road, leaving Hue around 02:00). **I don't recommend it.** A 02:00 departure after a long trip is the kind of decision that sours a final day, and Hue's tombs are not worth it relative to a second Phong Nha cave.

### Variant B — keep Pu Luong, drop Ninh Binh

Hanoi 12–13, **Pu Luong 14–15**, then Pu Luong → Thanh Hoa station (2.5h) for the night train south on 16 Dec, Phong Nha 17–18, Hue 19, Da Nang 20. Same shape, same number of moves. You trade Trang An's karst rivers and Cuc Phuong's forest for Thai-minority villages, better homestays and worse weather. If your group's priority word is "local life" rather than "nature", take this variant. If it's "nature", keep the main route.

### One thing I'd add that you didn't list

**Van Long Nature Reserve**, 40 minutes north of Tam Coc. It's a wetland with the same karst but a fraction of the traffic — flat-bottomed boats rowed by local women, no ticket queue, and a resident population of Delacour's langur on the cliffs. It's the answer to "Trang An but without the coaches," and it's built into Day 3.

**Total transport, group:** roughly 32,500,000 VND ≈ **$1,250**, or **$156 per person** at eight.

### How to book each of these

- **Private vans (Hanoi and Ninh Binh legs):** Ford Transit or Hyundai Solati 16-seaters run $160–200/day including driver, fuel, tolls and parking; airport transfers $50–100 one way. Book by WhatsApp two to three weeks out. HanoiCarRent (Hoan Kiem-based, hanoicarrent.com) and Asia Transport quote these rates publicly. **For eight people plus nine sets of luggage, insist on a 16-seater, not a 9-seat Dcar limousine** — the Dcar's rear seats fold into recliners and the boot disappears.
- **Night train:** Book 3–4 weeks ahead. Two routes: state railway via **dsvn.vn** (cheapest, patchy English, often rejects non-Vietnamese cards) or a reseller like **Baolau** or **Vexere**, which cost 10–20% more but take foreign cards and issue e-tickets. You want **two whole 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins** (Vietnamese: *giường nằm mềm điều hòa*) so your eight fill both and nobody shares with strangers. If those are gone, the private operators **Lotus Train** (lotustrain.vn, WhatsApp +84 868 060 269) and **Laman Express** attach refurbished cabins to the same SE19 service at 1,800,000–3,900,000 VND per person — a large premium for nicer bedding. Exchange the e-ticket voucher for a paper ticket at the station at least 30–60 minutes before departure.
- **Dong Hoi → Phong Nha:** Oxalis runs airport/station transfers at 650,000 VND per 7-seater one way. Book it inside your Oxalis tour booking and they'll meet the 06:25 train.
- **Phong Nha → Hue:** Culture Pham Travel and Phong Nha Locals both quote publicly — around $90–100 for a sedan, more for a minivan; budget $170–180 for a 16-seater with a Vinh Moc stop. Arrange 1–2 weeks out by email or WhatsApp.
- **Hue → Da Nang motorbikes:** **Style Motorbikes** (stylemotorbikes.com) does one-way Hue↔Hoi An/Da Nang with free luggage transfer. So does **Hidden Land Travel / Hue Scooter Rental** (huescooterrental.com, WhatsApp +84 348 421 282) and **Motorvina**. Reserve eight bikes at least three weeks ahead — eight is more than a small shop keeps in one size class. Expect a deposit of one passport or $300 per bike; **hand over cash, not passports** (see §11).

## 3. Day by day

## Day 1 — Saturday 12 December · Hanoi
*Theme: land, eat, don't be ambitious.*

### Movements
- **11:00** Van from Noi Bai, Terminal 2 arrivals. 35–40 km, ~1 hour to the Old Quarter. ~1,200,000 VND for a 16-seater. The driver should be holding a name board outside the arrivals hall.
- Book through your hotel (most Old Quarter hotels run their own cars and it's the least friction) or HanoiCarRent. Do **not** rely on airport taxi touts with eight people and eight bags — you'll end up split across three cars at inflated rates.
- Everything today is on foot. The Old Quarter is roughly 1 km across.

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **Hanoi La Siesta Classic Ma May** — Ma May St, Hoan Kiem. From ~1,400,000 VND ($56)/double. Four twin/double rooms sleeps eight. Rooftop bar with a view over to Long Bien Bridge. Book direct or Agoda. Chosen because it's two blocks off the noise of Ta Hien but still inside the Old Quarter food grid.
2. **Essence Hanoi Hotel & Spa** — 22 Ta Hien St. From ~1,200,000 VND ($48)/double. Cheaper, right on Beer Corner — take an upper floor or you will not sleep on a Saturday.

**Budget note:** your stated band is $40–80 per person per night. At eight people sharing four rooms, that's $320–640 a night, and nothing on this route needs that. See §6 — you are substantially over-budgeted, which is a nice problem.

### Activities
- **12:00** Check in, shower, do not nap for more than 45 minutes. Jet lag from the west is best broken by staying up to a local bedtime.
- **14:00–16:00** Walk a loop: Ma May → Hang Bac (silversmiths) → Hang Ma (paper goods) → Dong Xuan Market → Hang Chieu → back down Hang Buom. No tickets, no queues. The Old Quarter's street names are its trade names, and walking it is the cheapest orientation there is.
- **16:30** Coffee at **Cafe Dinh**, 13 Dinh Tien Hoang — up an unmarked staircase beside a silk shop, second floor, overlooking the lake. Egg coffee (*cà phê trứng*), ~30,000 VND. This is where egg coffee is actually drunk rather than photographed.
- **19:00–22:00** **Hoan Kiem weekend walking street.** The roads around the lake close to traffic Friday evening to Sunday midnight. Families, badminton, street games, and the least filtered version of Hanoi's public life you'll see. Free. Worth deliberately timing — this only happens at weekends and you have the right weekend.

### Food
- **Lunch, 12:45 — Bún Chả Đắc Kim**, 1 Hang Manh St, Hoan Kiem. Open ~10:00–14:00. 70,000–100,000 VND/person. Order **bún chả** (grilled pork patties and belly in a sweet-sour fish sauce broth with a mountain of herbs) and **nem cua bể**, the square sea-crab spring rolls. In the 2026 Michelin Guide, still a plastic-stool operation. It's the single best first meal in Vietnam because it explains the whole northern flavour logic in one bowl. Eight will need to split across two tables; go at 12:30, not 13:00.
- **Afternoon snack, 16:00 — Bánh Gối at 52 Ly Quoc Su.** ~15,000 VND each. Deep-fried "pillow dumplings" of pork, glass noodle and quail egg. Order four each; you will regret ordering two.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — Chả Cá Thăng Long**, 6B Duong Thanh St, Cua Dong Ward, Hoan Kiem. Michelin Bib Gourmand. ~200,000–250,000 VND/person. One dish only: **chả cá** — turmeric-marinated catfish (*cá lăng*) grilled in banana leaf, then finished at your table in a pan with a hedge of dill and spring onion, eaten over rice vermicelli with peanuts and *mắm tôm* shrimp paste. Say yes to the shrimp paste. **Reserve for eight** — call the day before; the pale-yellow townhouse at 6B is the good branch of three on the same street and it fills up.

### Local-life element
Walk up to **Long Bien Bridge** at dusk and cross the pedestrian walkway on the upstream side. It's the 1903 Eiffel-designed cantilever bridge, still carrying trains and scooters, and at 17:30 it fills with commuters, couples and people fishing off the piers. Free, five minutes' walk from Ma May. Stand aside for scooters — the walkway is narrow and they use it.

### Rain plan
December rain in Hanoi is drizzle, not downpour, and the whole day works under an umbrella. If it's properly wet: swap the walking loop for the **Vietnamese Women's Museum** (36 Ly Thuong Kiet, 08:00–17:00, 40,000 VND) — genuinely good on ethnic minority textiles and marriage customs, and mercifully small.

## Day 2 — Sunday 13 December · Hanoi
*Theme: the 6am food scene, then a kitchen.*

### Movements
- All on foot and by Grab bike. Download **Grab** before you fly and add a card; a Grab bike across the Old Quarter is 20,000–30,000 VND and there are always eight of them within two minutes. Eight people in Grab cars is two or three vehicles — fine, but the bikes are faster.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 1.

### Activities
- **06:00–07:30** Market breakfast (below).
- **08:00–09:30** **Temple of Literature** (Quoc Tu Giam, 58 Quoc Tu Giam St; 08:00–17:00; 70,000 VND). Get there at opening. It's Vietnam's 1070 Confucian academy and the stelae of doctoral laureates sit on stone tortoises in the third courtyard. By 10:00 it's full of coach groups; at 08:00 it's students and old men. Easy walking, one hour is enough.
- **10:00–11:30** **Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex** exterior and the **One Pillar Pagoda**, or skip it entirely. Honest assessment: the mausoleum queue is long, the interior is 40 seconds of walking past a body, and the rules (no bags, no phones, no stopping) are strictly enforced. **The Presidential Palace grounds and the stilt house** behind it, by contrast, are quiet and interesting. 40,000 VND. If anyone in the group is lukewarm, send them to the **Museum of Ethnology** in Cau Giay instead (open Tue–Sun, 8:30–17:30, 40,000 VND) — the outdoor collection of reconstructed minority houses is the best thing of its kind in the country and 25 minutes by Grab.
- **12:30–14:00** Lunch.
- **15:00–19:00** **Cooking class with a market walk** (below).
- **20:30** *Bia hơi* — fresh draught beer, brewed and delivered daily, 15,000–20,000 VND a glass. Go to the corner of **Nguyen Huu Huan and Hang Thung** or **Bia Hơi Hải Xồm**, rather than Ta Hien, which is now largely a backpacker street. Order *nem chua nướng* (grilled fermented pork) and *đậu phụ tẩm hành* (fried tofu with spring onion) to go with it.

### Food
- **Market breakfast, 06:15 — Chợ Châu Long (Chau Long Market)**, Chau Long St, Ba Dinh. Opens around 05:30, busiest 06:00–08:00. This is a working neighbourhood market, not a tourist one: live fish in basins, women shelling crab, a dozen breakfast stalls along the interior aisles. 30,000–50,000 VND/person. Eat **bánh cuốn** (steamed rice sheets rolled around minced pork and wood-ear mushroom, showered with fried shallots) and **xôi xéo** (sticky rice with mung bean paste, fried shallot and shredded chicken fat) from whichever stall has the most stools occupied. Bring small notes; nobody here takes cards.
- Alternative, five minutes away: **Bánh Cuốn Bà Xuân**, Dốc Hòe Nhai, Nguyen Trung Truc, Ba Dinh, 06:30 onwards. Further from the Old Quarter, correspondingly fewer foreigners.
- **Lunch, 12:30 — Phở Hà**, 15 Hang Hom St, Hang Gai, Hoan Kiem. ~60,000–80,000 VND. Order **phở gà** (chicken pho, which is the northern default more than beef) and, if you order one thing on this whole trip on my recommendation, **bún thang** — a clear, almost translucent broth with shredded egg crêpe, chicken, pork sausage, dried shrimp and a whisper of shrimp paste. It's the most technically demanding dish in the Hanoi repertoire and almost nobody outside the city cooks it. English menu, which here is a convenience rather than a warning sign.
- **Dinner — cooked by you**, at the class below.

### Local-life element
**A market-to-table cooking class.** This is both your home-cooked meal and your local-life element for Hanoi, and it earns both. The format that works: a morning or afternoon market walk with a Vietnamese cook who explains what's in season and how to buy it, then cooking four or five dishes in a home kitchen and eating them. Expect 900,000–1,400,000 VND per person for a 3–4 hour class. **Hanoi Cooking Centre** on Chau Long St has run this format for years and is the name most often recommended; **Rose Kitchen** and **Hanoi Chef Class** are the other commonly cited operators. *I can't confirm current pricing or that any specific one is still operating in December 2026* — email two of them three weeks out, confirm they can take eight in one session (some cap at six), and ask explicitly for a market walk rather than pre-prepped ingredients.

### Rain plan
The cooking class is indoors and the market has a roof. Swap the Temple of Literature and Ho Chi Minh complex for the **Museum of Ethnology** (all-weather, largely indoors) and add an hour at **Bảo tàng Mỹ thuật Việt Nam** (Fine Arts Museum, 66 Nguyen Thai Hoc, 08:30–17:00, 40,000 VND) — the lacquer and silk collections are unexpectedly strong.

## Day 3 — Monday 14 December · Ninh Binh
*Theme: move south, then find the quiet karst.*

### Movements
- **09:00** Van, Old Quarter → Tam Coc. 100 km via the CT01 expressway, **2h15**. ~3,900,000 VND for a 16-seater day hire, or ~1,800,000 VND for a one-way transfer. Book the day hire — you'll want the vehicle from 14:30 for Van Long.
- Provincial names changed in July 2025 (see §12). Your hotel's address may still say "Hoa Lu District, Ninh Binh Province"; commune-level names like **Ninh Hải** and **Nam Hoa Lư** are now what appears on official paperwork. Give drivers the hotel name and a pinned Google Maps location, not the postal address.
- **11:15** Arrive, check in, lunch.

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **Tam Coc Garden Homestay** — Ninh Hai, Hoa Lu. ~1,100,000–1,600,000 VND ($42–62)/room. Restored brick-and-tile buildings in a garden of fish ponds and banana palms, half the rooms by the pool. Book direct or Agoda. **The reason to pick this one: the family dinner.** Reviews consistently report a set home-cooked dinner at around 100,000 VND per person, cooked by the family, available on request. Ask for it when you book, for both nights.
2. **Tam Coc River View Homestay** — 4.7 km from Ninh Binh centre, 300 m from Tam Coc wharf. Five rooms including quadruples and a family room — an eight-person group can take the whole property, which is the cleanest possible solution to the "eight people" problem. Cheaper, simpler, restaurant on site.

### Activities
- **11:30** Check in and lunch at the homestay.
- **13:00–14:00 Bích Động Pagoda**, 2 km from Tam Coc wharf. Free. Three tiers of temple built into a limestone cliff, reached by a stone stair through a cave. Twenty minutes of climbing, and usually nearly empty because everyone is at the boats. Easy.
- **14:30–17:30 Van Long Nature Reserve**, ~40 minutes' drive north (Gia Vien). Entry plus boat around 80,000–100,000 VND/person — **cash only, and bring small notes**. Flat-bottomed boats, two or three passengers each (so three or four boats for your group), rowed by local women, 1h30 on open wetland under limestone cliffs. **Go late afternoon:** the light is better, the herons and egrets come in to roost, and if you're lucky the Delacour's langur troop comes down the cliffs to feed. This is the trip's answer to Halong Bay and nobody will try to sell you a photograph.
- **18:30** Back at the homestay.

### Food
- **Lunch, 11:45 — at the homestay.** 150,000–200,000 VND/person. Ask for **cơm cháy** (Ninh Binh's crisped rice cake, deep-fried and served with a rich pork or goat topping) and a plate of **dê núi** — mountain goat, either *dê nướng* (grilled) or *dê hấp* (steamed with lemongrass and lime leaf), eaten wrapped in herbs. Goat is the provincial obsession; it's raised on the limestone hills and it tastes of them.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — the homestay family dinner.** ~100,000–150,000 VND/person. Whatever the family is cooking, plus goat if you asked. This is your home-cooked meal for this base, and it's better than any restaurant in Tam Coc village — which has drifted heavily toward Western menus and beer gardens over the last five years.
- **If you want to eat out instead:** **Chookie's** and **Thao Beo** in Tam Coc village are both decent and both full of foreigners. For actual local eating, have the van drop you in **Ninh Binh city** and eat *cơm cháy* and goat at one of the *nhà hàng dê* on Tran Hung Dao — but it's a 20-minute drive each way and the homestay dinner is the better call.

### Local-life element
**Rent bicycles from the homestay** (usually free or ~50,000 VND/day) and ride the lanes between Tam Coc and Ninh Hai in the late morning or after Van Long. In December the paddies are between crops, so the fields are being turned, ducks are being herded down the lanes, and people are burning stubble. You'll be riding through farmyards. Slow down, nod, and don't photograph people's houses without a gesture first.

### Rain plan
Van Long boats run in light rain — take the rain cape the boatwoman offers and go anyway; misted karst is arguably better. If it's heavy: switch Van Long to Day 4 and spend this afternoon at **Hoa Lu ancient capital** (100,000 VND, 07:00–17:00, mostly covered temple buildings) and **Phát Diệm Cathedral** (free, ~30 km south) — an 1891 stone cathedral built in Vietnamese temple form, with wooden pillars the width of a person and no crowds at all in bad weather. It's one of the strangest buildings in the country.

## Day 4 — Tuesday 15 December · Ninh Binh
*Theme: the famous karst, beaten at its own game by starting early.*

### Movements
- Van on standby from 07:00 (~2,000,000 VND for the day) plus bicycles. Tam Coc wharf and Trang An wharf are 6–8 km apart; Mua Cave is 3 km from Tam Coc.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 3.

### Activities

**The honest brief on Trang An and Mua Cave:** both are heavily touristed and both are worth it anyway, provided you handle the timing. Trang An is a UNESCO mixed site and the boat route through nine flooded caves has no real equivalent. Mua Cave has become a photo-queue destination, with a genuinely magnificent view at the top of it.

- **07:00–07:30** Breakfast at the homestay, or take *bánh mì* to go.
- **07:30 Trang An, Route 2.** Ticket **250,000–300,000 VND/person** — sources disagree and 2026 listings lean to 300,000; assume the higher figure and be pleasantly surprised. Ticket includes the boat. Buy at the wharf; Klook sells the same ticket with a QR code that skips the queue, which for eight people is worth doing. **Boats hold 4–5, so you'll be in two.** The route is 2.5–3 hours, rowed, through nine water caves — some low enough that you duck — past temples and the *Kong: Skull Island* set. **Arrive by 07:30.** The wharf opens around 07:00 and the domestic coaches land between 09:00 and 11:00; the difference between 07:30 and 09:30 is the difference between gliding through the caves alone and queueing inside one. There's no tipping culture at Trang An — the rowers are paid by the site — but a small tip is still welcome.
- **11:00** Cycle or drive 8 km to **Hoa Lu ancient capital**. 100,000 VND, 07:00–17:00. Vietnam's capital from 968 to 1010, now two modest temples to the Dinh and Le emperors in a bowl of limestone. Forty minutes. It's history rather than spectacle — go in knowing that.
- **12:30** Lunch.
- **14:00 Mua Cave (Hang Múa)**, 100,000 VND, cash preferred. **486 stone steps** to a Quan Am statue and a dragon ridge with the best aerial view of the Tam Coc valley in the province. Steep, uneven, with a handrail; moderate difficulty and genuinely hard work in the last third. **This is your split point for the mixed hiking appetite** — the climb takes 25–40 minutes and there's a café and lotus pond at the bottom for anyone who'd rather not. Afternoon is correct here: morning light is flat against the valley and the Instagram crowd is thickest 08:00–11:00 and again at sunset.
- **16:00 Thung Nắng or Thung Nham**, optional. Thung Nham Bird Park (~150,000 VND) has a heron roost that fills at dusk. Thung Nang is a smaller, quieter boat route than Tam Coc. Either is a good decompression after the steps.
- **18:30** Back to the homestay.

### Food
- **Market breakfast option, 06:00 — Chợ Rồng (Rong Market)**, central Ninh Binh city, ~05:00–11:00. Twenty minutes by van. *Bún* stalls, *bánh đa* and the province's breakfast trade in full swing. 30,000 VND/person. Only do this if you're willing to sacrifice the 07:30 Trang An start — you can't have both, and Trang An at 07:30 is the better prize. **Put Chợ Rồng on Day 5 morning instead**, when you don't need an early start.
- **Lunch, 12:45 — Nhà hàng Đức Dê** or any of the goat restaurants signposted *dê núi* along the Tam Coc road. ~200,000 VND/person. Order **dê tái chanh** (goat sliced thin and "cooked" in lime juice with sesame and herbs), **dê nướng lá lốt** (grilled in wild betel leaf) and a plate of **cơm cháy**. *I can't verify specific opening hours for individual goat restaurants — there are a dozen and they're all open at lunch.* The homestay will phone ahead for eight.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — homestay family dinner again.** Ask them to cook something different: **ốc nấu chuối đậu** (snails stewed with green banana and tofu) if they'll do it, which is a northern home dish you will almost never see on a menu.

### Local-life element
Ask your homestay hosts whether anyone in the family or village keeps goats, and whether you can walk up with them in the morning. Ninh Binh's goat herds go out onto the limestone at dawn and it's the single most characteristic thing that happens here. Offer to pay something — 200,000–300,000 VND for the group is generous and not insulting. This is a request to make in person, the evening before, not something to book.

### Rain plan
Trang An operates year-round and is largely sheltered — go in rain. **Skip Mua Cave if it's wet**; the steps are polished limestone and genuinely dangerous slick. Substitute: **Bái Đính Pagoda** (free entry, electric cart 60,000 VND) — an enormous 2010s temple complex, architecturally hollow but vast and covered, and it's where Vietnamese pilgrims actually go. Or bring **Cúc Phương forward from Day 5** and push Trang An/Mua Cave back a day, since the primate centre is roofed.

## Day 5 — Wednesday 16 December · Cuc Phuong, then the night train
*Theme: old-growth forest, then go to bed on a train.*

### Movements
- **07:30** Van from Tam Coc → Cuc Phuong. ~50 km, 1h10. Full-day hire with a late finish: ~3,100,000 VND. **Tell the operator explicitly that you need the vehicle until 21:45** — a standard day hire ends at 18:00 and overtime runs 350,000–500,000 VND/hour.
- **17:00** Cuc Phuong → back to the homestay for showers and bag repacking (most will hold rooms until early evening if you ask; some charge a half-day late checkout).
- **21:15** Homestay → **Ninh Binh Railway Station**, 1 Hoang Dieu St (some listings say 41 Hoang Hoa Tham — the station has been re-addressed; both refer to the same place, central Ninh Binh city). 20 minutes.
- **22:02 Train SE19 → Dong Hoi, arriving 06:25.** Two 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins. *Times shift slightly between sources — 21:51, 22:00 and 22:02 all appear. Reconfirm on dsvn.vn a week out and again the day before.*

### Accommodation
**On the train.** Two private 4-berth cabins. Bedding, air-con and a power socket per berth. It's a 6-berth hard sleeper you want to avoid, not the 4-berth, which is genuinely comfortable. **Pack an overnight bag** with what you need for the morning so you're not opening main luggage in a cabin at 06:00.

### Activities
- **08:40 Arrive Cuc Phuong.** Entry **50,000–60,000 VND/person**, plus **60,000 VND** separately for the Endangered Primate Rescue Centre. **Cash only at the gate.** Park hours are roughly 07:00–18:00 (some sources say 08:00–18:00 or later; arrive by 09:00 and it's moot).
- **09:00–10:30 Endangered Primate Rescue Centre**, a ten-minute walk from park HQ. Fifteen species, around 180 animals, all either rescued from traffickers or born here — including the world's first captive-born Cat Ba langur and Delacour's langur. Your ticket also covers the **Turtle Conservation Centre** next door. Guided tours run on a schedule; ask at the gate. No feeding, no contact, and the primates are behind mesh — this is a conservation facility, not a zoo, and it is the best-run one in Vietnam. **Book nothing in advance; arrange at the gate.**
- **10:45–13:00 The Ancient Tree trail.** The 20 km road from the gate to the park centre runs under closed canopy almost the whole way — have the van drive it slowly. From the park centre, the trail to the **1,000-year-old cho tree** is about 3 km each way through primary forest, 2.5–3 hours round trip, undulating with some root-and-rock scrambling. **Moderate.** For the half-day contingent: the **Botanical Garden** and the **Prehistoric Cave** (Động Người Xưa) are both within 30 minutes of the centre on easy paths, so you can split the group here without anyone being stranded.
- **13:00** Picnic lunch.
- **14:00–16:30** Second trail or the Cave of Prehistoric Man, then back.
- **17:00** Depart. **19:00** Showers, repack, dinner. **21:15** Station.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:30 — Chợ Rồng, Ninh Binh city**, if you want the market breakfast here (and the van is going your way). Otherwise homestay breakfast at 07:00.
- **Lunch, 13:00 — bring it.** The restaurant at Cuc Phuong park centre is functional and not much more. Ask your homestay the night before to pack a picnic for eight — *bánh mì*, boiled eggs, *xôi*, fruit, a thermos. Expect 80,000–120,000 VND/person and it will be better than what's available in the park. There is a small café at the park centre for coffee.
- **Dinner, 19:30 — one last goat dinner in Tam Coc**, or the homestay. ~200,000 VND/person. **Eat properly before the train.** There's a trolley service on SE19 with instant noodles and beer, and that's all.
- **Buy on the way to the station:** water, fruit, *bánh mì*, beer. There's a small market and several shops on the approach to Ninh Binh station.

### Local-life element
Cuc Phuong's rescue centre is staffed largely by Vietnamese conservation biologists who will talk at length if you ask real questions — about the pangolin trade, about why released langurs mostly don't survive, about the Rewilding Action Plan running 2026–2035. Ask. It's a better conversation than any guided commentary you'll pay for.

### Rain plan
The forest is fine in rain — it's a rainforest — but the Ancient Tree trail becomes a mudslide and **leeches are real** in wet conditions. Wet-weather version: primate centre, turtle centre, the park museum, the Prehistoric Cave, and the drive under the canopy. That's still a full and worthwhile day, entirely under cover or on short paths. December is generally the dry end of the year here, so this is unlikely.

## Day 6 — Thursday 17 December · Phong Nha
*Theme: arrive off the train, get underground, keep it easy.*

### Movements
- **06:25** Arrive **Dong Hoi station**, Thuan Ly St (now Quang Tri province, formerly Quang Binh).
- **06:45** Two 7-seat transfers to Phong Nha, 45 km, ~1 hour. **650,000 VND per vehicle** through Oxalis; arrange it as part of your tour booking. ~1,300,000 VND total.
- **07:45** Arrive Son Trach (Phong Nha town).
- Private van for the day from 10:00: ~2,000,000 VND. Paradise Cave is 30 km out; the Phong Nha Cave wharf is in town.

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **Phong Nha Farmstay** — 8 km out of town, surrounded by paddy. Rooms from ~$37 on Agoda, rising to ~$96 in high season; family rooms available. French-colonial style, pool, free bikes, and the original foreign-run guesthouse in the region (Ben and Bich, open since 2010). Genuinely good food, if pricier than town. The distance from town is a feature, not a bug — it's quiet and the paddy views are the point. Book direct or Agoda.
2. **Thao Nguyen Homestay** or **Phong Nha Riverside Homestay** — in town, family-run, ~$20–35/room, several rooms each so eight fits. Simpler, closer to the morning market and cheap dinners, and more clearly a family's house. **Note: several Phong Nha homestays are cash-only.** This is your family-run stay for this base; ask about dinner when you book.
3. **Chay Lap Farmstay** — if you book Oxalis, you get a discounted rate of 1,100,000 VND/room/night (~$42). Pool, spa, kayaking. Comfortable rather than local.

### Activities
- **07:45–10:00** Breakfast, check in, wash, half an hour lying down. The train arrives early enough that this is dead time you can use properly.
- **10:00–12:00 Phong Nha Cave by boat.** Entry **150,000 VND/person**; the boat is **750,000 VND per boat, max 12 people** — so one boat for your eight, at ~94,000 VND each. Boats are managed by the Phong Nha Tourist Centre at a fixed price with no touting, and **no advance booking is possible or needed** — a coordinator assigns you on arrival. You motor 1.5 km up the underground Son River into the cave, then walk out onto a sandbar and explore a lit chamber on foot. Physically trivial. Add **Tien Son Cave** (80,000 VND) above it if you want; it's a dry cave up a flight of steps and it's fine rather than essential.
- **12:15** Lunch in town.
- **14:00–16:30 Paradise Cave (Thiên Đường).** **270,000 VND/person** (2026 rate; older sources say 250,000). Ticket includes the electric buggy from the car park to the base of the stairs — then it's 500 steps up and a descent into the cave. Allow 2.5 hours. The first kilometre is boardwalked in a chamber so large that the lighting doesn't reach the ceiling; the full cave runs 31 km. **Go after 14:00.** The coach groups from Dong Hoi and Hue arrive between 09:00 and 12:00 and are gone by early afternoon; the cave is ten degrees cooler than outside and effectively yours after 15:00. Moderate: the steps up are the only real effort, and there's a buggy-plus-shorter-route option for anyone who'd rather not.
- **17:00 Nước Moọc (Mooc Spring) Eco Trail**, optional and only if you have energy. Entry 80,000 VND; the 180,000 VND package includes kayaking and swimming, but in December the spring is cold enough that nobody will want to. The boardwalk trail through the stream valley is the good part. Low-season package pricing applies October–December.
- **18:00** Back for dinner.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 08:00 — Chợ Troc / Son Trach morning market**, central Phong Nha, from ~05:30. 30,000–40,000 VND/person. Noodle stalls and *bánh mì* carts, served to construction crews and cave guides. Order **bánh mì** with pâté and pickled carrot and a bowl of **bún** with whatever the broth is that morning. You've just come off a train; this is the correct breakfast.
- **Lunch, 12:15 — Phong Nha Bamboo Restaurant** or **Mountain View Restaurant**, both on the main street in Son Trach. 80,000–150,000 VND/person. Order **cá kho tộ** (fish braised in a clay pot with caramel and pepper) and **lươn xào nghệ** (eel stir-fried with turmeric). Quang Binh cooking is plainer than Hue's and leans on river fish and eel. *Restaurant turnover in Phong Nha is high — several of the names that appear in guides from three years ago have closed. Ask your homestay what's good this month; they will know and they will not send you wrong.*
- **Dinner, 19:00 — home-cooked at your homestay.** 150,000–250,000 VND/person. Ask for it when you check in, ideally when you book. If you're at Phong Nha Farmstay instead, their kitchen is good but it's a restaurant — in that case walk or bike into town.

### Local-life element
**The Phong Nha Caves Exploration Museum** in town: free admission, 08:00–17:30 daily. Not a local-life element on its own, but it's staffed by people from the caving community and it's where you'll understand who found these caves and how — Hồ Khanh, a local logger, found the entrance to Son Doong in 1991 while sheltering from a storm and couldn't relocate it for years. The Son Doong 5D VR experience is 500,000 VND and proceeds go to the Oxalis Foundation. Skip the VR, read the walls, talk to whoever's on the desk.

### Rain plan
Both caves are caves. Rain changes nothing about Phong Nha Cave or Paradise Cave except the drive. **What rain does change:** the road to Paradise Cave can flood after heavy rain, and the park very occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm. If the road's out, do Phong Nha Cave plus Tien Son plus the museum, and push Paradise Cave to Day 8 morning. Drop Nuoc Mooc without regret.

## Day 7 — Friday 18 December · Phong Nha
*Theme: the actual reason you came.*

### Movements
- **07:00–07:30** Oxalis guide and driver collect you from your hotel in Phong Nha and drive **70 km to the Oxalis office in Tan Hoa village**. Return to Phong Nha **18:30–19:30**. All transport included in the tour.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 6.

### The tour — and why this one

**Oxalis Tu Lan Experience (1 day) — 2,000,000 VND / ~$75 per person.** Includes VAT, entrance and forest fees, all food, all safety gear, a guide, two safety assistants, a chef and a kitchen assistant, and basic insurance to 10,000,000 VND.

- **Season:** operates mid-November to mid-September. December is in season.
- **Group size:** capped at **12**, which means your eight can effectively own a departure.
- **Difficulty:** rated Easy. 9 km trek through valleys and river crossings, 800 m of cave, 120 m elevation gain, a **15 m ladder descent on harness and rope**, and **150 m swimming through an underground river in a life vest**.
- **Age limit 16–70.** Fitness bar: able to climb five flights of stairs continuously, or run 3 km in 30 minutes, without distress.
- **Book:** online at oxalisadventure.com, select date, enter eight participants. Online bookings close 19:00 the day before; after that, hotline/WhatsApp **+84 919 900 357**. Full payment confirms the booking. Free cancellation to 7 days out, nothing after. **Book this the day you commit to the trip** — a 12-person cap in December fills.
- **Declare honestly** on the booking form: health conditions, medications, trekking experience, exercise frequency. Oxalis reserves the right to cancel without refund for false information, and given they're operating harness descents in a remote valley with a 30-minute-to-2-hour rescue window, they mean it.

**The December caveat you need to decide on.** The centrepiece of this tour is swimming 150 m through a dark underground river. Cave water in Quang Binh sits around 20–22°C year-round, and in December the air outside is 17–21°C with no sun to warm you afterward. Oxalis packs a waterproof jacket into their November-to-March kit list for exactly this reason. Most people find it exhilarating. Some find it grim. **If your group's weakest member would hate a cold swim, book the dry alternative instead:**

- **Hang Tien Cave Day Trek — 2,200,000 VND (~$85).** Same operator, same Easy rating, same one-day format. Dry cave, enormous entrance, huge calcite formations, and a natural swimming pool you can decline to enter. This is the better December choice for a mixed group and I'd lean toward it.
- **Phong Nha: Nuoc Nut Cave Discovery — 2,150,000 VND.** Also one day, also Easy, inside the national park proper rather than the Tu Lan system.

You can also **split the group** across two tours on the same date if opinions divide — call the hotline rather than trying to do it through the website.

### Itinerary of the day (Tu Lan Experience)
- **07:00–08:30** Pickup, drive to Tan Hoa, briefing, release form, gear issue.
- **08:45** Walk out through corn and peanut fields to the Rao Nan River; river crossing; short climb to **Rat Cave** — 300 m, heart-shaped entrance, big dome.
- **10:30** Snack, then over "Baby Hill" (60 m) and across Hung Ton Valley.
- **11:30 Hung Ton Cave** dry passage; harness up and descend the 15 m ladder; marine fossils in the passage walls.
- **12:30** Life vests on, swim the river passage out into **To Mo Valley**. Lunch at the emerald pool, swim, rest.
- **14:30** Trek back across the valleys.
- **17:00** Oxalis office: showers, cold drinks. **18:30–19:30** Back in Phong Nha.

### What to bring
Quick-dry long-sleeved shirt, quick-dry long trousers, waterproof jacket (they specify this for November–March), closed trekking or approach shoes with grip, **calf-length thick socks** (leeches), a change of clothes for afterward, insect repellent, towel, swimwear, passport. Oxalis lends trekking shoes in sizes 36–44 for a 50,000 VND cleaning fee — **bring your own if you can**, and break them in first. They supply helmet, headlamp, gloves, harness, 25 L dry pack, a shared dry box for electronics, water and purifier, first-aid, and a satellite phone.

### Food
- **All meals on the tour are included** — snack, a cooked picnic lunch at the river, tea and coffee. Oxalis cooks properly; reviews consistently single out the food. Note dietary requirements in the booking form.
- **Breakfast, 06:15** — before pickup. Your homestay will do it early if you ask the night before; otherwise *bánh mì* from the market at 06:00.
- **Dinner, 20:00 — Phong Nha town.** 100,000–200,000 VND/person. You will be tired and hungry. **Bomb Crater Bar** and **Omar's Namaste** are the standby foreigner options; for local food, eat at whatever *quán* on the main street your homestay recommends and order **gà nướng** (grilled chicken) and rice.

### Local-life element
This is the local-life element, and it's the most substantial one in the itinerary. Oxalis employs porters, cooks and safety staff from **Tan Hoa**, a village that was 85.7% below the poverty line in 2010 and is at around 2% now, largely through this. The village also built floating houses to survive the annual floods — Tan Hoa is Vietnam's first "weather-adaptive tourism village." Your porters live there. Ask them about the floods. They will tell you, and it is not a rehearsed story.

### Rain plan
Oxalis adjusts or reroutes the tour for weather and will tell you the morning of; the jungle trail gets "muddy and slippery" in their own words. They don't usually cancel for rain. **If they do cancel**, or if you decide against the trek on the day: Paradise Cave (if you haven't) plus the **Dark Cave** — 250,000 VND in low season (16 September–end February), which includes the zipline, mud bath and kayak. The mud bath is cold in December and the Dark Cave is a managed adventure-park experience rather than caving, but it's covered and it's fun. Book Dark Cave through an office in Phong Nha town the day before; **avoid booking it through Hanoi or Hoi An agencies, who add 20–30%.**

## Day 8 — Saturday 19 December · Hue
*Theme: two wheels out, then the best eating city in Vietnam.*

### Movements
- **07:00–10:00 Motorbikes, Bong Lai valley loop.** 8 scooters, ~150,000–200,000 VND each (~1,400,000 VND total), arranged through your homestay or any shop on the Son Trach main street — **arrange this on arrival on Day 6**, not in advance. Automatic Honda Vision or Yamaha Janus is right for this; you don't need a manual. The loop is 25–30 km of sealed and hard-packed farm road with almost no traffic, gentle gradients, one petrol station in town — **fill up before you leave, there is nothing out there.** Total riding time 1h15 across a 3-hour loop. Luggage stays at the hotel.
- **10:45 Van, Phong Nha → Hue.** 210 km, **4 hours direct**. With the Vinh Moc stop, ~4h45 of elapsed time. ~4,500,000 VND for a 16-seater. Book with **Culture Pham Travel** or **Phong Nha Locals** 1–2 weeks out; specify a 16-seater and a Vinh Moc stop.
- **15:30** Arrive Hue.

### Accommodation (1 night)
1. **Hotel Alba Spa** or **Jade Scene Hotel**, both in the Phu Hoi/Vinh Ninh grid south of the river — ~1,000,000–1,500,000 VND ($40–58)/double, four rooms for eight. This side of the Perfume River is where the food is and it's a 10-minute walk to Dong Ba Market across Truong Tien Bridge.
2. **A garden house (nhà rường) guesthouse in Kim Long**, west of the Citadel — quieter, traditional timber architecture, and closer to the Thien Mu Pagoda. Harder to fit eight into one property; check room counts carefully.

**Book Hue for a Saturday night with some urgency** — it's a weekend in a city with a compact hotel district.

### Activities
- **07:00 Bong Lai valley by scooter.** Free to ride; the stops are what cost. The valley is a farming basin 10 km out of Son Trach with a handful of family-run places strung along it: the **Duck Stop** (ducks, buffalo, ~150,000 VND), **Wild Boar Eco Farm**, and **the Pub With Cold Beer** (a farmhouse at the end of a dirt track that grills chicken to order). **Flag: this loop has become a circuit.** Ten years ago it was three farms; it's now a known route with signage, and mid-morning it has minibuses. **Ride it at 07:00 and you'll have it to yourselves** — the operators are still the same families, and at that hour you're the only ones there. Easy riding; the only hazards are buffalo and dogs.
- **12:30–13:30 Vinh Moc Tunnels**, en route, 07:00–17:00, **50,000 VND/person**. An entire village of 300 people lived underground here for six years, in three levels of tunnel dug into the coastal clay, with a maternity room where 17 children were born. You walk through them. It's low, dark and narrow — genuinely claustrophobic for some — and it's the most affecting hour on this route. **Hien Luong Bridge** and the Ben Hai River at the 17th parallel are ten minutes further on and free; a five-minute stop, half the bridge painted each colour.
- **16:00–17:30** Walk Hue: the riverfront on Le Loi, **Truong Tien Bridge**, and **An Dinh Palace** (50,000 VND, closes 17:00 — tight, skip if you're late). Save the Citadel for tomorrow's opening hour.
- **19:00** Eat, at length.

### Food

This is the day to be greedy. Hue has the most distinctive regional cooking in Vietnam — spicier than Hanoi, not sweet like Saigon, built on shrimp paste (*mắm ruốc*) and chilli, and split between a courtly repertoire of tiny composed dishes and a street repertoire of assertive noodle soups.

- **Breakfast, 06:30 — Phong Nha market** or homestay, before the ride. Light; you're on a bike.
- **Lunch, 11:30 — on the road.** Have the driver stop at a *quán cơm bình dân* (a point-and-choose rice shop) in Dong Ha or Gio Linh. 50,000–70,000 VND/person, six or seven dishes on a counter, you point. This is how Vietnam eats lunch on a working day and it's the best-value meal of the trip.
- **Dinner part one, 18:00 — Bún Bò Huế.** The dish. Beef and pork bones, lemongrass, shrimp paste, chilli oil, thick round rice noodles, brisket, pork knuckle and a cube of congealed blood if you let them. **Bún Bò Huế Bà Tuyết**, 47 Nguyen Cong Tru St, Phu Hoi — ~50,000 VND. Or **Quán Cẩm**, 38 Tran Cao Van, roughly 06:00–19:00 — go earlier rather than later; the good *bún bò* shops sell out and shut. *Several well-known bún bò places open only for a few hours in the morning; if you want the best version, eat it for breakfast tomorrow instead and do rice cakes tonight.*
- **Dinner part two, 19:30 — Hue rice cakes.** **Quán Bà Đỏ**, 7 Nguyen Binh Khiem, just off Chi Lang — or **Hàng Me Mé**, 60 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. 120,000–180,000 VND/person for a full spread. Order all of them: **bánh bèo** (thumbnail-sized steamed rice cakes in their own little dishes, topped with dried shrimp, scallion oil and pork crackling), **bánh bột lọc** (translucent tapioca dumplings with shrimp and pork, wrapped in banana leaf), **bánh nậm** (flat steamed cakes in leaf), **bánh ram ít** (a sticky dumpling on a fried rice cracker). Each is two bites. The point is the set, the range of textures, and the fact that this is court cooking that escaped into the street.
- **Dinner part three, 20:30 — Bánh khoái.** **Lạc Thiện**, 6 Dinh Tien Hoang, ~08:00–22:30, or **Chị Hạnh**, 11–15 Pho Duc Chinh, 09:00–21:00. ~100,000 VND/person. A thick, small, turmeric-yellow crisp pancake of shrimp, pork and bean sprout, folded and eaten by hand with herbs and a peanut-and-liver dipping sauce that is the actual star. Order **nem lụi** alongside — lemongrass-skewered grilled pork you roll yourself.
- **Reserve for eight at Bà Đỏ and Lạc Thiện.** Both are small. The hotel will phone.

### Local-life element
**Chợ Đông Ba (Dong Ba Market)**, on the north bank by Truong Tien Bridge, roughly 06:00–19:00. It's Hue's central market and the food section on the ground floor is where the city eats. Go in the evening for *bánh ép* — rice-and-shrimp batter pressed flat on a hot iron, rolled with herbs and green mango — which is a snack Hue people eat standing up and almost no visitor tries. 10,000 VND a piece. Anthony Bourdain found a *bún bò* stall in here; you probably won't find the same one, and it doesn't matter.

### Rain plan
**This is the day most likely to be rained on.** Hue's rainy season runs into December; the first half of the month can see 200–300 mm.
- **If it's raining in Phong Nha at 06:30:** cancel the scooters. Sealed farm roads in the wet with eight riders and no upside is a bad trade. Swap to an 08:00 departure and add **Khe Sanh Combat Base** (50,000 VND) to the drive, or just get to Hue three hours early and eat more.
- **If it's raining in Hue:** everything above is indoors or a two-minute dash. Add the **Hue Royal Antiquities Museum** (50,000 VND) and the covered upper floors of Dong Ba.

## Day 9 — Sunday 20 December · Hai Van Pass → Da Nang
*Theme: the ride, then be sensibly close to the airport.*

### Movements
- **09:15** Collect bikes in Hue. 8 one-way rentals to Da Nang with luggage transfer. **Style Motorbikes** (one-way Hue↔Da Nang/Hoi An, free luggage transfer), **Hidden Land Travel / Hue Scooter Rental** (WhatsApp +84 348 421 282, free luggage forwarding), or **Motorvina**. Expect **$25–40 per bike** including the luggage run; ~6,000,000 VND for the group with a chase van. **Reserve three weeks out** — eight bikes in one size class is a real ask.
- **Bikes:** for this route take **semi-automatic or manual 110–150 cc** (Honda Winner X, Yamaha Exciter, or Honda Blade) rather than automatic scooters. The Hai Van descent is long and steep and you want engine braking. All your riders are licensed and experienced, so this is the right call; a CVT scooter on that descent cooks its brakes.
- **Route:** Hue → coastal road south through Thuan An and past An Bang → **rejoin QL1A at Lang Co** → **Hai Van Pass** → Da Nang. **~85 km, 3h30–4h riding with stops.** The pass itself is 21 km of switchbacks, topping out around 500 m.
- **Motorbikes are banned from the Hai Van tunnel.** That's fine — the tunnel is what makes the pass quiet, because all the trucks and coaches are underneath you.
- **Fuel:** fill in Hue before leaving, and again at **Lang Co** at the foot of the climb. There's nothing on the pass.
- **Luggage:** goes by the rental company's van to your Da Nang hotel. Carry a day bag only. Confirm the drop-off address in writing.
- **15:00** Arrive Da Nang. Bikes returned to the company's Da Nang office or collected from the hotel.

### Accommodation (1 night)
Stay in **Hai Chau**, central Da Nang, not at the beach. DAD is 3 km from Hai Chau — a **10–15 minute, ~150,000–200,000 VND** run at 03:30. From An Thuong or My Khe it's 6–7 km; from Hoi An it's 30 km and 45 minutes.
1. **Sala Danang Beach Hotel** or **Grand Cititel Danang**, Hai Chau — ~1,000,000–1,600,000 VND ($40–62)/double. Central, four rooms for eight, walkable to Con Market and Han Market.
2. Anything on **Bach Dang** or in the grid between **Nguyen Van Linh** and **Tran Phu**.

**Book the hotel car for 03:30 the night before and confirm it at reception before you go to bed.** Grab works at that hour in Da Nang but do not stake a 05:00 flight on it.

### Activities
- **06:30–08:30 Hue Imperial Citadel (Đại Nội).** **200,000 VND/person** (2026; some sources still list 150,000). Opens **06:30**. Buy at Ngo Mon Gate or online at hueworldheritage.org.vn. **Be there at opening.** The Citadel is genuinely worth the crowds — Thai Hoa Palace, the Nine Dynastic Urns, the To Mieu ancestral temple, and the recently restored Kien Trung Palace — and at 06:30 on a Sunday you will have the courtyards to yourself in flat morning light. By 09:30 it's coaches. Two hours is the right amount; flat walking. **Skip the tombs.** You don't have time, the 4-site combo at 530,000 VND is wasted on you, and the Citadel alone at 200,000 VND is the right purchase.
- **09:15** Collect bikes.
- **10:00** Depart. **Thuan An** and the **An Bang "City of Ghosts"** — 3 km of extravagant, brightly painted family tombs along the coast road, built by relatives who emigrated. Free, and a 20-minute stop that will stay with you.
- **11:30 Lang Co / Lap An Lagoon.** Oyster rafts, mountains behind, fuel stop.
- **12:30–14:00 Hai Van Pass.** Stop at the top — French bunkers, a Nguyen-dynasty gate, and the view back over Lang Co on one side and Da Nang Bay on the other. There's a café. **Go slowly on the descent**; the switchbacks are tight, the surface is patched, and the south side gets wet from sea mist even on a dry day.
- **15:00** Da Nang. Check in, shower.
- **16:00–18:00 Chợ Cồn (Con Market)** street-food section, which comes alive from around 15:00. This is your local-life element and your dinner staging ground.
- **18:30** Dinner, then an early night. You are getting up at 03:00.
- **Optional Hoi An evening:** 30 minutes and ~400,000 VND by car each way. You'd get the lanterns, the old town at night, and **cao lầu** at its source. You'd also get back at 22:30 before a 03:00 alarm. Your call; I wouldn't.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:00 — Hue, before the Citadel.** **Cơm Hến Bà Đen** (06:00–11:30) or **Cơm hến Hương** near the Citadel. ~30,000 VND. **Cơm hến** is cold rice with baby clams from the Perfume River, clam broth, peanuts, pork crackling, banana flower, herbs and a lot of chilli, mixed hard before eating. It is Hue's breakfast, it is startling at 06:00, and it is the dish you will most regret not eating. *Bún hến* is the same thing over noodles if rice at dawn defeats you.
- **Lunch, 12:45 — on the pass or at Lang Co.** Seafood at a beach *quán* at Lang Co: grilled squid, clams, oysters. 150,000–250,000 VND/person. Or a *bánh mì* at the top of Hai Van. Don't eat heavily before the descent.
- **Dinner, 18:30 — Mì Quảng.** **Mì Quảng Bà Mua** — several branches; 95A Nguyen Tri Phuong (Hai Chau) and 19 Tran Binh Trong are the ones most often named. ~06:30–22:00 depending on branch. **35,000–50,000 VND**. Thick turmeric rice noodles with barely any broth — a few spoonfuls of intense stock — topped with shrimp and pork or chicken, peanuts, herbs, and a rice cracker you snap over the top. This is Quang Nam's defining dish and Da Nang is where to eat it. **Mì Quảng is a daytime dish** and the best shops sell out by mid-afternoon, so if you want the peak version, eat it at 16:00 when you arrive rather than at dinner.
- **Dinner, 19:30 — Bánh Xèo Bà Dưỡng**, in an alley off Hoang Dieu, Hai Chau. ~100,000–150,000 VND/person. Michelin-listed, 30-plus years old, hidden deep in a lane. Central-Vietnamese **bánh xèo** is small — taco-sized — so order six to eight each, plus **nem lụi**. The thick peanut-and-liver sauce is the reason to come. **Goes before 20:00** — they sell out.
- **Or, if you'd rather eat one thing well: Bún Chả Cá 109**, 109 Nguyen Chi Thanh, Hai Chau (breakfast and lunch mainly) or **Bà Lữ**, 319 Hung Vuong. Vermicelli in a clear, faintly sweet-sour broth built on fish bones, pumpkin, pineapple and tomato, with house-made fish cakes both steamed and fried. It's Da Nang's actual everyday breakfast and it's better than it sounds.
- **Reservations:** not needed at any of these, but arrive as a group of eight before the rush and expect to split tables.

### Local-life element
**Chợ Cồn**, Hung Vuong St, Hai Chau. The street-food section opens from around 15:00 and it is where Da Nang goes after work — *bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo*, *ốc* (sea snails), sugarcane juice, *chè*. Sit at a stall, point, and pay 20,000–40,000 VND a plate. **The alternative, if you had a morning here:** the fish market at **Tho Quang / Nai Hien Dong** when the boats land around 05:00. You don't have a morning, so Con Market it is.

### Rain plan
**Take this seriously.** Da Nang averages heavy rainfall in December, and a wet Hai Van Pass with eight riders on unfamiliar bikes is the single highest-risk item in this itinerary. Wet switchbacks, sea mist at the summit, poor visibility, and no shoulder.

**If there is meaningful rain forecast, do not ride.** Instead:
- **The Heritage Train, Hue → Da Nang.** Trains **HĐ1** (morning) and **HĐ3** (afternoon), 2.5–3 hours, purpose-built for sightseeing with large observation windows, a Hue food carriage, and a 10–15 minute photo stop at **Lang Co station**. **180,000–400,000 VND/person** depending on class; a weekend surcharge applies, so budget the top of that on a Sunday. *Departure times vary a lot between sources — 07:05, 07:45 and 07:50 all appear for HĐ1. Book on dsvn.vn or through Baolau/Vexere and reconfirm the day before.* Sit on the **left** going south for the sea. Honestly: this is one of the finest short rail journeys in Asia and it's arguably the better experience in any weather. The regular SE trains cover the same track for 77,000–200,000 VND if the heritage service is full.
- **Or a private van through the Hai Van tunnel** — 1h45, ~1,700,000 VND, dull but fast.
- **Or the van over the pass** — you still get the view from the top, with a roof.

**Decide by 07:00 on the day, not the night before**, and tell the rental company you may cancel. Most will let you if you ask honestly when reserving; get that in writing.

## 4. Regional food primer

**Hanoi and the north.** Restrained, savoury, sour, herb-forward. Salt and fish sauce rather than sugar; vinegar and lime rather than chilli. Dill appears in northern cooking and nowhere else in Vietnam. Freshwater fish, pork, and an enormous vocabulary of noodle dishes.
- **Must eat:** *bún chả* · *chả cá* · *bún thang* · *bánh cuốn* · *phở gà*
- **Best on your route:** all of it in Hanoi. Nothing on this itinerary replaces Hanoi for northern food, so eat properly on Days 1–2.

**Ninh Binh.** A small, specific repertoire built on the limestone hills.
- **Must eat:** *cơm cháy* (crisped rice cake) · *dê núi* (mountain goat, grilled, steamed, or lime-cured) · *ốc nấu chuối đậu* if you can find someone's grandmother to cook it
- **Best on your route:** Tam Coc and Ninh Binh city. Nowhere else.

**Quang Binh / Phong Nha.** The plainest cooking on the route, and not a failing — river fish, eel, chicken, and clay-pot braises, in a province that was poor until very recently.
- **Must eat:** *cá kho tộ* (clay-pot braised fish) · *lươn xào nghệ* (eel with turmeric) · *bánh bột lọc* in the Quang Binh style, thicker than Hue's
- **Best on your route:** Son Trach town, and your homestay's kitchen.

**Hue.** The most distinctive regional cuisine in Vietnam, and the reason to be annoyed you only have one night. Two traditions running in parallel: the imperial court's habit of many small, precisely composed dishes, and a street tradition of aggressive, chilli-and-shrimp-paste noodle soups. Spicier than Hanoi, not sweet like Saigon. *Mắm ruốc* (fermented shrimp paste) is the base note under almost everything.
- **Must eat:** *bún bò Huế* · *cơm hến* · *bánh bèo / bánh bột lọc / bánh nậm / bánh ram ít* (as a set) · *bánh khoái* with *nem lụi* · *bánh ép* from Dong Ba
- **Best on your route:** Hue, obviously. Eat *bún bò* for breakfast rather than dinner — the best shops open early and close when the pot's empty.

**Da Nang and Quang Nam.** Bolder than Hue, less courtly, built on the coast and on turmeric. Broth is used sparingly — several signature dishes are almost dry.
- **Must eat:** *mì Quảng* · *bánh xèo* with *nem lụi* and peanut-liver sauce · *bún chả cá* · *bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo* (pork belly with two rims of fat, rolled at the table with a bale of herbs)
- **Best on your route:** Da Nang. *Cao lầu* — thick chewy noodles with char siu, greens and crackling, made with water from a specific Hoi An well — genuinely only exists in Hoi An, and this route skips it. That's the food cost of the route.

**Ordering mechanics.** Most street places serve one thing; the menu is the sign. At a *quán*, sit and someone will come. Pay after eating. *Rau* (herbs) and *tương ớt* (chilli sauce) on the table are free and unlimited. Ice in cylinders with a hole through the middle is factory-made and safe; crushed ice is less reliable.

## 7. Cash and payments

**Carry 3,000,000–4,000,000 VND per person in cash** for a trip of this length, and top up from ATMs rather than arriving with all of it. Where cards work, use them; where they don't, it's absolute.

**Cards fail at:** every street food stall and market · Cuc Phuong's gate (explicitly cash-only) · Van Long · Mua Cave · most Phong Nha homestays · goat restaurants in Tam Coc · Bong Lai valley farms · the Phong Nha Cave boat · petrol stations for small fills · Grab bikes if you haven't linked a card.

**Cards work at:** hotels, Trang An, the Hue Citadel, Oxalis (pay in advance by transfer), mid-range restaurants in Hanoi, Hue and Da Nang, supermarkets.

**ATMs.** Use bank-branded machines inside bank premises — **Vietcombank, BIDV, Techcombank, VietinBank, TPBank**. Avoid standalone machines in tourist lanes. Most cap withdrawals at 3,000,000–5,000,000 VND per transaction and charge 22,000–55,000 VND per withdrawal; **TPBank and some Techcombank machines dispense up to 10,000,000 VND** with lower fees, which matters when you're withdrawing eight people's worth. **Take out large amounts less often.** ATMs in Tam Coc village, Son Trach and Cuc Phuong are sparse to nonexistent — **withdraw in Hanoi, Ninh Binh city, Hue and Da Nang.**

**Notes.** 500,000 VND notes are hard to break at a market stall. Ask for a mix. The 20,000 (blue) and 500,000 (blue-green) notes look similar in poor light and this is a real and common error.

**Tipping.** Not expected, increasingly present.
- Street food, markets, *quán*: nothing.
- Restaurants: round up, or 5–10% if there's no service charge. Michelin-listed places in Da Nang often add 5–10% already.
- Homestay family dinner: nothing, but a gift for the household or a contribution if they've gone out of their way.
- Boat rowers at Van Long and Tam Coc: 50,000–100,000 VND per boat is normal and welcome. Trang An's rowers are salaried and don't solicit; tip anyway if you enjoyed it.
- **Oxalis guides, porters and cooks: this is the one place to tip properly.** A pooled 500,000–1,000,000 VND from the group at the end of the day, handed to the guide for the team, is appropriate for an eight-person party.
- Drivers: 100,000–200,000 VND for a good full day.
- Hotel staff: 20,000–50,000 VND for bags.

**Payment apps.** Vietnamese QR payment (VietQR) is now nearly universal among locals, and you can't use it without a Vietnamese bank account. Cash remains the tourist's tool.

## 8. Packing for December, north to central

The temperature swing on this route is the thing people get wrong. Hanoi in December sits at **15–22°C** with lows near 14°C, high humidity and grey drizzle — it feels considerably colder than the number, and buildings are not heated. Ninh Binh is **15–21°C**. Da Nang and Hue are **18–26°C** and wet. You are packing for two different climates nine days apart.

**Bring:**
- **A proper rain shell**, not a poncho. You will use it in Hue and possibly on Hai Van. Oxalis specifies a waterproof jacket for November–March tours.
- **Layers for the north:** a thin merino or fleece mid-layer, a long-sleeved shirt, and something warmer for evenings. Nothing heavy.
- **Quick-dry long trousers and a long-sleeved quick-dry shirt** for the Oxalis day — mandatory, not optional, for protection against rock, insects and poison ivy.
- **Trekking or approach shoes with real grip**, broken in. Mua Cave's 486 steps and the Tu Lan trail both demand it. Oxalis lends shoes in 36–44 but yours will fit better.
- **Calf-length thick socks** — specifically for leeches on the Oxalis trek and at Cuc Phuong.
- **Swimwear and a quick-dry towel** — the Tu Lan underground swim, whether or not you enjoy it.
- **A second pair of shoes** so you're not putting wet ones on the next morning.
- **A small dry bag** for phones on the boats and in the caves. Oxalis provides a shared dry box but a personal one is better.
- **Motorcycle gloves and, if you're serious, your own helmet.** Rental helmets in Vietnam are legally compliant and not much more. A jacket with some abrasion resistance for the Hai Van day.
- **Headtorch** — useful at Cuc Phuong's cave, on unlit lanes in Tam Coc, and boarding a train at 22:00.
- **Small notes wallet** separate from your main one.
- **A universal adapter** — Vietnam uses Type A/C/F at 220 V.
- **Rehydration salts and loperamide.** Not pessimism, arithmetic: eight people, nine days, a lot of street food.
- **An overnight bag for the train** so you don't open suitcases in a sleeper cabin.
- **Passports on you** for the Oxalis tour (they require it) and for bike rental.

**Don't bring:** a heavy coat, hiking poles (unnecessary on these trails), a drone (restricted around the Citadel, the national parks and the caves, and permits are a bureaucratic dead end), or expectations about laundry — it's 20,000–40,000 VND/kg everywhere, done same-day, and you should use it in Ninh Binh before the train.

## 9. Group logistics: where eight people will be awkward

**Boats.** This is the recurring one. Trang An boats hold 4–5, so you're split across two. Van Long boats hold 2–3 — three or four boats. Tam Coc the same. **You will not all be in one boat at any point.** Agree in advance who's with whom so you're not negotiating at a wharf, and agree a meeting point at the other end.

**Restaurant tables.** Almost no street-food place in Vietnam has a table for eight. The normal solution is two or three tables of three or four, pushed roughly together, and you should expect this rather than fight it. **The places where you must reserve:** Chả Cá Thăng Long (Hanoi), Quán Bà Đỏ and Lạc Thiện (Hue), and any goat restaurant in Tam Coc. Everywhere else, arrive before the rush — 11:30 for lunch, 18:00 for dinner — and you'll get seated.

**Vehicles.** A 16-seater takes eight people and eight large bags with room to spare. A 9-seat "Dcar limousine" does not, because the rear seats recline into the luggage space. **Specify Ford Transit or Hyundai Solati.** Do not accept two 7-seaters split across the group unless you have to — you lose the ability to talk to each other for nine days of driving, and it costs more.

**The night train.** Two 4-berth cabins means the group splits 4/4 for the night. Decide the split before you book, because you're buying specific berths. Upper berths have less headroom and are harder to get into; assign accordingly.

**The Oxalis tour.** Eight of a 12-person cap. **Book all eight in one transaction** so you're on the same departure — booking in pairs risks being spread across dates. Ask on the phone whether you can have a private departure; for eight people they may be willing, which means the pace is yours.

**Motorbikes.** Eight bikes is more than a small shop stocks in one model. Book early, and expect a mix of models. **Ride in two groups of four** with a designated lead and sweep in each, and agree stop points in advance — eight bikes trying to stay together through Hue traffic will fragment within a kilometre. Hand signals and a group chat.

**Homestays.** Room counts are the constraint, not price. A homestay with five rooms can take you; one with three cannot. **Ask for the room count and configuration explicitly** — "two doubles and two twins" is what you want, and "one dorm and a family room" is not the same thing. Booking an entire small property (Tam Coc River View has five rooms) is the cleanest solution and often barely costs more.

**Cave tour minimums.** Not an issue — Oxalis's constraint is a maximum of 12, not a minimum. Dark Cave, Tu Lan and Hang En all require an authorised operator for safety reasons, but none has a minimum that eight people would trouble.

**Pace.** Eight adults move at roughly 70% of the speed of two. Every time above has slack in it, but the compounding effect is real: assume 20 minutes of drift on every departure and build it into the morning starts, not the ends of days.

## 11. Risk notes

### Motorbike licensing — read this before you book bikes

This is the highest-consequence detail in the document, and it's the one your group is most likely to get wrong precisely because you all hold motorcycle licences.

**Vietnam recognises only the 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit.** It is not a party to the 1949 Geneva Convention. A 1949 IDP has, in the words of one rental operator, "zero legal weight" — Vietnamese police treat it as no licence at all.

- **1968 IDPs** are issued by the UK, Germany, France and most of the EU. The **UK issues both versions** — you must explicitly ask for the 1968 permit, or the clerk may hand you the 1949 one.
- **1949 IDPs** are issued by the **USA, Canada and Japan**. *Australia is reported inconsistently across sources — verify with your own state motoring authority rather than trusting any travel site, including the ones I read.*
- The IDP must show **motorcycle category A**, must be carried **together with your original national licence**, and your national licence must itself entitle you to ride motorcycles. An IDP based on a car licence does not let you ride.
- Carry **both physical documents** whenever you ride. Enforcement has tightened since the 2024 traffic law overhaul: more checkpoints, higher fines, body cameras, and bribery is now a worse idea than it used to be.

**The insurance consequence is the real risk, not the fine.** If you are riding without a licence Vietnam recognises, **your travel and medical insurance is voided.** In a serious accident you are personally liable for your own medical costs, third-party damage and the bike. Vietnam's road fatality rate is among the highest in Southeast Asia and central-Vietnam hospital care for a major trauma means an air evacuation you would be paying for.

**Practical upshot.** Check every rider's IDP version **now**, not in December. If any rider is on a 1949 IDP, that person should take the van or the train on Days 8 and 9 — this is not a technicality to ride through. Rental shops will hand bikes to anyone; that transfers none of the liability.

Separately: the bike must carry **compulsory Vietnamese third-party liability insurance** — ask to see it, it's a paper slip. And read your own travel policy's motorcycle clause, including any engine-capacity cap (many exclude over 125 cc) and helmet requirements.

### Road safety
- **Helmets always**, including on the Bong Lai farm lanes. It's the law and it's enforced.
- **Ride in two groups of four.** Eight bikes cannot hold formation.
- **The Hai Van descent** is the technical section: tight switchbacks, patched surface, sea mist. Engine braking, not brake braking. Go slowly.
- **Buffalo, dogs and children** appear without warning on rural lanes. Do not ride Bong Lai or the Tam Coc lanes after dark.
- **Deposits:** rental shops commonly ask for a passport or $300 per bike. **Pay the cash deposit, never surrender a passport.** A withheld passport is a known leverage tactic in disputes over scratches, and you legally need it with you.
- **Photograph every bike** from all sides before you ride it, with the odometer and any existing damage, and get the rental contract in writing.

### Cave tour safety
Oxalis is the serious operator here and the safety infrastructure is real: gear from France, the UK and Switzerland, guides trained by British Caving Association members, a satellite phone on tour, a documented emergency response procedure, and dedicated rescue teams per operating area. But note the honest limitation in their own material: **rescue and medical support take 30 minutes to 2 hours to reach you**, phone signal is negligible, and smartphones don't work in the valley.

Which means: declare medical conditions and medications truthfully on the booking form. Bring your own medication. Follow the guides — they have the authority to end anyone's expedition for non-compliance and they use it. The 15 m ladder descent is on a harness and rope operated by the safety team; if anyone in the group has a serious problem with heights or with enclosed dark water, **book Hang Tien instead** and don't try to tough it out in a cave passage.

Dark Cave, Tu Lan, Hang En and Son Doong all legally require an authorised operator. Phong Nha Cave, Paradise Cave, Nuoc Mooc and the Botanical Gardens you can do independently with a ticket from the gate.

### Seasonal and weather
- **December is the tail of central Vietnam's wet season.** Hue and Da Nang can see 200–300 mm in the first half of the month; conditions typically improve after mid-December, which is when you're travelling — but "typically" is doing work in that sentence.
- **Typhoon season (October–November) is over**, but Phong Nha–Ke Bang occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm, and the road to Paradise Cave floods. Have Day 6 and Day 7 ready to swap.
- **Cold water.** Cave water sits at 20–22°C and December air won't warm you afterward. This is a comfort risk, not a safety one, but it can spoil a day.
- **The north is dry but grey.** December is one of Hanoi's driest months (20–25 mm across 5–8 days), but persistent drizzle and fog are normal and buildings are unheated.
- **Leeches** at Cuc Phuong and on the Tu Lan trail in wet conditions. Thick calf-length socks.

### Health and general
- **Ice:** cylindrical with a hole through the middle is factory-made and safe. Crushed ice is less reliable.
- **Street food:** pick busy stalls. Turnover is the safety mechanism.
- **Water:** bottled or filtered only. Oxalis filters and boils theirs on tour.
- **Pharmacies** are on every corner and stock more than you'd expect without prescription.
- **Nearest serious hospital to Phong Nha** is in Dong Hoi, 45 km. Nearest international-standard care is Da Nang or Hanoi. Factor this into your insurance choice.
- **Traffic as a pedestrian:** cross at a constant speed without stopping. Scooters flow around you. Stopping mid-road is what causes accidents.

## 12. Verify before you go

Things I could not pin down, or that moved recently. Check each a week out.

**Administrative reorganisation.** On 1 July 2025 Vietnam consolidated from 63 provinces to 34 and abolished the district tier. On your route: **Quang Binh no longer exists** — Phong Nha is now in **Quang Tri province**. **Quang Nam no longer exists** — Hoi An is now in **Da Nang City**. **Thua Thien Hue** is now simply **Hue**, a centrally governed city. **Ninh Binh** absorbed Ha Nam and Nam Dinh. Consequences for you: booking-site addresses, Google Maps labels and guidebook references are inconsistent; commune names have changed (Tam Coc's area is now **Ninh Hải**); and Oxalis's own address now reads "Phong Nha Commune, Quang Tri Province." **Give drivers hotel names and pinned map locations, not postal addresses.**

**Prices I'm unsure about:**
- **Trang An:** sources split between 250,000 and 300,000 VND. Recent 2026 listings favour 300,000. Budget the higher.
- **Hue Citadel:** listed as both 150,000 and 200,000 VND for 2026. Budget 200,000.
- **Paradise Cave:** 250,000 in older sources, 270,000 in 2026 listings.
- **Oxalis tours:** the Tu Lan Experience is 2,000,000 VND on Oxalis's own site as of this writing, but they raised the Tu Lan Cave Encounter from 7,700,000 to 7,900,000 effective 15 November 2026 — day-tour prices may follow. Check the site.
- **Heritage train Hue → Da Nang:** quoted anywhere from 180,000 to 600,000 VND depending on source and class, with a weekend surcharge. Wide enough that you should just look it up.

**Schedules I'm unsure about:**
- **SE19 departure from Ninh Binh:** 21:51, 22:00 and 22:02 all appear across operators. Arrival in Dong Hoi is given as 05:40, 06:15 and 06:25. **Reconfirm on dsvn.vn** — the whole Day 6 morning hangs off this.
- **Heritage train HĐ1 departure from Hue:** 07:05, 07:45 and 07:50 across three sources.
- **Cuc Phuong hours:** 07:00–18:00, 08:00–18:00 and 07:00–20:00 all appear. Arrive by 09:00 and it doesn't matter.

**Places I can't confirm are still trading:**
- **Hanoi cooking classes.** Hanoi Cooking Centre, Rose Kitchen and Hanoi Chef Class are the names in circulation; I have not verified current operation, pricing, or whether any can take eight in one session. **Email two of them.**
- **Phong Nha restaurants.** Turnover here is high and several names in three-year-old guides have closed. Ask your homestay what's open this month.
- **Individual goat restaurants in Tam Coc.** There are a dozen signposted *dê núi* places along the road; I can't vouch for specific hours. The homestay can.
- **Mì Quảng Bà Mua branches.** Sources give 95A Nguyen Tri Phuong, 19 Tran Binh Trong, 22 Trung Nu Vuong and 44 Le Dinh Duong. Some are branches, some may be defunct. Check the map on the day.
- **Bún bò Huế opening hours.** The best shops open early and close when the pot empties; several run only a few morning hours. If you want the peak bowl, plan it as breakfast.

**Exchange rate.** 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND in mid-September 2026, having ranged 25,900–26,400 over the preceding year. All USD figures in this document will drift.

**Visa.** Vietnam's e-visa programme now covers nationals of all countries and territories, with visa exemptions for 29 countries. Check your own nationality's current requirement and apply well ahead — and note that accommodation providers and tour operators are legally required to register your temporary residence, so **everyone needs their passport with them throughout**, not locked in a hotel safe in another city.
