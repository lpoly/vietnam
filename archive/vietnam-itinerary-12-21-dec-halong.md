# Northern → Central Vietnam with Ha Long Bay, 12–21 December

**Group:** 6–8 adults · **Riders:** all experienced, motorcycle licences held · **Hiking:** mixed appetite, need easy and hard options · **Priorities:** nature and the must-see landmarks first; good food alongside, not instead

**Dates fall on a weekend you should know about:** 12 December 2026 is a **Saturday**. So Hanoi is a weekend (good for the Hoan Kiem walking street and night market), the bay cruise is Monday night, Ninh Binh is Tue–Wed (fewer domestic coaches), and Hue is Fri–Sat (busier, book early). Departure day, 21 December, is a Monday: Da Nang → Hanoi in the morning, then the long way home via Shanghai and Athens.

**Exchange rate used throughout:** 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND (mid-September 2026; it has ranged 25,900–26,400 over the past year). Prices were re-checked in October 2026; where sources still disagree, that's flagged.

**This is the Ha Long Bay version.** Hanoi keeps its two nights; the bay takes a night from Phong Nha instead. On Monday you board a boat at Tuan Chau, on Tuesday you drive from the boat straight to Tam Coc, Ninh Binh and the night train move a day later, and Phong Nha becomes one night with the show caves (the Oxalis trek becomes an optional swap). From Friday 18 December on, nothing changes. The original route is the other version of this plan.

## 1. The route, and what it costs you

### Chosen route

| Nights | Base | Nights | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12–13 Dec | **Hanoi** | 2 | The capital's landmarks (Hoan Kiem Lake, the Temple of Literature, the Ho Chi Minh complex, Hoa Lo Prison) with the north's best food in between. Also the only sane place to absorb jet lag. |
| 14 Dec | **Ha Long Bay (overnight cruise from Tuan Chau)** | 1 (on the water) | Vietnam's best-known landscape: limestone islands rising out of the sea, seen by kayak and from the deck at sunset. A night on board is what sources consistently rate above a day trip, and the boat is the hotel. |
| 15 Dec | **Ninh Binh (Tam Coc / Hoa Lu)** | 1 | The best karst-and-river landscape reachable in the dry northern winter: Trang An's cave boats, Mua Cave's view, Van Long's wetland. One night and two days is enough if you start early. |
| 16 Dec | **Night train SE19** | 1 (on rails) | Converts a 9-hour road slog into a night you'd have spent in bed anyway. The single biggest time win in the itinerary. |
| 17 Dec | **Phong Nha (Son Trach)** | 1 | The nature centrepiece, cut to one day in this version: Phong Nha Cave by boat and Paradise Cave, then the Bong Lai valley at dawn before the drive south. |
| 18–19 Dec | **Hue** | 2 | The imperial capital: the Citadel and the royal tombs of Tu Duc, Khai Dinh and Minh Mang. Two nights is what it takes to see both. Also Vietnam's most distinctive regional cooking. |
| 20 Dec | **Hoi An** | 1 | The UNESCO old town, at the end of the Hai Van Pass ride: lanterns, the river, and *cao lầu* at its source. 40–50 minutes from DAD for the flight home. |

Six bases, nine nights, one of them on a train and one on a boat. No backtracking: from Hanoi east to the bay, then south to Ninh Binh without going back through Hanoi, and from there a single southward line.

### What you're giving up

**Cuc Phuong — cut in this version.** It was a full day of old-growth forest and the Endangered Primate Rescue Centre. It went so that Hue gets a second night and the royal tombs. The nature on this route is still Trang An, Van Long, Phong Nha's caves and the Hai Van ride.

**Phong Nha's second day — the price of the bay.** The original plan gives Phong Nha two nights; this one gives it one, and Hanoi keeps both of its. What goes is the **Oxalis cave trek** (Hang Tien or Tu Lan), the second Phong Nha day. You still get the show caves, Phong Nha Cave by boat and Paradise Cave, on the day you arrive. **If the group would rather trek**, swap them: book Oxalis for 17 December (pickup 07:00–07:30, half an hour after you reach Phong Nha) and do Paradise Cave on the morning of the 18th instead of the scooters, as the original rain plan does. Day 6 has the details.

The bay could have been paid for elsewhere. A **day cruise on the way to Tam Coc** keeps every night, but means about six hours in a van and no night on the water. **Taking a Hanoi night** leaves the capital one jet-lagged afternoon. Ninh Binh is already one night.

**Pu Luong and Mai Chau — cut.** Pu Luong is quieter than Ninh Binh, has better homestays and better walking. But in mid-December the terraces are bare stubble (harvest is September–October), the valley sits under fog for days at a time, and daytime highs are 13–17°C with high humidity. You would also pay for it in driving hours: Hanoi → Pu Luong is 4.5–5 hours west, then Pu Luong → Ninh Binh another 3, versus a flat 2h15 Hanoi → Ninh Binh on the CT01 expressway. **If you want it anyway, see Variant B below.**

**My Son Sanctuary — skipped.** The Cham temple ruins are 37–50 km from Hoi An, 150,000 VND, and need half a day. The last morning goes to the flight instead.

**Da Nang — ridden through, not stayed in.** The bikes pass through the city in the afternoon; *mì Quảng* is noted on Day 9 if you want to stop. Sleeping in Hoi An instead means the old town at night, and the airport is still 40–50 minutes away.

**Bach Ma — cut.** Don't go in December. The summit sits in cloud for most of the month, the waterfall trails are slick, and the road to the top is sometimes shut after rain. Phong Nha is the better forest.

**The far north (Ha Giang, Sapa) — a different trip.** For a group of licensed riders, the far-north mountain loops are the obvious temptation. They need several days on their own and don't fit nine nights going south.

### Variant A — Da Nang instead of Hoi An for the last night

If the 21 December flight out of DAD ends up before about 08:00, or Hoi An is flooding (see §11), finish the ride in Da Nang instead: stay in Hai Chau near the Han River, 15 minutes from the airport. **Brilliant Hotel**, **Avora Hotel** and **Hadana Boutique** were the well-rated options in October 2026 (from ~$52–85 a room). You lose the Hoi An evening; Hoi An becomes a 30-minute car ride for dinner if you still want it.

### Variant B — keep Pu Luong, drop Ninh Binh

Hanoi 12–13, **Pu Luong 14**, then Pu Luong → Thanh Hoa station (2.5h) for the night train south on 15 Dec, Phong Nha 16–17, Hue 18–19, Hoi An 20. Same shape, same number of moves, but it's a variant of the original route: with the bay as well, it doesn't fit in nine nights. You trade Trang An's karst rivers for Thai-minority villages, better homestays and worse weather.

### One thing I'd add if the budget allows

**Oxalis Hang En Cave Adventure (2 days, 1 night)** — 9,500,000 VND per person, sleeping on a beach inside one of the world's largest caves. It runs "December to mid-September"; the first December departure date wasn't published in October. It doesn't fit this version: with one Phong Nha night, Hang En would mean giving up the bay.

### How to book each of these

- **Private vans (Hanoi and Ninh Binh legs):** Ford Transit or Hyundai Solati 16-seaters run $160–200/day including driver, fuel, tolls and parking; airport transfers $50–100 one way. Book by WhatsApp two to three weeks out. HanoiCarRent (Hoan Kiem-based, hanoicarrent.com) and Asia Transport quote these rates publicly. **For eight people plus luggage, insist on a 16-seater, not a 9-seat Dcar limousine**: the Dcar's rear seats fold into recliners and the boot disappears.
- **Hanoi → Tuan Chau, and the bay:** Book the cruise first, then the transfers. Most boats sell a Hanoi shuttle, about US$30–40 per person for the round trip, picking up at 07:30–09:00. You only need it one way, so ask for a one-way price or a Ninh Binh drop-off: **Heritage Binh Chuan** quotes Hanoi pickup with Ninh Binh drop-off at US$35 per person. Otherwise a private 16-seater, 2,700,000 VND Hanoi → Ha Long on one operator's rate sheet. For **Tuan Chau → Tam Coc**, **Vivu Halong** lists a 16-seat van at US$120 one way, picking up at the pier; ask for it to stay the afternoon for Van Long.
- **Night train:** Book 3–4 weeks ahead. **dsvn.vn** is the state railway (cheapest, patchy English, often rejects non-Vietnamese cards); **Baolau** and **12go** take foreign cards and issue e-tickets. You want **two whole 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins** (*giường nằm mềm điều hòa*) so your eight fill both. The private operators **Lotus Train** and **Laman Express** still attach refurbished cabins to SE19, at about 1,800,000–2,200,000 VND per berth (Baolau, October 2026). Exchange the e-ticket voucher for a paper ticket at the station at least 30–60 minutes before departure.
- **Dong Hoi → Phong Nha:** Oxalis runs station transfers at 650,000 VND per 7-seater one way. Book it through Oxalis (or inside the trek booking, if you swap the trek in) and tell them the train now arrives around 05:16.
- **Phong Nha → Hue:** **Culture Pham Travel** quotes 3,000,000 VND for a minivan plus US$15 for the Vinh Moc stop; **Explore Vietnam** quotes US$130 for a van (max 12 people) plus US$20 for Vinh Moc. Arrange 1–2 weeks out by email or WhatsApp.
- **Hue tomb day:** **Hue Private Cars** lists a 16-seat van with driver at US$50 for a four-site day, US$65 for five; a guide is about US$30 extra. Entry tickets are not included.
- **Hue → Hoi An motorbikes:** **Hue Scooter Rental / Hidden Land Travel** (huescooterrental.com) does one-way Hue → Hoi An from US$25 per bike, luggage 50,000 VND per piece. **Motorvina** lists it from US$36 per person including bag transfer, helmets and petrol. **Style Motorbikes** (stylemotorbikes.com) has the bigger bikes but no published one-way price. Reserve eight bikes at least three weeks ahead. **Hand over cash deposits, not passports** (see §11).
- **Hoi An → airport:** Grab is scarce in Hoi An at night, so book a private car or 16-seat van the evening before (450,000–750,000 VND).

Whether it stops at T2 or only at T1 isn't clear (sources disagree), and it doesn't reach the hotel door. Sources: [Noi Bai Airport, official](https://www.noibaiairport.vn/en/public-transportations-nid1.html), [mia.vn, Jul 2025](https://mia.vn/cam-nang-du-lich/danh-sach-xe-buyt-san-bay-noi-bai-18948).

## Day 1 — Saturday 12 December · Hanoi
*Theme: land, see the lake and the Old Quarter, and catch the weekend.*

### Movements
- **11:00** Van from Noi Bai, Terminal 2 arrivals. 35–40 km, ~1 hour to the Old Quarter. ~1,200,000 VND for a 16-seater. The driver should be holding a name board outside the arrivals hall.
- Book through your hotel (most Old Quarter hotels run their own cars) or HanoiCarRent. Do **not** rely on airport taxi touts with eight people and eight bags; you'll end up split across three cars at inflated rates.
- Everything today is on foot. The Old Quarter is roughly 1 km across.

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **La Siesta Premium Hang Be** — 27 Hang Be, Old Quarter. Rated 9.5; weekend average ~$83 a room (Kayak snapshot, October 2026). Four rooms sleeps eight. Two minutes from Hoan Kiem Lake.
2. **Hanoi La Siesta Classic Ma May** — 94 Ma May St, Hoan Kiem. Rated 9.5; from ~$47, but the weekend average was ~$152 a room in the October snapshot. Rooftop bar with a view over to Long Bien Bridge.
3. **Hanoi Emerald Waters Hotel & Spa** — 47 Lo Su. Rated 9.5; weekend average ~$88 a room.

**Budget note:** your stated band is $40–80 per person per night. At eight people sharing four rooms, nothing on this route needs the top of it. See §6.

### Activities
- **12:00** Check in, shower, do not nap for more than 45 minutes. Jet lag from the west is best broken by staying up to a local bedtime.
- **14:00 Hoan Kiem Lake and Ngoc Son Temple** — 30,000 VND, roughly 07:00–18:00. The temple sits on an island in the lake, reached by the red bridge. The lake is the centre of the city and the start of everything else today.
- **15:00–16:30** Walk the Old Quarter loop: Ma May → Hang Bac (silversmiths) → Hang Ma (paper goods) → Dong Xuan Market → Hang Chieu → back down Hang Buom. The Old Quarter's street names are its trade names, and walking it is the cheapest orientation there is.
- **16:30** Coffee at **Cafe Dinh**, 13 Dinh Tien Hoang — up an unmarked staircase beside a silk shop, second floor, overlooking the lake. Egg coffee (*cà phê trứng*), ~30,000 VND.
- **18:30 Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre** — 57B Dinh Tien Hoang, on the lake. 100,000 / 150,000 / 200,000 VND by seat class. Shows at 16:10, 17:20, 18:30 and 20:00. Book 3–7 days ahead, at the box office or by phone or email. **The site thanglongwaterpuppet.org is not the theatre's** and should not be used.
- **20:30–22:00** **Hoan Kiem weekend walking street.** The roads around the lake close to traffic from Friday 19:00 to Sunday midnight (suspended in bad weather). Families, badminton, street games, and the least filtered version of Hanoi's public life you'll see. Free.

### Food
- **Lunch, 12:45 — Bún Chả Đắc Kim**, 1 Hang Manh St, Hoan Kiem. Open ~10:00–14:00. 70,000–100,000 VND/person. Order **bún chả** (grilled pork patties and belly in a sweet-sour fish sauce broth with a mountain of herbs) and **nem cua bể**, the square sea-crab spring rolls. In the 2026 Michelin Guide, still a plastic-stool operation. It's the best first meal in Vietnam because it explains the whole northern flavour logic in one bowl, and it's five minutes from the lake. Go at 12:30, not 13:00.
- **Dinner, 19:30 — Chả Cá Thăng Long**, 6B Duong Thanh St, Cua Dong Ward, Hoan Kiem. Michelin Bib Gourmand. ~200,000–250,000 VND/person. One dish only: **chả cá**, turmeric-marinated catfish grilled in banana leaf, then finished at your table with a hedge of dill and spring onion, over rice vermicelli with peanuts and *mắm tôm* shrimp paste. **Reserve for eight**: call the day before; the pale-yellow townhouse at 6B is the good branch of three on the same street.

### Local-life element
Walk up to **Long Bien Bridge** at dusk and cross the pedestrian walkway on the upstream side. It's the steel cantilever bridge over the Red River whose builder's plate reads 1899–1902, Daydé & Pillé, Paris, still carrying scooters and some trains (fewer since February 2026). At dusk it fills with commuters, couples and people fishing off the piers. Free, five minutes' walk from Ma May. Stand aside for scooters: the walkway is narrow and they use it. Do it before the puppets, or skip it today and do it at 17:30 tomorrow.

### Rain plan
December rain in Hanoi is drizzle, not downpour, and the whole day works under an umbrella. If it's properly wet, the walking street may be suspended: swap the Old Quarter loop for the **Vietnamese Women's Museum** (36 Ly Thuong Kiet, 08:00–17:00, 40,000 VND), genuinely good on ethnic minority textiles and marriage customs. The water puppets are indoors either way.

## Day 2 — Sunday 13 December · Hanoi
*Theme: the national landmarks, in the right order.*

### Movements
- All on foot and by Grab. Download **Grab** before you fly and add a card; a Grab bike across the Old Quarter is 20,000–30,000 VND. Eight people in Grab cars is two or three vehicles.

### Accommodation
Same as Day 1.

### Activities
- **06:15** Market breakfast at Chau Long (below), ten minutes' walk from the mausoleum.
- **07:45 Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum** — Free. Open Sundays 08:00–11:00 in the cool season, closed Mondays and Fridays, so today is the day. Arrive early: the queue is long and the rules are strict (shoulders and knees covered, no shorts, bags stored, phones and cameras collected, silence, hats off). The interior is a short walk past the embalmed body.
- **09:00** **The Presidential Palace grounds and the stilt house** behind the mausoleum: 25,000 or 40,000 VND, open mornings. The palace is seen from outside; the stilt house where Ho Chi Minh lived is the quiet, interesting part. The **One Pillar Pagoda**, next to the mausoleum, is free.
- **10:30 Temple of Literature** — 58 Quoc Tu Giam St; 08:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30); 70,000 VND, cash. Vietnam's 1070 Confucian academy: the stelae of doctoral laureates sit on stone tortoises in the third courtyard. One hour is enough.
- **12:00** Lunch (below).
- **13:30 Hoa Lo Prison** — 08:00–17:00 daily, ~50,000 VND (sources range 30,000–50,000), audio guide 50,000 VND. The French colonial prison later known as the "Hanoi Hilton". About an hour; ten minutes' walk south of the lake.
- **15:00 Museum of Ethnology** — In Cau Giay, 08:30–17:30 (closed Mondays), 40,000 VND, 25 minutes by Grab. The outdoor collection of reconstructed minority houses is the best thing of its kind in the country. **Or**, for the less museum-minded: **Thang Long Imperial Citadel** (100,000 VND, 08:00–17:00), the excavated royal citadel next to the mausoleum area.
- **17:30** Long Bien Bridge at dusk if you didn't do it yesterday.
- **20:00** *Bia hơi* — fresh draught beer, brewed and delivered daily, 15,000–20,000 VND a glass. Go to the corner of **Nguyen Huu Huan and Hang Thung** or **Bia Hơi Hải Xồm**, rather than Ta Hien, which is now largely a backpacker street. Order *nem chua nướng* (grilled fermented pork) and *đậu phụ tẩm hành* (fried tofu with spring onion) with it.
- **Optional, evening:** the **Temple of Literature night tour** (199,000 VND, with a 3D mapping show, typically Thu–Sun 19:00–22:00; book at least 2 days ahead via vanmieunighttour.com). *December dates weren't published in October.*

### Food
- **Market breakfast, 06:15 — Chợ Châu Long (Chau Long Market)**, Chau Long St, Ba Dinh. Opens around 05:30, busiest 06:00–08:00. A working neighbourhood market: live fish in basins, women shelling crab, a dozen breakfast stalls along the interior aisles. 30,000–50,000 VND/person. Eat **bánh cuốn** (steamed rice sheets rolled around minced pork and wood-ear mushroom) and **xôi xéo** (sticky rice with mung bean paste and fried shallot). Bring small notes; nobody here takes cards.
- **Lunch, 12:00 — Phở Hà**, 15 Hang Hom St, Hang Gai, Hoan Kiem. ~60,000–80,000 VND. Order **phở gà** (chicken pho, the northern default more than beef) and **bún thang**, a clear broth with shredded egg crêpe, chicken, pork sausage, dried shrimp and a whisper of shrimp paste: the most technically demanding dish in the Hanoi repertoire.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — your choice in the Old Quarter**, then *bia hơi*.

### Local-life element
**A market-to-table cooking class** is now optional rather than built in: it costs a whole afternoon of landmarks. If some of the group would rather cook than do Hoa Lo and the museum, **Hanoi Cooking Centre** on Chau Long St, **Rose Kitchen** and **Hanoi Chef Class** are the commonly cited operators, around 900,000–1,400,000 VND per person for 3–4 hours. *Current pricing and operation in December 2026 aren't confirmed.*

### Rain plan
Everything today is either indoors or a short walk between buildings. If it's properly wet, drop the Imperial Citadel for the Museum of Ethnology (largely indoors) and add **Bảo tàng Mỹ thuật Việt Nam** (Fine Arts Museum, 66 Nguyen Thai Hoc, 08:30–17:00, 40,000 VND): the lacquer and silk collections are unexpectedly strong.

## Day 3 — Monday 14 December · Ha Long Bay
*Theme: limestone from the water, and a night on the bay.*

### Movements
- **07:00** Breakfast at the hotel, then check out with all your bags. The boat is tonight's hotel, and you don't come back through Hanoi.
- **08:00** Cruise shuttle or private 16-seat van from the Old Quarter to **Tuan Chau harbour**, about 130 km on the Hanoi–Hai Phong–Ha Long expressway: **2.5–3 hours** (sources range from 2 to 3.5). Arrive 20–30 minutes before check-in.
- **11:30–12:00** Check in and board. Most cruises, Ha Long and Lan Ha alike, now leave from Tuan Chau.
- The Noi Bai–Ha Long seaplane stopped on 1 April 2026, so it's the road or nothing.
- Ask the boat to store the suitcases. *No source says how much luggage the cabins take; confirm it when you book.*

### Accommodation (1 night, on the boat)
**Lan Ha or Ha Long?** All three boats below board at Tuan Chau. **Lan Ha Bay**, the southern neighbour of Ha Long proper (administratively Hai Phong, not Quang Ninh), is the one sources rate quieter, with the best kayaking and lagoons. **Ha Long Bay** proper has the famous set pieces: Sung Sot cave, Ti Top island, the Fighting Cocks. Since 20 April 2026 a licensed connecting route lets overnight boats sail from Ha Long into Lan Ha. For a December night I'd take a Lan Ha boat.

1. **Indochine Cruise** — Lan Ha route, from Tuan Chau. From US$150 per person.
2. **Heritage Binh Chuan** — Lan Ha, 20 suites. From US$180 per person; one dated quote gives US$420 a cabin for a Delta Suite. Meals, kayaking, swimming, caving, sightseeing tickets and taxes included; transfers and drinks extra. Check-in from 12:10, check-out 10:00.
3. **Indochine Premium** — Ha Long Bay proper, 37 cabins. From US$175 per person, including a kayak or bamboo boat, the caves, Ti Top, all meals, a guide and the entrance and sightseeing fees; transfers and drinks extra.

**These are "from" prices.** No December 2026 quote was found for any boat. You need four cabins on one boat, so book early: some boats are tiny (Orchid Premium has five suites).

### Activities
The programme is the boat's, not yours, and it varies by route. What the boats above include:
- **Afternoon:** sail into the islands of **Lan Ha Bay** (or Ha Long Bay, depending on the boat), then **kayaking or a bamboo boat** among the karst. Lan Ha boats go to places like **Dark & Bright Cave** and the lagoons near **Ba Trai Dao beach**; Ha Long boats to **Sung Sot cave** and **Ti Top island**. Kayaking is allowed only in designated zones, so it's always with the boat's guide.
- **Swimming** is possible but cold: in December the sea is about 21–23°C and the air 15–22°C. Treat it as a short dip around midday.
- **Sunset and the evening on deck.** December mornings are often misty, usually clearing by midday.

Sources: [Best Ha Long Cruise, Apr 2026](https://besthalongcruise.com/halong-bay-vs-lan-ha-bay-vs-bai-tu-long-bay/), [Bhaya, May 2026](https://bhayacruises.com/blog/halong-bay-in-december/), [Nhan Dan, Apr 2026](https://en.nhandan.vn/ha-long-baylan-ha-bay-connecting-route-officially-launched-post161324.html), [Indochina Sails](https://www.indochinasails.com/en/tour/ha-long-indochine-premium-1-night.html), [Halong Bay Tours, Apr 2026](https://www.halongbaytours.com/cruise/heritage-cruise-binh-chuan/2-day-1-night.html).

### Food
- **Lunch, dinner and breakfast — on board.** Included in the price on all three boats above; drinks are extra. Give the boat the group's dietary needs when you book.

### Local-life element
Lan Ha boats can stop at **Viet Hai**, a village on Cat Ba island. If your boat offers a village stop, take it over a second cave.

### Rain plan
Drizzle doesn't stop a cruise: the caves are dry and kayaking runs in light rain. **Fog and wind can.** From December to February, thick fog and the northeast monsoon sometimes suspend departures. The bay's management board and the port authority decide, usually on the day and sometimes the afternoon before, and Ha Long and Lan Ha have different authorities, so one can sail while the other is shut. Complete cancellations are rare. **If it's cancelled before you leave Hanoi**, Bhaya refunds in full (other operators vary): take the van straight to Tam Coc instead, as in the original plan's Day 3, and book a second Tam Coc night. Source: [Bhaya](https://bhayacruises.com/blog/halong-bay-on-storm/).

## Day 4 — Tuesday 15 December · Ninh Binh
*Theme: off the boat and south, then the quiet karst.*

### Movements
- **09:00–10:00** Check out of the cabin. The boat is back at Tuan Chau at about **10:30–11:00** (it varies by boat).
- **11:00** Private 16-seat van, Tuan Chau → Tam Coc: 190–215 km via Hai Phong without going back through Hanoi, **3–3.5 hours**. Vivu Halong lists a 16-seater at US$120 one way, picking up at the pier (a 2024 rate page). Ask for the van to stay the afternoon for Van Long.
- Provincial names changed in July 2025 (see §12). Your hotel's address may still say "Hoa Lu District, Ninh Binh Province"; commune-level names like **Ninh Hải** and **Nam Hoa Lư** are now what appears on official paperwork. Give drivers the hotel name and a pinned Google Maps location, not the postal address.
- **14:30** Arrive, check in, late lunch.

### Accommodation (1 night)
1. **Tam Coc River View Homestay** — 4.7 km from Ninh Binh centre, 300 m from Tam Coc wharf. Five rooms including quadruples and a family room, so the eight of you can take the whole property. Simple, restaurant on site.
2. **Tam Coc Rice Fields Resort** — From about €39 a room (Expedia, July 2026 snapshot).
3. **Tam Coc Garden** — Restored brick-and-tile buildings in a garden of fish ponds. Now a boutique resort rather than a homestay, from about US$248 a night for two (Expedia, May 2026); late check-out "for a fee, subject to availability".

**Because the train leaves at night**, keep two rooms until about 21:00 on Day 5 for showers and repacking. Nobody in Tam Coc publishes a day-use rate: either book those two rooms for a second night or get late check-out agreed in writing.

### Activities
- **14:30** Check in and a late lunch.
- **15:15–18:00 Van Long Nature Reserve**, ~40 minutes' drive north (Gia Vien). Entry around 20,000 VND plus a boat at 50,000–60,000 VND per person (one older provincial list gives 100,000 VND all-in), **cash only, small notes**. Flat-bottomed boats, two or three passengers each, rowed by local women, 1h30 on open wetland under limestone cliffs. **Go late afternoon:** the light is better, the herons and egrets come in to roost, and if you're lucky the Delacour's langur troop comes down the cliffs to feed.
- **Bích Động Pagoda** moves to tomorrow afternoon (Day 5, 16:00), because you arrive later than in the original plan.
- **18:30** Back at the hotel.

### Food
- **Late lunch, 14:45 — at the hotel.** 150,000–200,000 VND/person. Ask for **cơm cháy** (Ninh Binh's crisped rice cake, deep-fried, with a pork or goat topping) and a plate of **dê núi**, mountain goat, grilled or steamed with lemongrass and lime leaf. Goat is the provincial obsession; it's raised on the limestone hills.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — a goat dinner.** At the homestay if they'll cook it, or at **Nhà hàng Đức Dê** or any of the goat restaurants signposted *dê núi* along the Tam Coc road, ~200,000 VND/person. *Hours for individual goat restaurants aren't verifiable; there are a dozen and they're all open at dinner.*

### Local-life element
**Rent bicycles** (usually free or ~50,000 VND/day) and ride the lanes between Tam Coc and Ninh Hai before dinner or early tomorrow. In December the paddies are between crops: fields being turned, ducks herded down the lanes, stubble burning. You'll be riding through farmyards. Slow down, nod, and don't photograph people's houses without a gesture first.

### Rain plan
Van Long boats run in light rain: take the rain cape the boatwoman offers and go anyway. If it's heavy, swap Van Long for **Hoa Lu ancient capital** (below; mostly covered temple buildings) and **Phát Diệm Cathedral** (free, ~30 km south), an 1891 stone cathedral built in Vietnamese temple form, with wooden pillars the width of a person.

## Day 5 — Wednesday 16 December · Ninh Binh, then the night train
*Theme: the famous karst, beaten at its own game by starting early.*

### Movements
- Van on standby from 07:00 until the station, plus bicycles. ~3,100,000 VND for a full day with a late finish. **Tell the operator explicitly that you need the vehicle until 21:45**: a standard day hire ends at 18:00 and overtime runs 350,000–500,000 VND/hour. Tam Coc wharf and Trang An wharf are 6–8 km apart; Mua Cave is 3 km from Tam Coc.
- **21:15** Hotel → **Ninh Binh Railway Station**, 1 Hoang Dieu St (some listings say 41 Hoang Hoa Tham — the station has been re-addressed; both refer to the same place, central Ninh Binh city). 20 minutes.
- **21:57 Train SE19 → Dong Hoi, arriving 05:16.** Two 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins. *Times disagree between sources: departure is given as 21:51, 21:57, 21:58 and 22:21, arrival anywhere from 04:40 to 06:00. The most recent (Baolau, October 2026) is 21:57 → 05:16. No December timetable was out yet. Reconfirm on dsvn.vn a week out and again the day before.*

### Accommodation
**On the train tonight.** Two private 4-berth cabins: bedding, air-con and a power socket per berth. It's the 6-berth hard sleeper you want to avoid, not the 4-berth. **Pack an overnight bag** so you're not opening suitcases in a cabin at 05:00. Keep two Tam Coc rooms until evening (see Day 4).

### Activities

**The honest brief on Trang An and Mua Cave:** both are heavily touristed and both are worth it anyway, provided you handle the timing.

- **07:00** Breakfast at the hotel, or take *bánh mì* to go.
- **07:30 Trang An, Route 2.** Ticket **300,000 VND/person** since 1 January 2026 (it was 250,000; many English blogs still say so). The ticket includes a seat in a 4–5 person rowed boat; open 07:00–16:30 in Oct–Dec. Buy at the wharf, or on Klook with a QR code that skips the queue. **Boats hold 4–5, so you'll be in two.** The route is 2.5–3 hours through flooded caves, some low enough that you duck, past temples and the *Kong: Skull Island* set. **Arrive by 07:30**: the domestic coaches land between 09:00 and 11:00. (Route 3 was suspended for high water in July 2026; Routes 1 and 2 kept running.)
- **11:00** Cycle or drive 8 km to **Hoa Lu ancient capital**. 20,000 VND. Vietnam's capital from 968 to 1010, now two modest temples to the Dinh and Le emperors in a bowl of limestone. Forty minutes. History rather than spectacle.
- **12:30** Lunch.
- **14:00 Mua Cave (Hang Múa)**, **150,000 VND** since 1 April 2026, open 06:00–19:00. **486 stone steps** to a Quan Am statue and a dragon ridge with the best aerial view of the Tam Coc valley in the province. Steep, uneven, with a handrail; genuinely hard work in the last third. **This is the split point for the mixed hiking appetite**: the climb takes 25–40 minutes and there's a café and lotus pond at the bottom for anyone who'd rather not.
- **16:00** Optional: **Bích Động Pagoda**, 2 km from Tam Coc wharf, free: three tiers of temple built into a limestone cliff, reached by a stone stair through a cave, usually nearly empty. Or **Bái Đính Pagoda** (free entry, electric cart 60,000 VND), the largest pagoda complex in Vietnam, vast and covered; or **Thung Nham** bird park (250,000 VND since April 2026), whose heron roost fills at dusk. Or rest: it's a long night.
- **18:00** Showers and repacking in the kept rooms. **19:00** Dinner. **21:15** Station.

### Food
- **Lunch, 12:45 — Nhà hàng Đức Dê** or any of the goat restaurants signposted *dê núi* along the Tam Coc road. ~200,000 VND/person. Order **dê tái chanh** (goat sliced thin and "cooked" in lime juice with sesame and herbs), **dê nướng lá lốt** (grilled in wild betel leaf) and **cơm cháy**. The hotel will phone ahead for eight.
- **Dinner, 19:00 — at the hotel.** Ask for **ốc nấu chuối đậu** (snails stewed with green banana and tofu) if they'll do it, a northern home dish you will almost never see on a menu. **Eat properly before the train**: there's a trolley service on SE19 with instant noodles and beer, and that's all.
- **Buy on the way to the station:** water, fruit, *bánh mì*, beer. There's a small market and several shops on the approach to Ninh Binh station.

### Local-life element
Ask your hosts the evening before whether anyone in the family or village keeps goats, and whether you can walk up with them at dawn. Ninh Binh's goat herds go out onto the limestone at first light, and it's the most characteristic thing that happens here. Offer something: 200,000–300,000 VND for the group is generous and not insulting.

### Rain plan
Trang An operates year-round and is largely sheltered: go in rain. **Skip Mua Cave if it's wet**: the steps are polished limestone and genuinely dangerous slick. Substitute **Bái Đính Pagoda**, covered and enormous, which is where Vietnamese pilgrims actually go.

## Day 6 — Thursday 17 December · Phong Nha
*Theme: arrive off the train, get underground, keep it easy.*

### Movements
- **05:16** Arrive **Dong Hoi station**, Thuan Ly St (now Quang Tri province, formerly Quang Binh). Earlier than the old 06:25: the transfer needs to know.
- **05:30** Two 7-seat transfers to Phong Nha, 45 km, ~1 hour. **650,000 VND per vehicle** through Oxalis. ~1,300,000 VND total.
- **06:30** Arrive Son Trach (Phong Nha town). Your rooms won't normally be ready this early: **ask for early check-in in writing when you book**, or book the room from the night of the 16th.
- Private van for the day from 10:00: ~2,000,000 VND. Paradise Cave is 30 km out; the Phong Nha Cave wharf is in town.

### Accommodation (1 night)
1. **Phong Nha Farmstay** — 8 km out of town, surrounded by paddy. Direct rates in October 2026: standard double or twin 1,300,000 VND, family room 2,400,000 VND; check-out 11:00; a 2-night minimum has been reported, which matters for one night: ask, or take a homestay. French-colonial style, pool, free bikes, and the original foreign-run guesthouse in the region (open since 2010). Genuinely good food, if pricier than town. The quiet and the paddy views are the point.
2. **Thao Nguyen Homestay**, **Phong Nha Riverside Homestay** or **Jungle Boss Homestay** — In town, family-run, roughly US$15–40 a room (Jungle Boss quoted ~800,000 VND for a double, ~1,000,000 for a family room). Simpler, closer to the morning market and cheap dinners. **Several Phong Nha homestays are cash-only.**
3. **Chay Lap Farmstay** — If you book Oxalis, a discounted rate of 1,100,000 VND/room/night (~$42). Pool, spa, kayaking. Comfortable rather than local.

### Activities
- **06:30–09:30** Breakfast at the market, check in if you can, wash, lie down. The train arrives early enough that this is time you can use properly.
- **10:00–12:00 Phong Nha Cave by boat.** Entry **150,000 VND/person**; the boat is priced per boat, max 12 people, so one boat for your eight. *Sources disagree on the boat price: 550,000 VND, 750,000 VND, or 55,000 VND per person.* Boats are managed by the Phong Nha Tourist Centre at a fixed price with no touting, and no advance booking is possible or needed. You motor 1.5 km up the underground Son River into the cave, then walk out onto a sandbar and explore a lit chamber on foot. Physically trivial. **Tien Son Cave** (80,000 VND) above it is a dry cave up a flight of steps: fine rather than essential.
- **12:15** Lunch in town.
- **13:30–16:00 Paradise Cave (Thiên Đường).** **270,000 VND/person** including the electric buggy (October 2026; older sources say 250,000 plus the buggy). **It closes at 16:00**, so arrive by 13:30. The buggy takes you to the base of the stairs; then it's 500 steps up and a descent into the cave. The first kilometre is boardwalked in a chamber so large that the lighting doesn't reach the ceiling; the full cave runs 31 km. The coach groups arrive between 09:00 and 12:00 and are thinning out by mid-afternoon.
- **16:30** **The Phong Nha Caves Exploration Museum** in town: free, 08:00–17:30. It's where you'll understand who found these caves and how: Hồ Khanh, a local logger, found the entrance to Son Doong in 1991 while sheltering from a storm and couldn't relocate it for years. Skip the 500,000 VND VR, read the walls, talk to whoever's on the desk.
- **18:00** Back for dinner.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:45 — Chợ Troc / Son Trach morning market**, central Phong Nha, from ~05:30. 30,000–40,000 VND/person. Noodle stalls and *bánh mì* carts serving construction crews and cave guides. Order **bánh mì** with pâté and pickled carrot and a bowl of **bún**.
- **Lunch, 12:15 — Phong Nha Bamboo Restaurant** or **Mountain View Restaurant**, both on the main street in Son Trach. 80,000–150,000 VND/person. Order **cá kho tộ** (fish braised in a clay pot with caramel and pepper) and **lươn xào nghệ** (eel stir-fried with turmeric). *Restaurant turnover in Phong Nha is high; several names in older guides have closed. Ask your hosts what's good this month.*
- **Dinner, 19:00 — home-cooked at your homestay.** 150,000–250,000 VND/person. Ask when you book.

### Local-life element
Arrange tomorrow morning's scooters today, through your homestay or any shop on the Son Trach main street: 8 scooters at ~150,000–200,000 VND each for the Bong Lai valley loop.

### Optional: the Oxalis trek instead
This version has one Phong Nha day, so it's the show caves or the trek, not both. **To trek instead:** Oxalis picks you up at 07:00–07:30, half an hour after you reach Phong Nha, and brings you back around 19:30–20:00, with all transport and meals included; do Paradise Cave on Day 7 morning instead of the scooters. It's a long day straight off a sleeper train.


**Oxalis day tours run on fixed dates with a 12-person cap, so your eight can effectively own a departure. Book the day you commit to the trip.** Two good December choices, both 2,000,000 VND / US$75 per person, both rated for fit beginners, ages 16–70:

- **Hang Tien Cave Day Trek** — Dry cave, enormous entrance, huge calcite formations, and a natural swimming pool you can decline to enter. Season November to end of September. **The better December choice for a mixed group, and the default here.**
- **Oxalis Tu Lan Experience (1 day)** — 9 km trek through valleys and river crossings, 800 m of cave, a **15 m ladder descent on harness and rope**, and **150 m swimming through an underground river in a life vest**. *Oxalis's own pages disagree on its season (one says year-round, another mid-November to mid-September).* The December caveat: cave water sits around 20–22°C and the air outside is 17–21°C with no sun to warm you afterward. Most people find it exhilarating; some find it grim.

You can split the group across two tours on the same date: call the hotline rather than trying to do it through the website. Book online at oxalisadventure.com; full payment confirms; free cancellation to 7 days out. **Declare honestly** on the booking form: health conditions, medications, trekking experience. Oxalis operates in remote valleys with a 30-minute-to-2-hour rescue window and means it.

**What to bring:** Quick-dry long-sleeved shirt and long trousers, a waterproof jacket (Oxalis specifies one for November–March), closed trekking shoes with grip, **calf-length thick socks** (leeches), a change of clothes for afterward, insect repellent, towel, swimwear, passport. Oxalis lends trekking shoes in sizes 36–44 for a 50,000 VND cleaning fee; bring your own if you can. They supply helmet, headlamp, gloves, harness, dry pack, water and first aid.

### Rain plan
Both caves are caves: rain changes nothing about Phong Nha Cave or Paradise Cave except the drive. **What rain does change:** the road to Paradise Cave can flood after heavy rain, and the park very occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm. If the road's out, do Phong Nha Cave, Tien Son and the museum, and try Paradise Cave on Day 7 morning instead of the scooters.

## Day 7 — Friday 18 December · Phong Nha → Hue
*Theme: two wheels through the valley, the DMZ on the way, then Hue by evening.*

### Movements

### Accommodation (2 nights)
1. **White Lotus Hue** — 5–7 Hoang Hoa Tham, south bank. Rated 9.2; ~US$68 a room with breakfast in autumn 2026 snapshots.
2. **Alba Spa Hotel** — 29 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. Rated 8.9–9.0; ~US$28–30 a room. This side of the Perfume River is where the food is, and it's a 10-minute walk to Dong Ba Market across Truong Tien Bridge.
3. **Silk Path Grand Hue** — 2 Le Loi, on the river. Upper mid-range, ~US$59–110 a room.

**Book Hue for a Friday and Saturday with some urgency**: it's a weekend in a compact hotel district.

### Activities
- **07:00 Bong Lai valley by scooter.** The valley is a farming basin 10 km out of Son Trach with a handful of family-run stops: the **Duck Stop** (ducks, buffalo, ~150,000 VND), **Wild Boar Eco Farm**, and **the Pub With Cold Beer** (a farmhouse at the end of a dirt track). **Flag: this loop has become a circuit**, with signage and mid-morning minibuses. **Ride it at 07:00 and you'll have it to yourselves.** Easy riding; the only hazards are buffalo and dogs.
- **12:30–13:30 Vinh Moc Tunnels**, en route, 07:00–17:00, *50,000 or 100,000 VND/person (sources disagree)*. An entire village of 300 people lived underground here for six years, in three levels of tunnel dug into the coastal clay, with a maternity room where 17 children were born. You walk through them. Low, dark and narrow, genuinely claustrophobic for some, and the most affecting hour on this route. **Hien Luong Bridge** and the Ben Hai River at the 17th parallel are ten minutes further on and free: a five-minute stop, half the bridge painted each colour.
- **16:00–18:00** Walk Hue: the riverfront on Le Loi, **Truong Tien Bridge**, and **Chợ Đông Ba** (Dong Ba Market). Save the Citadel for tomorrow's opening hour.
- **18:30** Eat, at length.

### Food
- **Breakfast, 06:30 — Phong Nha market** or homestay, before the ride. Light; you're on a bike.
- **Lunch, 11:30 — on the road.** Have the driver stop at a *quán cơm bình dân* (a point-and-choose rice shop) in Dong Ha or Gio Linh. 50,000–70,000 VND/person, six or seven dishes on a counter, you point. This is how Vietnam eats lunch on a working day.
- **Dinner part one, 18:30 — Hue rice cakes.** **Quán Bà Đỏ**, 7 Nguyen Binh Khiem, just off Chi Lang, or **Hàng Me Mé**, 60 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. 120,000–180,000 VND/person for a full spread. Order the set: **bánh bèo** (thumbnail-sized steamed rice cakes topped with dried shrimp, scallion oil and pork crackling), **bánh bột lọc** (translucent tapioca dumplings with shrimp and pork), **bánh nậm** (flat steamed cakes in leaf), **bánh ram ít** (a sticky dumpling on a fried rice cracker). Court cooking that escaped into the street.
- **Dinner part two, 20:00 — Bánh khoái.** **Lạc Thiện**, 6 Dinh Tien Hoang, ~08:00–22:30, or **Chị Hạnh**, 11–15 Pho Duc Chinh, 09:00–21:00. ~100,000 VND/person. A small, turmeric-yellow crisp pancake of shrimp, pork and bean sprout, eaten by hand with herbs and a peanut-and-liver dipping sauce. Order **nem lụi** alongside: lemongrass-skewered grilled pork you roll yourself.
- **Reserve for eight at Bà Đỏ and Lạc Thiện.** Both are small. The hotel will phone.

### Local-life element
**Chợ Đông Ba (Dong Ba Market)**, on the north bank by Truong Tien Bridge, roughly 06:00–19:00. Hue's central market; the food section on the ground floor is where the city eats. Go in the evening for *bánh ép*, rice-and-shrimp batter pressed flat on a hot iron and rolled with herbs and green mango, 10,000 VND a piece.

### Rain plan
- **If it's raining in Phong Nha at 06:30:** cancel the scooters. Sealed farm roads in the wet with eight riders and no upside is a bad trade. Leave at 08:00 instead and add **Khe Sanh Combat Base** (50,000 VND) to the drive, or get to Hue early.
- **If it's raining in Hue:** the market is covered and the food is indoors. Add the **Hue Royal Antiquities Museum** (50,000 VND).

### Accommodation

## 4. Regional food primer

Food on this route is the thing you do between the landmarks, not instead of them, but it's very good, and it changes every few hundred kilometres.

**Hanoi and the north.** Restrained, savoury, sour, herb-forward. Salt and fish sauce rather than sugar; vinegar and lime rather than chilli. Dill appears in northern cooking and nowhere else in Vietnam.
- **Must eat:** *bún chả* · *chả cá* · *bún thang* · *bánh cuốn* · *phở gà*
- **Best on your route:** Hanoi, Days 1–2.

**Ninh Binh.** A small, specific repertoire built on the limestone hills.
- **Must eat:** *cơm cháy* (crisped rice cake) · *dê núi* (mountain goat, grilled, steamed, or lime-cured) · *ốc nấu chuối đậu* if you can find someone's grandmother to cook it
- **Best on your route:** Tam Coc and Ninh Binh city.

**Quang Binh / Phong Nha.** The plainest cooking on the route, and not a failing: river fish, eel, chicken, and clay-pot braises.
- **Must eat:** *cá kho tộ* (clay-pot braised fish) · *lươn xào nghệ* (eel with turmeric)
- **Best on your route:** Son Trach town, and your homestay's kitchen.

**Hue.** The most distinctive regional cuisine in Vietnam: the imperial court's habit of many small, precisely composed dishes, and a street tradition of chilli-and-shrimp-paste noodle soups. *Mắm ruốc* (fermented shrimp paste) is the base note under almost everything.
- **Must eat:** *bún bò Huế* · *cơm hến* · *bánh bèo / bánh bột lọc / bánh nậm / bánh ram ít* (as a set) · *bánh khoái* with *nem lụi* · *bánh ép* from Dong Ba
- **Best on your route:** Hue, two nights now, so there's time. Eat *bún bò* for breakfast.

**Hoi An, Da Nang and Quang Nam.** Bolder than Hue, less courtly, built on the coast and on turmeric. Broth is used sparingly.
- **Must eat:** *cao lầu* (Hoi An only) · *mì Quảng* · seafood at Lang Co
- **Best on your route:** Hoi An on the last evening; Lang Co on the ride.

**Ordering mechanics.** Most street places serve one thing; the menu is the sign. At a *quán*, sit and someone will come. Pay after eating. *Rau* (herbs) and *tương ớt* (chilli sauce) on the table are free and unlimited. Ice in cylinders with a hole through the middle is factory-made and safe; crushed ice is less reliable.

3. **Upgrade the train.** Lotus Train or Laman Express cabins on SE19 at ~1,800,000–2,200,000 VND per berth: better bedding and a welcome drink.

I'd take option 2.

## 7. Cash and payments

**Carry 3,000,000–4,000,000 VND per person in cash** for a trip of this length, and top up from ATMs rather than arriving with all of it. Where cards work, use them; where they don't, it's absolute.

**Cards fail at:** every street food stall and market · Van Long · Mua Cave · the Temple of Literature (cash only) · most Phong Nha homestays · goat restaurants in Tam Coc · Bong Lai valley farms · the Phong Nha Cave boat · petrol stations for small fills · Grab bikes if you haven't linked a card.

**Cards work at:** hotels, Trang An, the Hue Imperial City, Oxalis (pay in advance online), mid-range restaurants in Hanoi, Hue and Hoi An, supermarkets.

**ATMs.** Use bank-branded machines inside bank premises: **Vietcombank, BIDV, Techcombank, VietinBank, TPBank**. Avoid standalone machines in tourist lanes. Most cap withdrawals at 3,000,000–5,000,000 VND per transaction and charge 22,000–55,000 VND per withdrawal; **TPBank and some Techcombank machines dispense up to 10,000,000 VND** with lower fees. ATMs in Tam Coc village and Son Trach are sparse to nonexistent — **withdraw in Hanoi, Ninh Binh city, Hue and Hoi An.**

**Notes.** 500,000 VND notes are hard to break at a market stall. Ask for a mix. The 20,000 (blue) and 500,000 (blue-green) notes look similar in poor light and this is a real and common error.

**Tipping.** Not expected, increasingly present.
- Street food, markets, *quán*: nothing.
- Restaurants: round up, or 5–10% if there's no service charge.
- Homestay family dinner: nothing, but a gift for the household if they've gone out of their way.
- Boat rowers at Van Long and Trang An: 50,000–100,000 VND per boat is normal and welcome.
- **Oxalis guides, porters and cooks, if you swap the trek in: the one place to tip properly.** A pooled 500,000–1,000,000 VND from the group, handed to the guide for the team.
- Drivers: 100,000–200,000 VND for a good full day.
- Hotel staff: 20,000–50,000 VND for bags.

**Payment apps.** Vietnamese QR payment (VietQR) is nearly universal among locals, and you can't use it without a Vietnamese bank account. Cash remains the tourist's tool.

## 8. Packing for December, north to central

The temperature swing on this route is the thing people get wrong. Hanoi in December sits at **15–22°C** with lows near 14°C, high humidity and grey drizzle; it feels considerably colder than the number, and buildings are not heated. Ninh Binh is **15–21°C**. Hue averages a high of about **24°C** and a low of about **19°C**, with around 350 mm of rain in December. You are packing for two different climates nine days apart.

**Bring:**
- **A proper rain shell**, not a poncho. You will use it in Hue and possibly on Hai Van. Oxalis specifies a waterproof jacket for November–March tours.
- **Layers for the north:** a thin merino or fleece mid-layer, a long-sleeved shirt, and something warmer for evenings. Nothing heavy.
- **Quick-dry long trousers and a long-sleeved quick-dry shirt** for the Oxalis trek, if you book it: required for protection against rock, insects and plants.
- **Trekking or approach shoes with real grip**, broken in. Mua Cave's 486 steps and Paradise Cave's 500 both demand it.
- **Calf-length thick socks** — for leeches on the Oxalis trek in wet conditions.
- **Swimwear and a quick-dry towel** — the cave pools, whether or not you go in.
- **A second pair of shoes** so you're not putting wet ones on the next morning.
- **Clothes that cover shoulders and knees** for the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, pagodas and the Hue tombs.
- **A small dry bag** for phones on the boats and in the caves.
- **Motorcycle gloves and, if you're serious, your own helmet.** Rental helmets in Vietnam are legally compliant and not much more. A jacket with some abrasion resistance for the Hai Van day.
- **Headtorch** — useful on unlit lanes in Tam Coc and boarding a train at 22:00.
- **Small notes wallet** separate from your main one.
- **A universal adapter** — Vietnam uses Type A/C/F at 220 V.
- **Rehydration salts and loperamide.** Not pessimism, arithmetic: eight people, nine days, a lot of street food.
- **An overnight bag for the train** so you don't open suitcases in a sleeper cabin.
- **Passports on you** for the Oxalis tour, the train and bike rental.

**Don't bring:** a heavy coat, hiking poles (unnecessary on these trails), a drone (restricted around the Citadel, the national parks and the caves), or expectations about laundry — it's 20,000–40,000 VND/kg everywhere, done same-day; use it in Hanoi before you head south.

## 9. Group logistics: where eight people will be awkward

**Boats.** The recurring one. Trang An boats hold 4–5, so you're split across two. Van Long boats hold 2–3, so three or four boats. **You will not all be in one boat at any point.** Agree in advance who's with whom, and a meeting point at the other end.

**Restaurant tables.** Almost no street-food place in Vietnam has a table for eight. The normal solution is two or three tables of three or four, pushed roughly together. **The places where you must reserve:** Chả Cá Thăng Long (Hanoi), Quán Bà Đỏ and Lạc Thiện (Hue), and any goat restaurant in Tam Coc. Everywhere else, arrive before the rush — 11:30 for lunch, 18:00 for dinner.

**Vehicles.** A 16-seater takes eight people and eight large bags with room to spare. A 9-seat "Dcar limousine" does not, because the rear seats recline into the luggage space. Do not accept two 7-seaters split across the group unless you have to: you lose the ability to talk to each other for the whole trip, and it costs more.

**The night train.** Two 4-berth cabins means the group splits 4/4 for the night. Decide the split before you book, because you're buying specific berths. Upper berths have less headroom; assign accordingly.

**Four cabins on one boat.** Book the cruise as one group of four cabins, not four separate bookings, or you may be split across boats.

**The Oxalis tour, if you swap it in.** Eight of a 12-person cap. **Book all eight in one transaction** so you're on the same departure. Ask on the phone whether you can have a private departure.

**Motorbikes.** Eight bikes is more than a small shop stocks in one model. Book early, and expect a mix of models. **Ride in two groups of four** with a designated lead and sweep in each, and agree stop points in advance: eight bikes trying to stay together through Hue traffic will fragment within a kilometre.

**Hotels.** Room counts are the constraint, not price. **Ask for the room count and configuration explicitly**: "two doubles and two twins" is what you want, and "one dorm and a family room" is not the same thing.

**The mausoleum queue.** Eight people, no bags, no phones: decide who holds the bags outside, or use the bag store, before you join the queue.

**Pace.** Eight adults move at roughly 70% of the speed of two. Every time above has slack in it, but the compounding effect is real: assume 20 minutes of drift on every departure and build it into the morning starts.

## 11. Risk notes

### Motorbike licensing — read this before you book bikes

This is the highest-consequence detail in the document, and the one your group is most likely to get wrong precisely because you all hold motorcycle licences.

**Vietnam recognises only the 1968 Vienna Convention International Driving Permit.** It is not a party to the 1949 Geneva Convention. A 1949 IDP has, in the words of one rental operator, "zero legal weight": Vietnamese police treat it as no licence at all.

- **1968 IDPs** are issued by the UK, Germany, France and most of the EU, including Greece. The **UK issues both versions**: you must explicitly ask for the 1968 permit.
- **1949 IDPs** are issued by the **USA, Canada and Japan**. *Australia is reported inconsistently across sources — verify with your own state motoring authority rather than trusting any travel site.*
- The IDP must show **motorcycle category A**, must be carried **together with your original national licence**, and your national licence must itself entitle you to ride motorcycles. An IDP based on a car licence does not let you ride.
- Carry **both physical documents** whenever you ride. Enforcement has tightened since the 2024 traffic law overhaul: more checkpoints, higher fines, body cameras.

**The insurance consequence is the real risk, not the fine.** If you are riding without a licence Vietnam recognises, **your travel and medical insurance is voided.** In a serious accident you are personally liable for your own medical costs, third-party damage and the bike.

**Practical upshot.** Check every rider's IDP version **now**, not in December. If any rider is on a 1949 IDP, that person takes the van on Day 7 and the train or a van on Day 9. Rental shops will hand bikes to anyone; that transfers none of the liability.

Separately: the bike must carry **compulsory Vietnamese third-party liability insurance** — ask to see it, it's a paper slip. And read your own travel policy's motorcycle clause, including any engine-capacity cap (many exclude over 125 cc) and helmet requirements.

### Road safety
- **Helmets always**, including on the Bong Lai farm lanes. It's the law and it's enforced.
- **Ride in two groups of four.** Eight bikes cannot hold formation.
- **The Hai Van descent** is the technical section: tight switchbacks, patched surface, sea mist. Engine braking, not brake braking. Go slowly.
- **Buffalo, dogs and children** appear without warning on rural lanes. Do not ride Bong Lai or the Tam Coc lanes after dark.
- **Deposits:** rental shops commonly ask for a passport or $300 per bike. **Pay the cash deposit, never surrender a passport.** A withheld passport is a known leverage tactic in disputes over scratches, and you legally need it with you.
- **Photograph every bike** from all sides before you ride it, with the odometer and any existing damage, and get the rental contract in writing.

### Cave tour safety
Oxalis is the serious operator here and the safety infrastructure is real: gear from France, the UK and Switzerland, guides trained by British Caving Association members, a satellite phone on tour, and dedicated rescue teams. But note the honest limitation in their own material: **rescue and medical support take 30 minutes to 2 hours to reach you**, and phone signal is negligible in the valleys.

Declare medical conditions truthfully on the booking form. Bring your own medication. Follow the guides: they have the authority to end anyone's day for non-compliance and they use it. If anyone has a serious problem with heights or enclosed dark water, **book Hang Tien, not Tu Lan.**

### Seasonal and weather
- **December is the tail of central Vietnam's wet season.** Hue averages around 350 mm of rain over about 20 days in December. Flood peaks are usually October–November, but "usually" is doing work in that sentence: Hue had record floods in late October 2025.
- **Hoi An floods.** The old town's flood risk runs from late October to mid-December; on 15 December 2016 the riverside streets were under 1.5–2 m of water. Watch the forecast in the week before, and use Variant A if it's flooding.
- **Phong Nha–Ke Bang** occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm, and the road to Paradise Cave floods. Have Day 6 and Day 7 morning ready to swap.
- **Cold water.** Cave water sits at 20–22°C and December air won't warm you afterward. A comfort risk, not a safety one.
- **The north is dry but grey.** Persistent drizzle and fog are normal in Hanoi in December, and buildings are unheated.
- **Fog on the bay.** From December to February, fog and the northeast monsoon can suspend cruise departures. See Day 3's rain plan.
- **Leeches** on the Oxalis trails in wet conditions. Thick calf-length socks.

### Health and general
- **Ice:** cylindrical with a hole through the middle is factory-made and safe. Crushed ice is less reliable.
- **Street food:** pick busy stalls. Turnover is the safety mechanism.
- **Water:** bottled or filtered only.
- **Pharmacies** are on every corner and stock more than you'd expect without prescription.
- **Nearest serious hospital to Phong Nha** is in Dong Hoi, 45 km. Nearest international-standard care is Da Nang or Hanoi. Factor this into your insurance choice.
- **Traffic as a pedestrian:** cross at a constant speed without stopping. Scooters flow around you. Stopping mid-road is what causes accidents.
- **Train Street (Hanoi):** groups are banned and barriers are up; the cafés operate in a grey zone under police watch. Optional and at your own risk; the legal alternative is the café inside Long Bien station.

## 12. Verify before you go

Things that could not be pinned down in the October 2026 re-check, or that moved recently. Check each a week out.

**Administrative reorganisation.** On 1 July 2025 Vietnam consolidated from 63 provinces to 34 and abolished the district tier. On your route: **Quang Binh no longer exists** — Phong Nha is now in **Quang Tri province**. **Quang Nam no longer exists** — Hoi An is now in **Da Nang City**. **Thua Thien Hue** is now simply **Hue**, a centrally governed city. **Ninh Binh** absorbed Ha Nam and Nam Dinh. **Quang Ninh**, where Ha Long Bay is, became a centrally governed city on 1 September 2026, and Tuan Chau is now a ward of it. **Lan Ha Bay** is in Hai Phong, which absorbed Hai Duong. Booking-site addresses, Google Maps labels and guidebook references are inconsistent; commune names have changed (Tam Coc's area is now **Ninh Hải**). **Give drivers hotel names and pinned map locations, not postal addresses.**

**Prices I'm unsure about:**
- **Van Long:** 20,000 VND entry plus a 50,000–60,000 VND boat, or 100,000 VND all-in, depending on the source.
