# Northern → Central Vietnam: the guide

Nine nights, five places, one long line south: from Hanoi's food streets, through the limestone karst of Ninh Binh, a night on the train, the caves of Phong Nha, the court cooking of Hue, and over the Hai Van Pass into Da Nang. No backtracking.

This is the readable version. Every price, time and address below comes from the full itinerary; where the itinerary wasn't sure, it says so here too, and those bits are flagged. The hour-by-hour schedule lives in the Plan tab.

A few things worth knowing before anything else: 12 December 2026 is a **Saturday**, so Hanoi is a weekend (good for the Hoan Kiem walking street and night market), Ninh Binh is Mon–Wed (fewer domestic coaches), and Hue and Da Nang are Sat–Sun (busier). Prices use 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND. And **carry cash**: street food, markets, most homestays, park gates and boats don't take cards.

## Hanoi

*12–13 December · 2 nights · Days 1–2*

Hanoi is the deepest food city on the route, and the reason it comes first: food is a primary reason for the trip, and nothing later on replaces Hanoi for northern cooking. It's also the only sane place to absorb jet lag.

You'll be based in the Old Quarter, which is roughly 1 km across and best done on foot. Its street names are its trade names, and walking it is the cheapest orientation there is. You're here on the right weekend: the roads around Hoan Kiem Lake close to traffic from Friday evening to Sunday midnight.

December here is **15–22°C** with lows near 14°C, high humidity and grey drizzle. It feels considerably colder than the number, and buildings are not heated.

### See and do

- **Hoan Kiem weekend walking street** — From Friday evening to Sunday midnight the roads around the lake close to traffic. Families, badminton, street games, and the least filtered version of Hanoi's public life you'll see. Free. Worth deliberately timing (19:00–22:00 on Saturday): this only happens at weekends, and you have the right weekend.
- **Long Bien Bridge** — The 1903 Eiffel-designed cantilever bridge, still carrying trains and scooters. Walk up at dusk and cross the pedestrian walkway on the upstream side: at 17:30 it fills with commuters, couples and people fishing off the piers. Free, five minutes' walk from Ma May. Stand aside for scooters; the walkway is narrow and they use it.
- **Temple of Literature** — Quoc Tu Giam, 58 Quoc Tu Giam St; 08:00–17:00; 70,000 VND. Vietnam's 1070 Confucian academy; the stelae of doctoral laureates sit on stone tortoises in the third courtyard. Get there at opening: by 10:00 it's full of coach groups, at 08:00 it's students and old men. One hour is enough.
- **Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum complex** — The honest assessment: the mausoleum queue is long, the interior is 40 seconds of walking past a body, and the rules (no bags, no phones, no stopping) are strictly enforced. **The Presidential Palace grounds and the stilt house** behind it, by contrast, are quiet and interesting (40,000 VND). The day plan pairs it with the **One Pillar Pagoda**.
- **Museum of Ethnology** — In Cau Giay, open Tue–Sun, 8:30–17:30, 40,000 VND, 25 minutes by Grab. The outdoor collection of reconstructed minority houses is the best thing of its kind in the country, and it's largely indoors if it rains.
- **Vietnamese Women's Museum** — 36 Ly Thuong Kiet, 08:00–17:00, 40,000 VND. Genuinely good on ethnic minority textiles and marriage customs, and mercifully small. The rain-day option.
- **Bảo tàng Mỹ thuật Việt Nam** — The Fine Arts Museum, 66 Nguyen Thai Hoc, 08:30–17:00, 40,000 VND. The lacquer and silk collections are unexpectedly strong.
- **A market-to-table cooking class** — A market walk with a Vietnamese cook who explains what's in season and how to buy it, then cooking four or five dishes in a home kitchen and eating them. Expect 900,000–1,400,000 VND per person for a 3–4 hour class. **Hanoi Cooking Centre** on Chau Long St is the name most often recommended; **Rose Kitchen** and **Hanoi Chef Class** are the other commonly cited operators. *The itinerary can't confirm current pricing or that any specific one is still operating in December 2026.*

### Eat and drink

Northern food is restrained, savoury, sour and herb-forward: salt and fish sauce rather than sugar, vinegar and lime rather than chilli. Dill appears in northern cooking and nowhere else in Vietnam. The must-eats are *bún chả*, *chả cá*, *bún thang*, *bánh cuốn* and *phở gà*. Eat properly here.

- **Bún Chả Đắc Kim** — 1 Hang Manh St, Hoan Kiem. Open ~10:00–14:00, 70,000–100,000 VND/person. Order **bún chả** (grilled pork patties and belly in a sweet-sour fish sauce broth with a mountain of herbs) and **nem cua bể**, the square sea-crab spring rolls. In the 2026 Michelin Guide, still a plastic-stool operation. It's the single best first meal in Vietnam because it explains the whole northern flavour logic in one bowl. Go at 12:30, not 13:00.
- **Chả Cá Thăng Long** — 6B Duong Thanh St, Cua Dong Ward, Hoan Kiem. Michelin Bib Gourmand, ~200,000–250,000 VND/person. One dish only: **chả cá**, turmeric-marinated catfish grilled in banana leaf and finished at your table with a hedge of dill and spring onion, eaten over rice vermicelli with peanuts and *mắm tôm* shrimp paste. Say yes to the shrimp paste. The pale-yellow townhouse at 6B is the good branch of three on the same street. **Reserve for eight** the day before.
- **Phở Hà** — 15 Hang Hom St, Hang Gai, Hoan Kiem. ~60,000–80,000 VND. Order **phở gà** (chicken pho, the northern default more than beef) and **bún thang**, a clear, almost translucent broth with shredded egg crêpe, chicken, pork sausage, dried shrimp and a whisper of shrimp paste. It's the most technically demanding dish in the Hanoi repertoire and almost nobody outside the city cooks it.
- **Bánh Gối** — At 52 Ly Quoc Su. ~15,000 VND each. Deep-fried "pillow dumplings" of pork, glass noodle and quail egg. Order four each; you will regret ordering two.
- **Cafe Dinh** — 13 Dinh Tien Hoang, up an unmarked staircase beside a silk shop, second floor, overlooking the lake. Egg coffee (*cà phê trứng*), ~30,000 VND. This is where egg coffee is actually drunk rather than photographed.
- **Bánh Cuốn Bà Xuân** — Dốc Hòe Nhai, Nguyen Trung Truc, Ba Dinh, 06:30 onwards. Five minutes from Chau Long Market, further from the Old Quarter, correspondingly fewer foreigners.
- **Bia hơi** — Fresh draught beer, brewed and delivered daily, 15,000–20,000 VND a glass. Go to the corner of **Nguyen Huu Huan and Hang Thung** or **Bia Hơi Hải Xồm**, rather than Ta Hien, which is now largely a backpacker street. Order *nem chua nướng* (grilled fermented pork) and *đậu phụ tẩm hành* (fried tofu with spring onion) with it.

### Markets and shops

- **Hang Bac** — The silversmiths' street. The Old Quarter loop runs Ma May → Hang Bac → **Hang Ma** (paper goods) → **Dong Xuan Market** → Hang Chieu → back down Hang Buom. No tickets, no queues.
- **Chợ Châu Long (Chau Long Market)** — Chau Long St, Ba Dinh. Opens around 05:30, busiest 06:00–08:00. A working neighbourhood market, not a tourist one: live fish in basins, women shelling crab, a dozen breakfast stalls along the interior aisles. 30,000–50,000 VND/person for breakfast. Eat **bánh cuốn** and **xôi xéo** from whichever stall has the most stools occupied. Bring small notes; nobody here takes cards.
- **The weekend night market** — The itinerary picks this weekend partly because Hanoi on a weekend is good for the Hoan Kiem walking street and night market.

### Where we sleep

- **Hanoi La Siesta Classic Ma May** — Ma May St, Hoan Kiem. From ~1,400,000 VND ($56)/double; four twin/double rooms sleeps eight. Rooftop bar with a view over to Long Bien Bridge. Two blocks off the noise of Ta Hien but still inside the Old Quarter food grid.
- **Essence Hanoi Hotel & Spa** — 22 Ta Hien St. From ~1,200,000 VND ($48)/double. Cheaper, right on Beer Corner: take an upper floor or you will not sleep on a Saturday.

### Getting around

- **From the airport** — A pre-booked 16-seat van from Noi Bai, Terminal 2 arrivals: 35–40 km, ~1 hour to the Old Quarter, ~1,200,000 VND. The driver should be holding a name board outside the arrivals hall. Do **not** rely on airport taxi touts with eight people and eight bags.
- **Grab** — Download it before you fly and add a card. A Grab bike across the Old Quarter is 20,000–30,000 VND and there are always eight of them within two minutes. Eight people in Grab cars is two or three vehicles.
- **On foot** — The Old Quarter is roughly 1 km across.

### Good to know

- **Rain** — December rain in Hanoi is drizzle, not downpour, and the whole place works under an umbrella. If it's properly wet, the museums above are the fallback.
- **Crossing the road** — Cross at a constant speed without stopping. Scooters flow around you. Stopping mid-road is what causes accidents.

## Ninh Binh

*14–16 December · 2 nights · Days 3–5*

The best karst-and-river landscape reachable in the dry northern winter: limestone, rice paddies and rowed boat routes through flooded caves. You'll stay around **Tam Coc**, a 20-minute drive from Ninh Binh city, and you're here Monday to Wednesday, which means fewer domestic coaches.

Two things make it work. First, timing: Trang An and Mua Cave are heavily touristed and worth it anyway, provided you go when the crowds aren't there. Second, the quieter places: Van Long has the same karst with a fraction of the traffic, and Bích Động is usually nearly empty because everyone is at the boats. Cuc Phuong adds old-growth forest.

Provincial names changed in July 2025: Tam Coc's area is now **Ninh Hải**, and hotel addresses may still say "Hoa Lu District". Give drivers the hotel name and a pinned map location, not the postal address. December here is **15–21°C**.

### See and do

- **Trang An, Route 2** — A UNESCO mixed site, and the boat route through nine flooded caves has no real equivalent. Ticket **250,000–300,000 VND/person**; *sources disagree and 2026 listings lean to 300,000, so assume the higher figure.* The ticket includes the boat; buy at the wharf, or on Klook with a QR code that skips the queue. Boats hold 4–5. The route is 2.5–3 hours, rowed, through caves low enough that you duck, past temples and the *Kong: Skull Island* set. **Arrive by 07:30**: the domestic coaches land between 09:00 and 11:00.
- **Mua Cave (Hang Múa)** — 100,000 VND, cash preferred. **486 stone steps** to a Quan Am statue and a dragon ridge with the best aerial view of the Tam Coc valley in the province. Steep, uneven, with a handrail, genuinely hard work in the last third; the climb takes 25–40 minutes, and there's a café and lotus pond at the bottom for anyone who'd rather not. Go in the afternoon. **Skip it if it's wet**: the steps are polished limestone and genuinely dangerous slick.
- **Van Long Nature Reserve** — About 40 minutes north (Gia Vien): a wetland with the same karst but a fraction of the traffic. Entry plus boat around 80,000–100,000 VND/person, **cash only, bring small notes**. Flat-bottomed boats, two or three passengers each, rowed by local women, 1h30 on open wetland under limestone cliffs. Go late afternoon, when the herons and egrets come in to roost and, if you're lucky, the Delacour's langur troop comes down the cliffs to feed. The trip's answer to Halong Bay. Boats run in light rain; take the rain cape the boatwoman offers.
- **Bích Động Pagoda** — 2 km from Tam Coc wharf, free. Three tiers of temple built into a limestone cliff, reached by a stone stair through a cave. Twenty minutes of climbing, usually nearly empty because everyone is at the boats.
- **Hoa Lu ancient capital** — 100,000 VND, 07:00–17:00. Vietnam's capital from 968 to 1010, now two modest temples to the Dinh and Le emperors in a bowl of limestone. Forty minutes. History rather than spectacle; mostly covered, so it works in rain.
- **Thung Nắng** and **Thung Nham** — Thung Nang is a smaller, quieter boat route than Tam Coc. Thung Nham Bird Park (~150,000 VND) has a heron roost that fills at dusk. Either is a good decompression after the Mua Cave steps.
- **Cuc Phuong** — Old-growth forest about 50 km (1h10) from Tam Coc. Entry **50,000–60,000 VND/person**, plus **60,000 VND** for the Endangered Primate Rescue Centre, **cash only at the gate**. Park hours are roughly 07:00–18:00 (*some sources say 08:00–18:00 or later; arrive by 09:00 and it's moot*). The 20 km road from the gate to the park centre runs under closed canopy almost the whole way.
- **Endangered Primate Rescue Centre** — Fifteen species, around 180 animals, all rescued from traffickers or born here, including the world's first captive-born Cat Ba langur and Delacour's langur. Your ticket also covers the **Turtle Conservation Centre** next door. A conservation facility, not a zoo, and the best-run one in Vietnam. It's staffed largely by Vietnamese conservation biologists who will talk at length if you ask real questions.
- **The Ancient Tree trail** — From the Cuc Phuong park centre, about 3 km each way through primary forest to the **1,000-year-old cho tree**: 2.5–3 hours round trip, undulating, with some root-and-rock scrambling. Moderate. For a half-day, the **Botanical Garden** and the **Prehistoric Cave** (Động Người Xưa) are within 30 minutes of the centre on easy paths. **Leeches are real** in wet conditions.
- **Bái Đính Pagoda** — Free entry, electric cart 60,000 VND. An enormous 2010s temple complex: architecturally hollow but vast and covered, and it's where Vietnamese pilgrims actually go.
- **Phát Diệm Cathedral** — Free, ~30 km south. An 1891 stone cathedral built in Vietnamese temple form, with wooden pillars the width of a person and no crowds at all in bad weather. One of the strangest buildings in the country.

### Eat and drink

Ninh Binh has a small, specific repertoire built on the limestone hills: **cơm cháy** (crisped rice cake, deep-fried and served with a rich pork or goat topping) and **dê núi**, mountain goat, which is the provincial obsession. It's raised on the limestone hills and it tastes of them.

- **The homestay family dinner** — ~100,000–150,000 VND/person, cooked by the family. Better than any restaurant in Tam Coc village, which has drifted heavily toward Western menus and beer gardens over the last five years. Ask for goat, and for **ốc nấu chuối đậu** (snails stewed with green banana and tofu), a northern home dish you will almost never see on a menu.
- **Goat restaurants** — **Nhà hàng Đức Dê** or any of the goat restaurants signposted *dê núi* along the Tam Coc road, ~200,000 VND/person. Order **dê tái chanh** (goat sliced thin and "cooked" in lime juice with sesame and herbs), **dê nướng lá lốt** (grilled in wild betel leaf) and a plate of **cơm cháy**. *The itinerary can't verify opening hours for individual goat restaurants; there are a dozen and they're all open at lunch.* The homestay will phone ahead for eight.
- **Chợ Rồng (Rong Market)** — Central Ninh Binh city, ~05:00–11:00, twenty minutes by van. *Bún* stalls, *bánh đa* and the province's breakfast trade in full swing; 30,000 VND/person.
- **Eating out in Tam Coc** — **Chookie's** and **Thao Beo** in Tam Coc village are both decent and both full of foreigners. For actual local eating, eat *cơm cháy* and goat at one of the *nhà hàng dê* on Tran Hung Dao in Ninh Binh city, a 20-minute drive each way.
- **A picnic for Cuc Phuong** — The restaurant at the park centre is functional and not much more. Ask the homestay to pack *bánh mì*, boiled eggs, *xôi*, fruit and a thermos: 80,000–120,000 VND/person.

### Markets and shops

- **Chợ Rồng (Rong Market)** — The central market in Ninh Binh city, ~05:00–11:00.
- **On the way to the station** — There's a small market and several shops on the approach to Ninh Binh station: water, fruit, *bánh mì*, beer.

### Where we sleep

- **Tam Coc Garden Homestay** — Ninh Hai, Hoa Lu. ~1,100,000–1,600,000 VND ($42–62)/room. Restored brick-and-tile buildings in a garden of fish ponds and banana palms, half the rooms by the pool. The reason to pick it: the family dinner, a set home-cooked meal at around 100,000 VND per person.
- **Tam Coc River View Homestay** — 4.7 km from Ninh Binh centre, 300 m from Tam Coc wharf. Five rooms including quadruples and a family room, so the eight of you can take the whole property. Cheaper, simpler, restaurant on site.

### Getting around

- **From Hanoi** — 100 km via the CT01 expressway, **2h15** by 16-seat van.
- **Bicycles** — From the homestay, usually free or ~50,000 VND/day. Ride the lanes between Tam Coc and Ninh Hai: in December the paddies are between crops, so fields are being turned, ducks herded down the lanes, and stubble burned. You'll be riding through farmyards: slow down, nod, and don't photograph people's houses without a gesture first.
- **Distances** — Tam Coc wharf and Trang An wharf are 6–8 km apart; Mua Cave is 3 km from Tam Coc.

### Good to know

- **The goats at dawn** — Ninh Binh's goat herds go out onto the limestone at dawn, and it's the single most characteristic thing that happens here. Ask your hosts the evening before whether anyone keeps goats and whether you can walk up with them; 200,000–300,000 VND for the group is generous and not insulting.
- **Boats split the group** — Trang An boats hold 4–5, Van Long boats 2–3. You will not all be in one boat at any point; agree who's with whom and where to meet at the other end.
- **Laundry** — 20,000–40,000 VND/kg everywhere, done same-day. Use it here before the train.

## The night train

*16–17 December · 1 night, on rails · Day 5*

The single biggest time win of the trip: the train converts a 9-hour road slog into a night you'd have spent in bed anyway. **Train SE19** leaves Ninh Binh at 22:02 and arrives in Dong Hoi at 06:25. *Times shift slightly between sources: 21:51, 22:00 and 22:02 all appear for departure, and 05:40, 06:15 and 06:25 for arrival. Reconfirm on dsvn.vn.*

### Where we sleep

- **Two 4-berth soft-sleeper cabins** — Bedding, air-con and a power socket per berth; genuinely comfortable (it's the 6-berth hard sleeper you want to avoid). Upper berths have less headroom and are harder to get into. Pack an overnight bag so you're not opening suitcases in a cabin at 06:00.

### Eat and drink

- **Eat before you board** — There's a trolley service on SE19 with instant noodles and beer, and that's all. Buy water, fruit, *bánh mì* and beer on the way to the station.

### Getting around

- **Ninh Binh Railway Station** — 1 Hoang Dieu St (*some listings say 41 Hoang Hoa Tham; the station has been re-addressed, and both refer to the same place, central Ninh Binh city*). Exchange the e-ticket voucher for a paper ticket at the station at least 30–60 minutes before departure.
- **Dong Hoi station** — Thuan Ly St (now Quang Tri province, formerly Quang Binh). Two 7-seat transfers take you the 45 km (~1 hour) to Phong Nha.

## Phong Nha

*17–18 December · 2 nights · Days 6–7*

The nature centrepiece: world-class karst, real caving, and the least performative tourism economy on the route. The base is **Son Trach** (Phong Nha town). Quang Binh province no longer exists: Phong Nha is now in **Quang Tri province**.

Both main caves are caves: rain changes nothing about Phong Nha Cave or Paradise Cave except the drive. What rain does change is the road to Paradise Cave, which can flood after heavy rain, and the park very occasionally closes for 1–3 days in a storm.

### See and do

- **Phong Nha Cave by boat** — Entry **150,000 VND/person**; the boat is **750,000 VND per boat, max 12 people**, so one boat for the eight of you. Boats are managed by the Phong Nha Tourist Centre at a fixed price with no touting, and **no advance booking is possible or needed**. You motor 1.5 km up the underground Son River into the cave, then walk out onto a sandbar and explore a lit chamber on foot. Physically trivial. **Tien Son Cave** (80,000 VND) above it is a dry cave up a flight of steps: fine rather than essential.
- **Paradise Cave (Thiên Đường)** — **270,000 VND/person** (2026 rate; *older sources say 250,000*). The ticket includes the electric buggy from the car park to the base of the stairs; then it's 500 steps up and a descent into the cave. The first kilometre is boardwalked in a chamber so large the lighting doesn't reach the ceiling; the full cave runs 31 km. **Go after 14:00**: the coach groups from Dong Hoi and Hue come between 09:00 and 12:00, and the cave is effectively yours after 15:00. Allow 2.5 hours.
- **Oxalis Tu Lan Experience (1 day)** — **2,000,000 VND / ~$75 per person**, everything included, capped at 12 people. A 9 km trek through valleys and river crossings, 800 m of cave, a **15 m ladder descent on harness and rope**, and **150 m swimming through an underground river in a life vest**. Rated Easy; age limit 16–70. The December caveat: cave water sits around 20–22°C and the air outside is 17–21°C with no sun to warm you afterward. Most people find it exhilarating; some find it grim. The dry alternatives from the same operator are the **Hang Tien Cave Day Trek** (2,200,000 VND, a dry cave with huge calcite formations) and **Phong Nha: Nuoc Nut Cave Discovery** (2,150,000 VND).
- **Tan Hoa** — The village where the Oxalis tours start, 70 km from Phong Nha. Oxalis employs porters, cooks and safety staff from here; Tan Hoa was 85.7% below the poverty line in 2010 and is at around 2% now, largely through this. It also built floating houses to survive the annual floods, Vietnam's first "weather-adaptive tourism village". Ask the porters about the floods; it is not a rehearsed story.
- **The Phong Nha Caves Exploration Museum** — In town, free, 08:00–17:30 daily. Staffed by people from the caving community. It's where you'll understand who found these caves and how: Hồ Khanh, a local logger, found the entrance to Son Doong in 1991 while sheltering from a storm and couldn't relocate it for years. Skip the 500,000 VND VR, read the walls, talk to whoever's on the desk.
- **Nước Moọc (Mooc Spring) Eco Trail** — Entry 80,000 VND (the 180,000 VND package adds kayaking and swimming, but in December the spring is cold enough that nobody will want to). The boardwalk trail through the stream valley is the good part.
- **Dark Cave** — 250,000 VND in low season (16 September–end February), including the zipline, mud bath and kayak. A managed adventure-park experience rather than caving, but covered and fun. Book through an office in Phong Nha town; Hanoi or Hoi An agencies add 20–30%.
- **Bong Lai valley** — A farming basin 10 km out of Son Trach, 25–30 km of sealed and hard-packed farm road with almost no traffic, best on a scooter (~150,000–200,000 VND each). A handful of family-run places are strung along it: the **Duck Stop** (ducks, buffalo, ~150,000 VND), **Wild Boar Eco Farm**, and **the Pub With Cold Beer**, a farmhouse at the end of a dirt track that grills chicken to order. It has become a circuit with signage and mid-morning minibuses; **ride it at 07:00** and you'll have it to yourselves. Fill up with petrol in town first: there is nothing out there.

### Eat and drink

The plainest cooking on the route, and that isn't a failing: river fish, eel, chicken and clay-pot braises, in a province that was poor until very recently. Look for **cá kho tộ** (fish braised in a clay pot with caramel and pepper) and **lươn xào nghệ** (eel stir-fried with turmeric).

- **Chợ Troc / Son Trach morning market** — Central Phong Nha, from ~05:30, 30,000–40,000 VND/person. Noodle stalls and *bánh mì* carts serving construction crews and cave guides. Order **bánh mì** with pâté and pickled carrot and a bowl of **bún**. The correct breakfast after a night on the train.
- **Phong Nha Bamboo Restaurant** or **Mountain View Restaurant** — Both on the main street in Son Trach, 80,000–150,000 VND/person. Order the clay-pot fish and the turmeric eel. *Restaurant turnover in Phong Nha is high; several names from guides three years old have closed. Ask your homestay what's good this month.*
- **Dinner at the homestay** — 150,000–250,000 VND/person, home-cooked. Ask when you book.
- **After the cave tour** — 100,000–200,000 VND/person. **Bomb Crater Bar** and **Omar's Namaste** are the standby foreigner options; for local food, eat at whatever *quán* on the main street your homestay recommends and order **gà nướng** (grilled chicken) and rice.
- **On the Oxalis tour** — All meals are included, and reviews consistently single out the food.

### Where we sleep

- **Phong Nha Farmstay** — 8 km out of town, surrounded by paddy. Rooms from ~$37 on Agoda, rising to ~$96 in high season; family rooms. French-colonial style, pool, free bikes, and the original foreign-run guesthouse in the region (Ben and Bich, open since 2010). Genuinely good food, if pricier than town. The quiet and the paddy views are the point.
- **Thao Nguyen Homestay** or **Phong Nha Riverside Homestay** — In town, family-run, ~$20–35/room, several rooms each so eight fits. Simpler, closer to the morning market and cheap dinners, and more clearly a family's house. **Several Phong Nha homestays are cash-only.**
- **Chay Lap Farmstay** — If you book Oxalis, a discounted 1,100,000 VND/room/night (~$42). Pool, spa, kayaking. Comfortable rather than local.

### Getting around

- **From the train** — Oxalis transfers from Dong Hoi station, 650,000 VND per 7-seater, 45 km, ~1 hour.
- **Distances** — Paradise Cave is 30 km out of town; the Phong Nha Cave wharf is in town. A private van for the day is ~2,000,000 VND.
- **Scooters** — Arrange on arrival through your homestay or any shop on the Son Trach main street. An automatic Honda Vision or Yamaha Janus is right for the farm lanes.

### Good to know

- **Cash** — Most Phong Nha homestays, the Bong Lai farms and the Phong Nha Cave boat don't take cards, and ATMs in Son Trach are sparse to nonexistent. Withdraw in Hanoi, Ninh Binh city, Hue and Da Nang.
- **Hospital** — The nearest serious hospital is in Dong Hoi, 45 km away.
- **Leeches** — On the Tu Lan trail in wet conditions. Bring calf-length thick socks.

## Hue

*19 December · 1 night · Days 8–9*

Vietnam's most distinctive regional cuisine, and the reason to be annoyed you only have one night. You get an afternoon, an evening, a dinner, a breakfast and two hours at the Citadel. That's enough to eat well but not enough to see the tombs.

Hue cooking runs on two traditions at once: the imperial court's habit of many small, precisely composed dishes, and a street tradition of aggressive, chilli-and-shrimp-paste noodle soups. Spicier than Hanoi, not sweet like Saigon, with *mắm ruốc* (fermented shrimp paste) as the base note under almost everything. Thua Thien Hue is now simply **Hue**, a centrally governed city.

This is the stop most likely to be rained on: Hue's rainy season runs into December, and Hue and Da Nang sit at **18–26°C** and wet.

### See and do

- **Hue Imperial Citadel (Đại Nội)** — **200,000 VND/person** (2026; *some sources still list 150,000*). Opens **06:30**; buy at Ngo Mon Gate or online at hueworldheritage.org.vn. Genuinely worth the crowds: Thai Hoa Palace, the Nine Dynastic Urns, the To Mieu ancestral temple, and the recently restored Kien Trung Palace. At 06:30 on a Sunday you'll have the courtyards to yourselves in flat morning light; by 09:30 it's coaches. Two hours, flat walking. Skip the tombs: the 4-site combo at 530,000 VND is wasted on a one-night visit.
- **The riverfront and Truong Tien Bridge** — Walk the riverfront on Le Loi and cross **Truong Tien Bridge** to the market side.
- **An Dinh Palace** — 50,000 VND, closes 17:00. Tight on arrival day; skip it if you're late.
- **Hue Royal Antiquities Museum** — 50,000 VND. The rain-day addition.
- **Vinh Moc Tunnels** — On the way from Phong Nha, 07:00–17:00, **50,000 VND/person**. An entire village of 300 people lived underground here for six years, in three levels of tunnel dug into the coastal clay, with a maternity room where 17 children were born. You walk through them. Low, dark and narrow, genuinely claustrophobic for some, and the most affecting hour on this route. **Hien Luong Bridge** and the Ben Hai River at the 17th parallel are ten minutes further on and free: a five-minute stop, half the bridge painted each colour.
- **Khe Sanh Combat Base** — 50,000 VND. The alternative stop on the drive if the morning is rained off.

### Eat and drink

This is the city to be greedy in. The must-eats: *bún bò Huế*, *cơm hến*, the rice-cake set (*bánh bèo / bánh bột lọc / bánh nậm / bánh ram ít*), *bánh khoái* with *nem lụi*, and *bánh ép* from Dong Ba. Several of the small places need booking for eight.

- **Bún bò Huế** — The dish: beef and pork bones, lemongrass, shrimp paste, chilli oil, thick round rice noodles, brisket, pork knuckle and a cube of congealed blood if you let them. **Bún Bò Huế Bà Tuyết**, 47 Nguyen Cong Tru St, Phu Hoi, ~50,000 VND; or **Quán Cẩm**, 38 Tran Cao Van, roughly 06:00–19:00. *Several well-known bún bò places open only for a few hours in the morning; for the best version, eat it for breakfast.*
- **Hue rice cakes** — **Quán Bà Đỏ**, 7 Nguyen Binh Khiem, just off Chi Lang, or **Hàng Me Mé**, 60 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. 120,000–180,000 VND/person for a full spread. Order all of them: **bánh bèo** (thumbnail-sized steamed rice cakes topped with dried shrimp, scallion oil and pork crackling), **bánh bột lọc** (translucent tapioca dumplings with shrimp and pork in banana leaf), **bánh nậm** (flat steamed cakes in leaf) and **bánh ram ít** (a sticky dumpling on a fried rice cracker). Each is two bites; the point is the set. Court cooking that escaped into the street. **Reserve for eight at Bà Đỏ.**
- **Bánh khoái** — **Lạc Thiện**, 6 Dinh Tien Hoang, ~08:00–22:30, or **Chị Hạnh**, 11–15 Pho Duc Chinh, 09:00–21:00. ~100,000 VND/person. A thick, small, turmeric-yellow crisp pancake of shrimp, pork and bean sprout, folded and eaten by hand with herbs and a peanut-and-liver dipping sauce that is the actual star. Order **nem lụi** alongside: lemongrass-skewered grilled pork you roll yourself. **Reserve for eight at Lạc Thiện.**
- **Cơm hến** — **Cơm Hến Bà Đen** (06:00–11:30) or **Cơm hến Hương** near the Citadel, ~30,000 VND. Cold rice with baby clams from the Perfume River, clam broth, peanuts, pork crackling, banana flower, herbs and a lot of chilli, mixed hard before eating. Hue's breakfast, startling at 06:00, and the dish you will most regret not eating. *Bún hến* is the same thing over noodles.
- **Lunch on the road** — On the drive in, stop at a *quán cơm bình dân* (a point-and-choose rice shop) in Dong Ha or Gio Linh: 50,000–70,000 VND/person, six or seven dishes on a counter. How Vietnam eats lunch on a working day, and the best-value meal of the trip.

### Markets and shops

- **Chợ Đông Ba (Dong Ba Market)** — On the north bank by Truong Tien Bridge, roughly 06:00–19:00. Hue's central market; the food section on the ground floor is where the city eats, and the upper floors are covered. Go in the evening for *bánh ép*, rice-and-shrimp batter pressed flat on a hot iron and rolled with herbs and green mango, a snack Hue people eat standing up and almost no visitor tries. 10,000 VND a piece.

### Where we sleep

- **Hotel Alba Spa** or **Jade Scene Hotel** — Both in the Phu Hoi/Vinh Ninh grid south of the river, ~1,000,000–1,500,000 VND ($40–58)/double. This side of the Perfume River is where the food is, and it's a 10-minute walk to Dong Ba Market across Truong Tien Bridge. Hue on a Saturday night is a weekend in a compact hotel district, so book early.
- **A garden house (nhà rường) guesthouse in Kim Long** — West of the Citadel: quieter, traditional timber architecture, closer to the Thien Mu Pagoda. Harder to fit eight into one property.

### Getting around

- **From Phong Nha** — 210 km, 4 hours direct by 16-seat van, ~4h45 with the Vinh Moc stop.
- **On foot** — From the Phu Hoi/Vinh Ninh hotels it's a 10-minute walk to Dong Ba Market across Truong Tien Bridge.

## Hai Van Pass and Da Nang

*20 December · 1 night · Day 9*

The last day is the ride: Hue to Da Nang along the coast and over the **Hai Van Pass**, 21 km of switchbacks topping out around 500 m, with Lang Co lagoon on one side and Da Nang Bay on the other. Motorbikes are banned from the Hai Van tunnel, which is exactly what keeps the pass quiet: the trucks and coaches are all underneath you.

Then a night in Da Nang, staying in **Hai Chau**, central Da Nang, not the beach. DAD airport is 3 km away for the pre-dawn flight, and Da Nang gives you *mì Quảng* and *bánh xèo* at their source. Quang Nam province no longer exists; Hoi An is now in **Da Nang City**.

### See and do

- **The coast road out of Hue** — Through **Thuan An** and the **An Bang "City of Ghosts"**: 3 km of extravagant, brightly painted family tombs along the coast road, built by relatives who emigrated. Free, and a 20-minute stop that will stay with you.
- **Lang Co / Lap An Lagoon** — Oyster rafts, mountains behind, and the fuel stop at the foot of the climb.
- **Hai Van Pass** — Stop at the top: French bunkers, a Nguyen-dynasty gate, and the view back over Lang Co on one side and Da Nang Bay on the other. There's a café. Go slowly on the descent: the switchbacks are tight, the surface is patched, and the south side gets wet from sea mist even on a dry day.
- **The Heritage Train, Hue → Da Nang** — The wet-weather version of the day, and arguably the better experience in any weather. Trains **HĐ1** (morning) and **HĐ3** (afternoon), 2.5–3 hours, with large observation windows, a Hue food carriage, and a 10–15 minute photo stop at **Lang Co station**. **180,000–400,000 VND/person** depending on class, with a weekend surcharge. *Departure times vary a lot between sources: 07:05, 07:45 and 07:50 all appear for HĐ1.* Sit on the **left** going south for the sea.
- **Optional Hoi An evening** — 30 minutes and ~400,000 VND by car each way: the lanterns, the old town at night, and **cao lầu** at its source. You'd also get back at 22:30 before a 03:00 alarm. The itinerary's verdict: "Your call; I wouldn't."

### Eat and drink

Bolder than Hue and less courtly, built on the coast and on turmeric, with broth used sparingly: several signature dishes are almost dry. The must-eats: *mì Quảng*, *bánh xèo* with *nem lụi* and peanut-liver sauce, *bún chả cá*, and *bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo* (pork belly with two rims of fat, rolled at the table with a bale of herbs). *Cao lầu* genuinely only exists in Hoi An, and this route skips it.

- **Mì Quảng Bà Mua** — Several branches; 95A Nguyen Tri Phuong (Hai Chau) and 19 Tran Binh Trong are the ones most often named. ~06:30–22:00 depending on branch, **35,000–50,000 VND**. Thick turmeric rice noodles with barely any broth, topped with shrimp and pork or chicken, peanuts, herbs, and a rice cracker you snap over the top. Quang Nam's defining dish. It's a daytime dish and the best shops sell out by mid-afternoon, so eat it at 16:00 when you arrive. *Sources give four addresses for Bà Mua; some may be defunct. Check the map on the day.*
- **Bánh Xèo Bà Dưỡng** — In an alley off Hoang Dieu, Hai Chau. ~100,000–150,000 VND/person. Michelin-listed, 30-plus years old, hidden deep in a lane. Central-Vietnamese **bánh xèo** is taco-sized, so order six to eight each, plus **nem lụi**. The thick peanut-and-liver sauce is the reason to come. Go before 20:00; they sell out.
- **Bún Chả Cá 109** — 109 Nguyen Chi Thanh, Hai Chau (breakfast and lunch mainly), or **Bà Lữ**, 319 Hung Vuong. Vermicelli in a clear, faintly sweet-sour broth built on fish bones, pumpkin, pineapple and tomato, with house-made fish cakes both steamed and fried. Da Nang's actual everyday breakfast, and better than it sounds.
- **Seafood at Lang Co** — On the ride: a beach *quán* at Lang Co for grilled squid, clams and oysters, 150,000–250,000 VND/person. Don't eat heavily before the descent.

### Markets and shops

- **Chợ Cồn (Con Market)** — Hung Vuong St, Hai Chau. The street-food section comes alive from around 15:00, and it's where Da Nang goes after work: *bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo*, *ốc* (sea snails), sugarcane juice, *chè*. Sit at a stall, point, and pay 20,000–40,000 VND a plate.
- **Han Market** — Walkable from the hotels below, like Con Market.
- **Tho Quang / Nai Hien Dong** — The fish market, when the boats land around 05:00. Only if you had a morning here; you don't.

### Where we sleep

- **Sala Danang Beach Hotel** or **Grand Cititel Danang** — Hai Chau, ~1,000,000–1,600,000 VND ($40–62)/double. Central, four rooms for eight, walkable to Con Market and Han Market.
- **Anywhere central** — Anything on **Bach Dang** or in the grid between **Nguyen Van Linh** and **Tran Phu**. From An Thuong or My Khe it's 6–7 km to the airport; from Hoi An, 30 km and 45 minutes.

### Getting around

- **The bikes** — Eight one-way rentals from Hue with the luggage going ahead by van: **Style Motorbikes**, **Hidden Land Travel / Hue Scooter Rental** or **Motorvina**, $25–40 per bike, ~6,000,000 VND for the group. Take **semi-automatic or manual 110–150 cc** (Honda Winner X, Yamaha Exciter or Honda Blade): the descent is long and steep and you want engine braking. ~85 km, 3h30–4h riding with stops. Fill up in Hue, and again at Lang Co.
- **Without bikes** — A private van through the Hai Van tunnel takes 1h45, ~1,700,000 VND; or the van over the pass, which still gets you the view from the top.
- **To the airport** — DAD is 3 km from Hai Chau: a 10–15 minute, ~150,000–200,000 VND run at 03:30. Book the hotel car the night before; Grab works at that hour, but don't stake a 05:00 flight on it.

### Good to know

- **Motorbike licences** — Vietnam recognises only the **1968** Convention International Driving Permit, and it must show motorcycle category A, carried with your original licence. Riding without one voids your travel and medical insurance. Anyone on a 1949 IDP takes the van or the train.
- **If it rains, don't ride** — A wet Hai Van Pass with eight riders on unfamiliar bikes is the single highest-risk item in the trip. If there's meaningful rain forecast, take the train or a van. Decide by 07:00 on the day.
- **Ride in two groups of four** — With a lead and sweep in each, and agreed stop points. Eight bikes cannot hold formation through Hue traffic.
