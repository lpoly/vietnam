# Central → Northern Vietnam: the guide

Nine nights, five places, one line north: land in Hanoi and fly straight on to Da Nang, then Hoi An's lanterns and old houses, over the Hai Van Pass to the imperial city and tombs of Hue, the caves of Phong Nha, a night on the train, the limestone karst of Ninh Binh, and finally Hanoi's lakes and monuments, flying home from the same airport. Nature and the must-see landmarks come first; the food is what happens in between, and it's very good.

Every price, time and address below comes from the full itinerary, re-checked in October 2026. Where sources still disagree, it says so here too, and those bits are flagged. The hour-by-hour schedule lives in the Plan tab.

A few things worth knowing before anything else: 12 December 2026 is a **Saturday**, so Hoi An is a weekend, Hue and Phong Nha are midweek, Ninh Binh is Friday–Saturday, and Hanoi gets the end of the weekend: the walking street on Saturday night and the mausoleum on Sunday morning. Going north means the central coast comes first, in the wetter half of December. Prices use 1 USD ≈ 26,000 VND. And **carry cash**: street food, markets, most homestays, park gates and boats don't take cards.

## Hoi An and the Hai Van Pass

*12–14 December · 2 nights · Days 1–3*

Hoi An's UNESCO old town is where the trip starts: lanterns, the river, the assembly halls and the Japanese Covered Bridge, and *cao lầu*, a noodle dish that only exists here. You land in Hanoi at 10:00, fly on to Da Nang in the afternoon, and reach Hoi An by about 17:00, in time for the lanterns.

Day 2 is the old town by daylight, with the Cham ruins at My Son first if you want them. Day 3 is the drive north with Quang, about 150 km over the **Hai Van Pass**, 21 km of switchbacks topping out around 500 m, with Da Nang Bay behind you and Lang Co lagoon ahead, into Hue. The tunnel underneath takes the trucks, which keeps the pass quiet; ask for the pass road.

### At a glance

- **When** — Saturday 12 to Monday 14 December; you leave on Monday morning.
- **Weather** — Wet season tail: the chance of a wet day is about 41% on 1 December, falling through the month. Hoi An's flood risk runs to mid-December. Watch the forecast.
- **Don't miss** — A lantern boat on the Thu Bon, the Japanese Covered Bridge, the view from the top of Hai Van.
- **Signature dish** — *Cao lầu*, Hoi An only.
- **Sleep** — Nu Ni Hotel, booked.

### See and do

- **Hoi An Ancient Town** — A ticket for foreigners is **120,000 VND**, open 08:00–17:00, and covers a set of the old houses (Tan Ky, Phung Hung, Duc An, Quan Thang), the assembly halls, the Tran Family Chapel, museums and the **Japanese Covered Bridge** (reopened in August 2024). After 17:00 the old town is the streets themselves, pedestrian until 21:00 in winter. *Sources describe what the ticket covers differently.*
- **Lantern boat on the Thu Bon** — About 170,000 VND for 1–3 people, 220,000 for 4–5, roughly 16:00–21:30. Lanterns to float are 20,000–40,000 VND. The **night market** on Nguyen Hoang runs from about 17:00.
- **My Son Sanctuary** — The Cham temple ruins, 37–50 km out, 1–1.5 hours each way. 150,000 VND, open 06:00–18:00 (some sources say 17:00). Go early, before the heat and the groups; skip it in heavy rain.
- **Marble Mountains** — Limestone hills with caves and pagodas inside, on the coast road south of Da Nang: the first stop on the drive to Hue. *40,000 VND to climb or 80,000 with the lift; the listing is ambiguous.*
- **Hai Van Pass** — Stop at the top: French bunkers, a Nguyen-dynasty gate, and the view back over Da Nang Bay and ahead to Lang Co. *One 2026 source mentions a new 70,000 VND gate ticket; no other confirms it.*
- **Lang Co / Lap An Lagoon** — Oyster rafts and mountains behind; the lunch stop at the foot of the descent.

### Eat and drink

Bold, coastal, built on turmeric, with broth used sparingly.

- **Cao lầu** — Thick, chewy noodles with char siu, greens and crackling, made with water from a specific Hoi An well. Order it with *mì Quảng* at a busy local place in the old town; ask the hotel which this year.
- **Mì Quảng** — Turmeric rice noodles with barely any broth, shrimp and pork, peanuts, herbs and a rice cracker snapped over the top: Quang Nam's defining dish, and a lunchtime one.
- **Seafood at Lang Co** — Grilled squid, clams and oysters at a beach *quán* on the way, 150,000–250,000 VND/person.

### Where we sleep

- **Nu Ni Hotel Hoi An** — Booked by Christina through Booking.com: 2 nights, €149 for the whole group (≈ 4,320,000 VND), not paid yet.
- **If that falls through** — Emm Hotel Hoi An (0.7 km from the old town, from ~US$76 a room), River Suites Hoi An (0.6 km, from ~US$66) or Hoi An Historic Hotel (0.5 km, from ~US$99).

### Getting around

- **The Da Nang flight** — You land at Noi Bai Terminal 2 at 10:00 and fly from Terminal 1. On separate tickets allow 3.5–4 hours: a free shuttle runs between terminals, or it's a 10-minute walk. Vietjet's cheapest fares include only 7 kg of cabin bags.
- **From Da Nang airport** — Booked: Quang, the driver, 400,000 VND (≈ €14), arranged by Lefteris on WhatsApp. 40–50 minutes, about an hour at 17:00. Send him the flight number.
- **Other ways from the airport** — Two 7-seat Grab cars at 350,000–450,000 VND each, or metered Mai Linh or Vinasun taxis at 300,000–450,000 VND a car. Shared shuttles are 130,000–150,000 VND a seat (timetables disagree). Sources: [Vietnamtourism.com, Oct 2026](https://www.vietnamtourism.com/en/getting-to-hoi-an-from-da-nang-airport-and-beyond), [Jungle Boss, Oct 2026](https://junglebosstours.com/explorer/tourism-blog/da-nang-to-hoi-an), [Vietnam Drive](https://www.vietnamdrive.com/danang-airport-to-hoi-an/).
- **To Hue** — Booked: a private car with Quang over the Hai Van Pass, stopping at Marble Mountains, the top of the pass and Lang Co. 3,200,000 VND (≈ €110), arranged by Lefteris on WhatsApp. Your bags ride with you.
- **Other ways to Hue** — If that falls through, or some of you want to ride: one-way motorbikes with luggage transfer from Hidden Land / Hue Scooter Rental (from US$22 a bike) or Hoianit (750,000 VND including luggage). Or the Heritage Train from Da Nang, HĐ2 07:05 → 11:03 or HĐ4 15:20 → 18:15, with a stop at Lang Co station (330,000 VND on 12Go; fares listed elsewhere from 207,000), or an Easy Rider, pillion with a local driver, US$58–75 per person on booking sites. Sources: [Vietnamtourism.com, Oct 2026](https://www.vietnamtourism.com/en/transport/da-nang-hue), [Goodmorning Hoi An, Oct 2026](https://goodmorning-hoian.com/en/how-to-get-from-hoi-an-to-hue/).

### Good to know

- **Rain on the pass** — The top is often in cloud in December. In the car that just means the tunnel instead: you lose the view, not the day.
- **If Hoi An floods** — Risk runs to mid-December, which is when you arrive. Sleep in Da Nang's Hai Chau instead, 40 minutes away.
- **Laundry** — 20,000–40,000 VND/kg, same day.

## Hue

*14–15 December · 1 night · Days 3–4*

The imperial capital of the Nguyen dynasty, seen in two halves: the walled Imperial City on the afternoon you arrive, and the emperors' tombs in the hills along the Perfume River at opening time the next morning, before the drive north. It's also Vietnam's most distinctive regional cooking: court dishes in miniature, and chilli-and-shrimp-paste noodle soups.

On the way out to Phong Nha you cross the old DMZ: the Vinh Moc Tunnels and the bridge at the 17th parallel.

### At a glance

- **When** — Monday 14 (arrive about 16:00) and Tuesday 15 December (leave about 10:30).
- **Weather** — A high of about 24°C and a low of 19°C, and around 350 mm of rain in December, more of it in the first half. Bring a shell.
- **Don't miss** — The Imperial City before it closes, the tombs of Tu Duc, Khai Dinh and Minh Mang at 07:00, Vinh Moc on the way north.
- **Signature dish** — *Bún bò Huế*, for breakfast.
- **Sleep** — The south bank, a 10-minute walk from Dong Ba Market.

### See and do

- **Hue Imperial City (Đại Nội)** — The walled royal city: Thai Hoa Palace, the Nine Dynastic Urns, the To Mieu ancestral temple and the restored Kien Trung Palace. **200,000 VND** alone, or **530,000 VND** for the combo with three tombs, valid two days. **Ticket sales close at 17:00**, so go straight there when you arrive.
- **Tomb of Tu Duc** — About 7 km out, open from 07:00: pavilions and a lake in a wooded park, the emperor's retreat as much as his tomb. About an hour.
- **Tomb of Khai Dinh** — About 10 km out: the steep, dark concrete-and-mosaic one, unlike the others. 40–45 minutes, with a long stair.
- **Tomb of Minh Mang** — About 12 km out: the most symmetrical of the three. About 50 minutes.
- **Vinh Moc Tunnels** — On the drive north: a village of 300 people lived underground here for six years, with a maternity room where 17 children were born. Low, dark and narrow; the most affecting hour on the route. *50,000 or 100,000 VND.* **Hien Luong Bridge** at the 17th parallel is ten minutes on.
- **Truong Tien Bridge** — Walk the riverfront on Le Loi and cross to the Dong Ba side on the evening you arrive.
- **Thien Mu Pagoda** — Not in the plan: the seven-storey tower on the river bank, Hue's emblem, ~4 km upriver. One night in Hue doesn't leave time for it.

### Eat and drink

- **Hue rice cakes** — **Quán Bà Đỏ**, 7 Nguyen Binh Khiem, or **Hàng Me Mé**, 60 Tran Quang Khai: **bánh bèo**, **bánh bột lọc**, **bánh nậm** and **bánh ram ít** as a set, 120,000–180,000 VND/person. **Reserve for eight.**
- **Bánh khoái** — **Lạc Thiện**, 6 Dinh Tien Hoang, or **Chị Hạnh**, 11–15 Pho Duc Chinh, ~100,000 VND: a crisp turmeric pancake with **nem lụi** and peanut-and-liver sauce. **Reserve for eight at Lạc Thiện.**
- **Bún bò Huế** — Breakfast before the tombs: **Bún Bò Huế Bà Tuyết**, 47 Nguyen Cong Tru St, ~50,000 VND, or **Quán Cẩm**, 38 Tran Cao Van, roughly 06:00–19:00. *The best shops close when the pot empties.*
- **Cơm hến** — **Cơm Hến Bà Đen** (06:00–11:30): cold rice with baby clams from the Perfume River and a lot of chilli.

### Markets and shops

- **Chợ Đông Ba (Dong Ba Market)** — By Truong Tien Bridge, roughly 06:00–19:00. Hue's central market; just before it closes look for *bánh ép*, rice-and-shrimp batter pressed on a hot iron, 10,000 VND a piece.

### Where we sleep

- **White Lotus Hue** — 5–7 Hoang Hoa Tham, south bank. Rated 9.2; ~US$68 a room with breakfast.
- **Alba Spa Hotel** — 29 Tran Quang Khai, Phu Hoi. ~US$28–30 a room; a 10-minute walk to Dong Ba.
- **Silk Path Grand Hue** — 2 Le Loi, on the river. ~US$59–110 a room.

### Getting around

- **On foot** — From the south-bank hotels it's a 10-minute walk to Dong Ba across Truong Tien Bridge.
- **To Phong Nha** — One van all day, with your bags: the three tombs from 07:00, then north via **Vinh Moc**, arriving about 17:00. ~4,500,000 VND; get a written quote (Culture Pham Travel quotes 3,000,000 VND plus US$15 for Vinh Moc).
- **Other ways to Phong Nha** — The new Hue–Phong Nha tourist train, HQ2, leaves Hue daily at 07:15 and reaches Tho Loc, about 20 km from Phong Nha, at 11:55, with a stop at My Trach for Vinh Moc; 380,000–760,000 VND depending on the seat (sources differ). Tourist buses leave between 08:05 and 10:00, 3.5–4 hours, US$7–17, with no stops. You'd lose the tombs either way. Sources: [VietnamNet, Aug 2026](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/tickets-for-huephong-nha-tourist-train-go-on-sale-2546791.html), [Oxalis, tourist train](https://oxalisadventure.com/hue-phong-nha-tourist-train/), [North-Vietnam.com, Aug 2026](https://north-vietnam.com/how-to-get-from-hue-to-phong-nha-ke-bang-bus-train).

### Good to know

- **Rain** — The tombs are short walks from the van; in heavy rain keep **Khai Dinh**, the most covered, and add the Hue Royal Antiquities Museum (50,000 VND).
- **Dress** — Shoulders and knees covered for the tombs and pagodas.

## Phong Nha

*15–17 December · 2 nights · Days 4–6*

The nature centrepiece: world-class karst, real caving, and the least performative tourism economy on the route. The base is **Son Trach**, Phong Nha town, now in **Quang Tri province**.

Two cave days: a guided trek into the wild caves with Oxalis on the first, then an early scooter loop through the Bong Lai valley and the show caves (Phong Nha Cave by boat, Paradise Cave in the afternoon) on the second, before the night train north.

### At a glance

- **When** — Tuesday 15 (arrive about 17:00) to Thursday 17 December; the train leaves just before midnight.
- **Weather** — Caves are caves; rain only matters for the road to Paradise Cave.
- **Don't miss** — The Oxalis cave day, Paradise Cave's first kilometre, Bong Lai at 07:00.
- **Signature dish** — *Cá kho tộ*, fish braised in a clay pot.
- **Sleep** — Phong Nha Farmstay in the paddies, or a family homestay in town, with two rooms kept until the train.

### See and do

- **Hang Tien Cave Day Trek** — The Oxalis day for a mixed group in December: a dry cave with an enormous entrance and huge calcite formations, and a pool you can decline. 2,000,000 VND / US$75, max 12, ages 16–70; back around 19:30–20:00.
- **Oxalis Tu Lan Experience (1 day)** — The wetter alternative: 9 km of valleys and river crossings, a **15 m ladder descent on harness and rope**, and **150 m swimming through an underground river**. Same price. December cave water is 20–22°C with no sun afterward. *Oxalis's pages disagree on its season.*
- **Paradise Cave (Thiên Đường)** — A boardwalk through a chamber so large the lighting doesn't reach the ceiling; the full cave runs 31 km. **270,000 VND** including the buggy (older sources say 250,000 plus the buggy). **It closes at 16:00**: arrive by 13:30.
- **Phong Nha Cave by boat** — You motor 1.5 km up an underground river into the cave, then walk out onto a sandbar. **150,000 VND/person**, plus a boat priced per boat (max 12). *Sources give 550,000 VND, 750,000 VND or 55,000 VND per person for the boat.* **Tien Son Cave** above it is 80,000 VND.
- **Bong Lai valley** — A farming basin 10 km out of Son Trach, by scooter (~150,000–200,000 VND each), past the **Duck Stop**, **Wild Boar Eco Farm** and **the Pub With Cold Beer**. It's become a circuit with mid-morning minibuses; **ride it at 07:00**.
- **The Phong Nha Caves Exploration Museum** — Free, 08:00–17:30. Where you learn how Hồ Khanh, a local logger, found Son Doong's entrance in 1991 while sheltering from a storm.
- **Dark Cave** — The rain backup: zipline, mud bath and kayak, *350,000 or 450,000 VND*. Book in a Phong Nha town office, not through a Hoi An agency.

### Eat and drink

River fish, eel, chicken and clay-pot braises: the plainest cooking on the route, and good.

- **Chợ Troc / Son Trach morning market** — From ~05:30, 30,000–40,000 VND/person. **Bánh mì** with pâté and a bowl of **bún** before the scooters.
- **Phong Nha Bamboo Restaurant** or **Mountain View Restaurant** — On the main street, 80,000–150,000 VND/person: **cá kho tộ** and **lươn xào nghệ** (turmeric eel). *Turnover is high; ask your hosts what's good this month.*
- **After the cave day** — **Bomb Crater Bar** and **Omar's Namaste**, or a local *quán* for **gà nướng** (grilled chicken).
- **Before the train** — Eat properly at the homestay: the trolley on board sells instant noodles and beer, and that's all.

### Where we sleep

- **Phong Nha Farmstay** — 8 km out of town in the paddies. Standard rooms 1,300,000 VND, family rooms 2,400,000 VND (direct, October 2026). Pool, free bikes, good food.
- **Thao Nguyen Homestay** or **Jungle Boss Homestay** — In town, family-run, roughly US$15–40 a room. **Several Phong Nha homestays are cash-only.**

### Getting around

- **From Hue** — The tomb-and-DMZ van, arriving about 17:00.
- **To the train** — Dong Hoi station is 40–45 km away, 45–60 minutes. Pre-book two 7-seaters or one van for about 22:15, ~1,300,000 VND (*private-car prices run from 300,000 to 1,200,000 VND depending on the source*).
- **Other ways to the station** — Mai Linh and Sun Taxi are metered, 350,000–400,000 VND (quoted for the other direction). Grab is scarce or absent here (sources disagree), and the last local bus leaves at 17:15: at 22:00, pre-book. Sources: [Vietnamtourism.com, Oct 2026](https://www.vietnamtourism.com/en/dong-hoi-to-phong-nha-the-complete-transport-guide-for-2026), [Local Vietnam, Jul 2026](https://localvietnam.com/quang-binh/phong-nha/from-dong-hoi-to-phong-nha/), [12Go](https://12go.asia/en/travel/phong-nha/dong-hoi?z=13836450).

### Good to know

- **Cash** — ATMs in Son Trach are sparse to nonexistent. Withdraw in Hoi An and Hue.
- **Keep two rooms until 22:00** — For showers and repacking before the train: book a third night or agree late check-out in writing.
- **Hospital** — The nearest serious hospital is in Dong Hoi, 45 km away.
- **Socks** — Calf-length and thick, for leeches on wet trails.
- **Scooter licences** — Vietnam recognises only the **1968** Convention International Driving Permit, showing category A, carried with your original licence. Riding without one voids your travel and medical insurance. Anyone on a 1949 IDP skips the Bong Lai scooters.

*17–18 December · 1 night, on rails · Day 6*

The biggest time win of the trip: **Train SE20** turns a long road day into a night you'd have spent in bed anyway, from Dong Hoi at 23:54 to Ninh Binh at 07:59, arriving in daylight. *That's Baolau's timetable for 1 November 2026; older listings give 00:10–00:53 from Dong Hoi. Reconfirm on dsvn.vn.*

A private minivan is US$294–355 and costs you the night's sleep. ## Ninh Binh

*18–19 December · 1 night · Days 7–8*

Limestone, rice paddies and rowed boat routes through flooded caves: the best karst-and-river landscape reachable in the dry northern winter. You stay around **Tam Coc**, a 20-minute drive from Ninh Binh city.

It's one night and two half-days, so the timing matters: off the train at 08:00, the quiet places (Bích Động, Hoa Lu) in the morning, Mua Cave in the afternoon and Van Long at dusk; then Trang An at 07:30 the next morning before the Saturday coaches, and on to Hanoi after lunch.

### At a glance

- **When** — Friday 18 and Saturday 19 December; you leave for Hanoi at about 13:30.
- **Weather** — 15–21°C.
- **Don't miss** — Trang An by rowed boat at 07:30, the Mua Cave view, Van Long at dusk.
- **Signature dish** — *Dê núi*, mountain goat, and *cơm cháy* crisped rice.
- **Sleep** — Tam Coc, with early check-in arranged.

### See and do

- **Trang An, Route 2** — A UNESCO mixed site: 2.5–3 hours by rowed boat through flooded caves, some low enough that you duck, past temples and the *Kong: Skull Island* set. **300,000 VND/person** since 1 January 2026, open 07:00–16:30. Boats hold 4–5. Arrive by 07:30: on a Saturday the coaches land between 09:00 and 11:00.
- **Mua Cave (Hang Múa)** — **486 stone steps** to a Quan Am statue and a dragon ridge with the best aerial view of the Tam Coc valley. **150,000 VND** since April 2026, open 06:00–19:00. There's a café and lotus pond at the bottom for anyone who'd rather not. Skip it if it's wet.
- **Van Long Nature Reserve** — A wetland with the same karst but a fraction of the traffic, 40 minutes north. Flat-bottomed boats rowed by local women under limestone cliffs. Around 20,000 VND entry plus a 50,000–60,000 VND boat (one list says 100,000 all-in), **cash only**. Go late afternoon, when the herons come in to roost and, if you're lucky, the Delacour's langurs come down the cliffs.
- **Bích Động Pagoda** — Free, 2 km from Tam Coc wharf. Three tiers of temple built into a limestone cliff, reached by a stone stair through a cave. Usually nearly empty.
- **Hoa Lu ancient capital** — 20,000 VND. Vietnam's capital from 968 to 1010: two modest temples to the Dinh and Le emperors in a bowl of limestone. Forty minutes, history rather than spectacle.
- **Bái Đính Pagoda** — Free, electric cart 60,000 VND. The largest pagoda complex in Vietnam, vast and covered: the wet-weather alternative to Mua Cave.

### Eat and drink

- **Goat** — **Nhà hàng Đức Dê** or any of the goat restaurants signposted *dê núi* along the Tam Coc road, ~200,000 VND/person: **dê tái chanh** (goat "cooked" in lime), **dê nướng lá lốt** (grilled in betel leaf) and **cơm cháy**. *Hours for individual goat restaurants can't be verified.*
- **Dinner at the hotel** — Ask for **ốc nấu chuối đậu**, snails stewed with green banana and tofu, a northern home dish you'll almost never see on a menu.

### Where we sleep

- **Tam Coc River View Homestay** — 300 m from Tam Coc wharf; five rooms, so the eight of you can take the whole property.
- **Tam Coc Rice Fields Resort** — From about €39 a room.
- **Tam Coc Garden** — Now a boutique resort, from about US$248 a night for two.

### Getting around

- **From the train** — A van on standby for the day with bicycles, ~2,000,000 VND.
- **Bicycles** — From the hotel, free or ~50,000 VND a day: ride the lanes between Tam Coc and Ninh Hai through farmyards and paddies.
- **To Hanoi** — 100 km on the CT01 expressway, 2h15–2h30 by 16-seat van, ~1,800,000 VND.
- **Other ways to Hanoi** — The train from Ninh Binh station: SE8 at 13:59 → 16:20, from 280,000 VND, or SE6 at 16:55 → 19:14. Or a limousine van: Trang An Limousine runs 9-seat door-to-door vans at 150,000–230,000 VND a seat, booked 30–45 minutes ahead. Sources: [Vietnamtourism.com, Oct 2026](https://www.vietnamtourism.com/en/transport/hanoi-ninh-binh), [Sunparadiseland](https://sunparadiseland.com/en/tin-tuc/pricing-and-tips-for-booking-limousine-cars-in-ninh-binh-13662).

### Good to know

- **Early check-in** — You arrive at about 08:30, long before check-in: ask for a room for showers, in writing.
- **Boats split the group** — Trang An boats hold 4–5, Van Long boats 2–3. Agree who's with whom and where to meet.

## Hanoi

*19–21 December · 2 nights · Days 8–9*

The capital, and the place to see the landmarks of modern Vietnam: Hoan Kiem Lake and its island temple, the Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum, the 1070 Temple of Literature, the Hoa Lo Prison. You're based in the Old Quarter, roughly 1 km across, where the street names are still the trades that once lined them, and you fly home from here on Monday.

You're here on the right weekend: the roads around Hoan Kiem Lake close to traffic from Friday evening to Sunday midnight.

### At a glance

- **When** — Saturday 19 (arrive about 16:00) and Sunday 20 December; fly home Monday 21. The mausoleum is closed Mondays and Fridays, so Sunday morning is the slot.
- **Weather** — 15–22°C with lows near 14°C, humid and drizzly. Buildings are not heated.
- **Don't miss** — The lake and the walking street on Saturday night, the mausoleum at 08:00 on Sunday, the Temple of Literature.
- **Signature dish** — *Bún chả*, the dish that explains northern cooking in one bowl.
- **Sleep** — Old Quarter, two minutes from the lake.

### See and do

- **Hoan Kiem Lake and Ngoc Son Temple** — The centre of the city. The temple sits on an island in the lake, reached by the red bridge: 30,000 VND, roughly 07:00–18:00. Start here when you arrive.
- **Ho Chi Minh Mausoleum** — Free, open Sundays 08:00–11:00 in the cool season, closed Mondays and Fridays. The queue is long and the rules are strict: shoulders and knees covered, no shorts, bags stored, phones and cameras collected, silence. Arrive early.
- **The Presidential Palace grounds and the stilt house** — Behind the mausoleum, 25,000 or 40,000 VND, open mornings. The stilt house where Ho Chi Minh lived is the quiet, interesting part. The **One Pillar Pagoda** next door is free.
- **Temple of Literature** — 58 Quoc Tu Giam St; 08:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30); 70,000 VND, cash. Vietnam's 1070 Confucian academy; the stelae of doctoral laureates sit on stone tortoises in the third courtyard. An optional **night tour** with a 3D mapping show runs typically Thu–Sun, 199,000 VND.
- **Hoa Lo Prison** — 08:00–17:00 daily, ~50,000 VND (sources range 30,000–50,000). The French colonial prison later known as the "Hanoi Hilton". About an hour, ten minutes' walk south of the lake.
- **Museum of Ethnology** — In Cau Giay, 08:30–17:30 (closed Mondays), 40,000 VND, 25 minutes by Grab. The outdoor collection of reconstructed minority houses is the best thing of its kind in the country.
- **Thang Long Imperial Citadel** — 100,000 VND, 08:00–17:00. The excavated royal citadel near the mausoleum: the alternative if the group would rather walk than do another museum.
- **Thang Long Water Puppet Theatre** — 57B Dinh Tien Hoang, on the lake. 100,000–200,000 VND by seat. Shows at 16:10, 17:20, 18:30 and 20:00; book 3–7 days ahead, direct with the theatre.
- **Hoan Kiem weekend walking street** — Friday 19:00 to Sunday midnight, suspended in bad weather. Families, badminton, street games: Hanoi's public life at its least filtered. Free.
- **Long Bien Bridge** — The steel cantilever bridge over the Red River whose builder's plate reads 1899–1902, Daydé & Pillé, Paris. Walk the upstream side at dusk, when it fills with commuters, couples and people fishing off the piers. Stand aside for scooters.

### Eat and drink

Northern food is restrained, savoury, sour and herb-forward, with dill in places you won't see it elsewhere in Vietnam.

- **Chả Cá Thăng Long** — 6B Duong Thanh St, Cua Dong Ward, Hoan Kiem. ~200,000–250,000 VND/person. One dish only: turmeric catfish finished at your table with a hedge of dill, over vermicelli with peanuts and shrimp paste. **Reserve for eight.**
- **Bún Chả Đắc Kim** — 1 Hang Manh St, Hoan Kiem, ~10:00–14:00, 70,000–100,000 VND/person. **Bún chả** and the square sea-crab spring rolls, **nem cua bể**. In the 2026 Michelin Guide, still a plastic-stool operation.
- **Phở Hà** — 15 Hang Hom St, Hang Gai, Hoan Kiem. ~60,000–80,000 VND. **Phở gà** and **bún thang**, the most technically demanding dish in the Hanoi repertoire.
- **Cafe Dinh** — 13 Dinh Tien Hoang, up an unmarked staircase beside a silk shop, overlooking the lake. Egg coffee, ~30,000 VND.
- **Bia hơi** — Fresh draught beer, 15,000–20,000 VND a glass, at the corner of **Nguyen Huu Huan and Hang Thung** or **Bia Hơi Hải Xồm**, rather than backpacker Ta Hien.

### Markets and shops

- **Hang Bac** — The silversmiths' street, on the Old Quarter loop: Ma May → Hang Bac → **Hang Ma** (paper goods) → **Dong Xuan Market** → Hang Chieu → Hang Buom.
- **Chợ Châu Long (Chau Long Market)** — Chau Long St, Ba Dinh, from around 05:30, busiest 06:00–08:00. A working neighbourhood market, ten minutes' walk from the mausoleum: breakfast of **bánh cuốn** and **xôi xéo** before the queue. Small notes only.

### Where we sleep

- **La Siesta Premium Hang Be** — 27 Hang Be, two minutes from the lake. Rated 9.5; weekend average ~$83 a room in October 2026.
- **Hanoi La Siesta Classic Ma May** — 94 Ma May St. Rated 9.5; pricier at weekends (~$152 a room in the October snapshot). Rooftop bar with a view to Long Bien Bridge.
- **Hanoi Emerald Waters Hotel & Spa** — 47 Lo Su. Rated 9.5; ~$88 a room.

### Getting around

- **Grab** — Download it before you fly and add a card. A Grab bike across the Old Quarter is 20,000–30,000 VND.
- **To the airport** — A pre-booked 16-seat van from the Old Quarter to Noi Bai, 35–60 minutes, ~1,200,000 VND. Check-in for international flights opens 180 minutes and closes 50 minutes before departure.
- **Other ways to the airport** — Two 7-seat Grab cars at 280,000–450,000 VND each (sources differ), or metered Mai Linh or Vinasun taxis at 240,000–400,000 VND, 35–50 minutes. Bus 86 from Hanoi Railway Station is 35,000–50,000 VND but takes 60–80 minutes and has little luggage room. Leave 4 hours before departure, plus 30 minutes in the 17:00–19:00 rush. Sources: [Unchartedlens](https://unchartedlens.com/routes/vietnam/hoan-kiem-lake-to-noi-bai-airport/), [Vietnam-Visa, Feb 2026](https://www.vietnam-visa.com/bus-86-hanoi-arport-old-quarter/).

### Good to know

- **Dress for the mausoleum** — Shoulders and knees covered on Sunday morning.
- **Train Street** — Groups are banned and barriers are up; the cafés operate in a grey zone under police watch. Skip it.
- **Crossing the road** — Cross at a constant speed without stopping. Scooters flow around you.
- **Confirm the airport van** — At reception on Sunday evening.
